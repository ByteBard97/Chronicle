# Hybrid LLM + Symbolic Simulation Architectures: State of the Art for a Three-Tier NPC Belief System

*Research report for Prompt 3 — prepared August 2026. Scoped to stand alone as the third section of `docs/research.md`. Companion surveys (modding substrate; social-simulation literature) cover the Skyrim integration layer and the belief/rumor formal models; this report covers the hybrid compute architecture that sits between them.*

## Executive summary

The proposed three-tier design — a deterministic math tier for all ~1,000 NPCs, a small local LLM tier for ~30 high-centrality gossip hubs, and a large-LLM tier for player-facing conversation — is not an exotic bet. It is where the research frontier has converged over the past eighteen months. Every system that has scaled LLM-agent social simulation past a few hundred agents has done so by driving a small "core" with language models and the masses with cheap symbolic dynamics: RumorSphere reaches **one million agents** by letting a Dynamic Interaction Strategy activate LLM "core agents" only at information-conflict boundaries while regular agents exchange Deffuant-model opinion scores [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02172v1) ; HiSim (ACL 2024) pairs LLM-driven core users with bounded-confidence ABM ordinary users [(themoonlight.io)](https://www.themoonlight.io/fr/review/unveiling-the-truth-and-facilitating-change-towards-agent-based-large-scale-social-movement-simulation) ; AgentTorch simulates **8.4 million** New Yorkers with only ~100 LLM-informed archetypes and shows this configuration beats a full-LLM configuration on forecasting error [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10568v1) . Critically, the fidelity concern the prompt cites is real and now has multiple independent confirmations: MF-LLM (NeurIPS 2025) found **smaller LLM backbones outperformed larger ones** at simulating population decision dynamics, because large models produce homogeneous responses that flatten behavioral diversity [(Nanyang Technological University)](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/boan/papers/NeurIPS25_MFLLM.pdf) , and RumorSphere's ablations show that both "all-LLM" and "static-top-degree-core" configurations degrade alignment with real data while raising token cost tenfold [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02172v1) .

Three further findings should shape the build. First, the interface between tiers is a solved problem with a recognizable shape: LLM text is converted to numeric state through stance/sentiment extraction or schema-constrained JSON, and symbolic state is rendered into prompts as compact structured documents — HiSim, RumorSphere, Concordia, and the shipped-game case Vox Deorum (Civilization V) all implement variants of this seam [(themoonlight.io)](https://www.themoonlight.io/fr/review/unveiling-the-truth-and-facilitating-change-towards-agent-based-large-scale-social-movement-simulation) . Second, structured-output write-back is effectively free in 2026: grammar-constrained decoding with XGrammar or Guidance adds no measurable per-token overhead (Guidance is actually faster than unconstrained decoding) and guarantees parseable belief-update JSON even from 3B–8B models [(tianpan.co)](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-16-grammar-constrained-generation-output-reliability) . Third, the tier-2 hardware story is comfortable: a 4B-class model at Q4_K_M occupies roughly **3 GB** of VRAM and generates fast enough on a mid-range consumer GPU to run continuous-batched background inference alongside Skyrim, which community measurements place at ~2 GB of VRAM [(Intuz)](https://www.intuz.com/blog/best-small-language-models/) . The honest weak spots are evaluation (the field's "central challenge," per a 2025 critical review [(Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-025-11412-6) ) and long-horizon failure modes — persona drift, memory fabrication, and error cascades — which are documented, measurable, and only partially mitigated.

## 1. The evidence for a small-LLM-core design

### 1.1 The anchor systems, re-verified

All five anchors in the prompt check out, and two have materially evolved since they were last surveyed. **RumorSphere** (arXiv 2509.02172, v2 January 2026) is the strongest direct precedent for the proposed architecture: a million-scale rumor simulation in which a Dynamic Interaction Strategy (DIS), inspired by information-cocoon theory, adaptively promotes agents at "information conflict" boundaries into LLM-driven core agents each timestep, while regular agents interact purely through the Deffuant bounded-confidence model. The cross-tier interface is explicit: when a core agent speaks, an LLM converts its natural-language content into opinion scores, which then propagate to regular agents through the ordinary Deffuant update [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02172v1) . On three real-world rumor datasets (Moon Landing conspiracy, Xinjiang cotton, Trump–Russia) the framework reports an average **64% reduction in opinion bias** (ΔBias) versus prior models in v1 — softened to a 26.5% bias reduction in the v2 abstract — plus a 46% improvement in simulation stability, and it estimates the full million-agent run at about **$48** of GPT-4o-mini tokens, versus a tenfold increase when the adaptive grouping is removed [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02172v1) . The UT Austin anchor is Hu et al., *Simulating Rumor Spreading in Social Networks using LLM Agents* (arXiv 2502.01450): an all-LLM framework at the much smaller scale of ~100+ agents across four network topologies, demonstrating that network structure, personas, and spreading schemes swing outcomes from no spread to 83% infection — useful as evidence of sensitivity to topology, not as a scaling proof [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01450) .

**AgentSociety** (Piao et al., arXiv 2502.08691) remains the flagship all-LLM urban simulator — over 10,000 agents and 5 million interactions, validated through survey, interview, and intervention experiments on polarization, inflammatory-message spread, UBI, hurricanes, and urban sustainability — and it has since been published in *iFuture* and, more importantly for a builder, superseded by **AgentSociety 2** (`pip install agentsociety2`), which provides LLM-native `PersonAgent`s, a workspace-bound execution model, Ray task scaling, and JSONL replay [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08691) . DeepMind's **Concordia** has likewise moved: v2.0 (tech report arXiv 2507.08892, released August 2025) rebuilds the library on an Entity-Component pattern in which game masters are themselves entities, clocks are optional, "prefabs" replace factories, and an explicit engine structures agent–game-master interaction with serialization and checkpointing support [(cooperativeai.com)](https://www.cooperativeai.com/post/google-deepmind-releases-concordia-library-v2-0) . Concordia's game-master loop is the most directly transplantable design pattern in this entire report for the tier-3 conversation layer (Section 2.3).

### 1.2 Why "more LLM agents" stops paying off — and when scale still helps

The claim that adding LLM agents past a small core degrades fidelity needs careful restating, because the literature actually supports three distinct, narrower claims. The first is a **cost claim**: RumorSphere's ablation removing adaptive grouping (all agents LLM-driven) produced a tenfold increase in resource consumption with no fidelity gain, and its static-top-100-degree core selection actually *hurt* alignment by letting a fixed elite dominate dynamics toward premature opinion convergence [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02172v1) . The second is a **diversity claim**: MF-LLM (NeurIPS 2025, mean-field hybrid) reports that Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct outperformed GPT-4o-mini and DeepSeek-R1 as backbones for population decision dynamics, because larger models emit near-identical responses under identical prompts — "larger models tend to produce uniform responses over time, limiting their ability to capture diverse agent behavior and degrading long-horizon simulation fidelity" [(Nanyang Technological University)](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/boan/papers/NeurIPS25_MFLLM.pdf) . The third is a **calibration claim**: AgentTorch's controlled comparison of three agent configurations on an 8.4M-agent COVID-era New York model found the archetype configuration (8.4M agents, ~100 LLM archetypes) achieved lower held-out forecasting error on infections and unemployment than either a pure-heuristic population or a 100-agent full-LLM sub-sample — the full-LLM configuration sacrifices simulation scale, and scale itself is fidelity-relevant [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10568v1) .

The countervailing finding is a **scaling law for population size**: RumorSphere found 100-agent simulations converge to consensus too quickly and misalign with real dynamics, while the 1M-agent run showed realistic polarization and information-cocoon metrics [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02172v1) ; ElectionSim similarly found simulation error decreases as the sampled population grows, across 340,000 agents [(百度百科)](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/SocioVerse/2618735) . OASIS reports response diversity and perceived helpfulness improving (by 76.5%) when scaling from 196 to 10,196 agents [(MarkTechPost)](https://www.marktechpost.com/2024/12/27/camel-ai-open-sourced-oasis-a-next-generation-simulator-for-realistic-social-media-dynamics-with-one-million-agents/) . Read together, the consistent synthesis is: scale the **population** as large as the math tier allows; keep the **LLM-driven share** small, dynamically assigned, and biased toward conflict boundaries and high-centrality nodes; and prefer small backbones where behavioral variance matters more than reasoning polish. That is precisely the proposed three-tier point in the design space (Figure 2), and at ~1,000 NPCs with ~30 hubs it sits in a region no published system occupies — game-scale rather than social-media-scale — with HiSim's ~1,000-user hybrid as the nearest neighbor [(themoonlight.io)](https://www.themoonlight.io/fr/review/unveiling-the-truth-and-facilitating-change-towards-agent-based-large-scale-social-movement-simulation) .

## 2. Hybrid architectures and the interfaces they define between tiers

### 2.1 Dynamic core/mass partitioning: RumorSphere, HiSim, SocioVerse

RumorSphere's interface contract is the cleanest published specification of a tier seam. Regular agents hold scalar opinions and update via the Deffuant bounded-confidence rule; core agents are full LLM agents with persona (demographics plus Big Five traits), dual memory (personal experience and environmental observations, retrieved by recency/relevance/importance with a reflection mechanism), and a five-action module (post, retweet, reply, like, do nothing). Core-to-regular influence flows through **LLM-based conversion of generated text into opinion scores**; regular-to-regular influence is numeric only; and crucially the *set* of core agents is recomputed each step from information-confusion levels, so an NPC-equivalent would drift in and out of LLM-driven status as rumors reach it [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02172v1) . HiSim implements the same partition with a different interface detail: core users' LLM-generated tweets are converted to numerical attitude scores via an external LLM stance classifier (support/neutral/oppose) plus sentiment-intensity analysis, and the ordinary population is simulated with a menu of five classic opinion-dynamics ABMs (Deffuant BC, Hegselmann–Krause, Relative Agreement, Social Judgment, Lorenz) implemented in Mesa, while the LLM side is built on AgentVerse [(themoonlight.io)](https://www.themoonlight.io/fr/review/unveiling-the-truth-and-facilitating-change-towards-agent-based-large-scale-social-movement-simulation) . Two caveats transfer directly to a game setting: HiSim's influence is **one-directional** (ordinary users do not talk back to core users), and its micro-evaluation found LLM agents systematically struggle to produce neutral content — they over-commit to clear stances, which for a rumor system means the small-LLM tier will exaggerate conviction unless the numeric tier dampens it [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16333) .

SocioVerse (Fudan DISC, arXiv 2504.10157) generalizes the lineage into a "world model" with four alignment components (Social Environment, User Engine, Scenario Engine, Behavior Engine) over a pool of 10 million real user profiles, and its Behavior Engine keeps the hierarchical "LLM-driven core users, ABM-driven ordinary users" driving mechanism explicitly to cap inference cost [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10157) . Its validation style — presidential-election state-level prediction above 90% accuracy, news-feedback attitude distributions scored by NRMSE and KL divergence against real polls — is the macro-alignment template worth copying for regression scenarios (Section 5.2) [(MarkTechPost)](https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/04/26/llms-can-now-simulate-massive-societies-researchers-from-fudan-university-introduce-socioverse-an-llm-agent-driven-world-model-for-social-simulation-with-a-user-pool-of-10-million-real-individuals/) . For the Skyrim design, the transferable lesson across all three systems is that the LLM tier never writes raw text into the shared state: it writes **scores with provenance**, and natural language rides along as display metadata. That inversion — symbolic state is authoritative, LLM text is an annotated view of it — is the single most important interface decision in the architecture.

### 2.2 Archetype and mean-field compression: AgentTorch and MF-LLM

AgentTorch offers a different compression of the same idea: rather than promoting individual agents to LLM status, it clusters the population into **archetypes** — unique combinations of the prompt variables (demographics, disease context, policy context) — queries the LLM once per archetype per timestep, converts the response to a Bernoulli behavior probability, and samples the entire sub-population from that distribution. The framework is differentiable end-to-end, so gradient-assisted calibration fits the symbolic dynamics' parameters against historical time series even with LLMs in the loop, and it has been run at 8.4M agents on commodity hardware with archetypes preserving the speed advantage [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10568v1) . For a 1,000-NPC game world the archetype trick is less about scale and more about **cacheability**: NPCs with identical (belief-bucket, relationship-to-rumor, trait) tuples can share one tier-2 inference, which turns ~30 hub mutations per tick into a handful of unique prompts. The documented trade-off is reduced individual diversity among agents sharing an archetype — acceptable for background NPCs, wrong for named quest characters [(TechTalks)](https://bdtechtalks.com/2024/10/02/agenttorch-llm-agents/) .

MF-LLM compresses in the opposite direction: instead of per-agent LLM calls, a single backbone is fine-tuned (their "IB-Tune" procedure) to generate population decision sequences conditioned on a **mean-field signal** summarizing the population state, so the LLM models the aggregate and individuals are sampled from it. Removing either the mean-field module or the fine-tuning significantly degrades fidelity, and the framework reproduces real rumor-propagation trajectories closely enough to forecast prevalence from partial observations (predicted 0.645 vs. actual 0.643 at one checkpoint) [(Nanyang Technological University)](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/boan/papers/NeurIPS25_MFLLM.pdf) . Two of MF-LLM's findings belong in the design doc verbatim: low token-level NLL does **not** imply high rollout fidelity — a model can be locally fluent and globally wrong, which argues against selecting the tier-2 model by perplexity or generic benchmarks — and, as noted above, smaller backbones preserved behavioral diversity better than frontier models. A mean-field summary channel (e.g., "of your 14 social ties, 9 now believe the rumor, mean confidence 0.7") is also exactly the kind of compact state rendering a 4B model can condition on without a long prompt.

### 2.3 Game-master loops and concurrent brains: Concordia and Project Sid

Concordia v2.0 is the most instructive architecture for the player-facing tier because it formalizes the **adjudication loop** between language and state. Its engine drives a turn cycle in which game-master components respond to typed output requests: `MAKE_OBSERVATION` (generate what the agent perceives), `NEXT_ACTION_SPEC` (define the action format the agent must answer in — including JSON schemas), `NEXT_ACTING` (turn scheduling), `RESOLVE` (adjudicate an attempted action into an outcome), and `NEXT_GAME_MASTER` (context handoff) [(Github)](https://github.com/google-deepmind/concordia/blob/main/CHEATSHEET.md) . State lives in components attached to entities; the LLM proposes, the game master disposes. Mapped onto the Skyrim design: the symbolic belief state plays the game master — tier 3's dialogue model never mutates beliefs directly; it emits an attempted utterance plus structured claims, and the belief store's `RESOLVE` equivalent validates those claims against provenance rules before committing them. Concordia's costs are real but bounded: a 4-agent scenario is ~350 lines of Python, a 7-agent scenario with detailed memories 1,000–1,300 lines, and complex scenarios exceed 2,000 lines — budget accordingly for component wiring rather than prompt authoring [(unu.edu)](https://c3.unu.edu/blog/concordia-simulation-builder-research-education) .

Project Sid (Altera, arXiv 2411.00114) contributes the **concurrency pattern** the tier architecture needs for real-time play. Its PIANO architecture runs ~10 modules (memory, goal generation, social awareness, talking, skill execution, action awareness, etc.) concurrently as stateless functions reading and writing a shared Agent State, with a Cognitive Controller acting as an information bottleneck that synthesizes module outputs into coherent decisions broadcast back to all modules — explicitly so that slow deliberation never blocks fast reactions and so that speech and motor outputs cannot contradict each other [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1) . In its 10–1,000+ agent Minecraft simulations this sustained specialization into roles, collective rule-following with constitutional amendment, and cultural/religious transmission [(substack.com)](https://digitalhumanity.substack.com/p/project-sid-many-agent-simulations) . The cautionary details matter as much as the headline: agents were prone to groundedness failures (claiming to eat nonexistent food) whose errors cascade, and miscommunication between agents (agreeing to pass a pick, then executing a different function) propagated incoherence — both are the failure modes Section 6 tracks, and both argue for the Cognitive-Controller-style bottleneck between the rumor-mutation tier and the dialogue tier rather than letting them share a context window [(andlukyane.com)](https://andlukyane.com/blog/paper-review-piano) .

### 2.4 The shipped-game case: Vox Deorum

Vox Deorum (arXiv 2512.18564, December 2025) is the closest published analog to the whole project: a hybrid LLM+X architecture inside a commercial game (Civilization V with the Vox Populi mod), validated across **2,327 complete games**. The layering is exactly the tier discipline proposed here — the LLM handles only macro-strategic reasoning once per turn, while all micro-execution stays in the deterministic C++ tactical AI. The interface is the part to copy: game state is serialized into a structured **Markdown document** (victory progress, strategic options, player/city/military summaries, events) chosen to minimize tokens; the LLM's response is a selection from predefined enums, which the game applies as adjustments to "flavor" weight numbers inside the existing symbolic AI — the LLM never touches game state directly, it *re-weights a deterministic decision system* [(Emergent Mind)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/vox-populi-mod) . The transport chain (Windows named pipe out of the game process → REST bridge → MCP server exposing `getState()/setStrategy()/setTech()/setPolicy()/setPersona()` → LLM client) is a proven template for SKSE-plugin-to-Python-service plumbing, and the decision to run LLM inference after each player turn — overlapping inference with other players' turns — is the latency-hiding scheduling trick a background rumor tier should adopt [(themoonlight.io)](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/vox-deorum-a-hybrid-llm-architecture-for-4x-grand-strategy-game-ai-lessons-from-civilization-v) .

Equally relevant are the results and their texture: with a deliberately minimal prompt, GPT-OSS-120B and GLM-4.6 both survived competitively against the tuned algorithmic AI (97% mean survival across configurations) while exhibiting play styles that diverged substantially from the symbolic AI and from each other — evidence that a thin LLM layer over a symbolic core yields *behaviorally distinct* agents rather than noise around the same policy, which is precisely what a gossip-hub tier is meant to produce [(Emergent Mind)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/vox-populi-mod) . The emergentmind analysis of hybrid LLM+X systems generalizes the same pattern into interface guidance: strict layering so no tier embeds lower-level dependencies, stateless schema-defined APIs so LLMs never persist or directly access external state, and event-driven orchestration buses between strategic and tactical layers — all three are satisfiable in a Python sidecar service with a typed event stream from the game [(Emergent Mind)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/hybrid-llm-x-architecture) .

## 3. Writing LLM dialogue back into symbolic state

### 3.1 The constrained-decoding stack: guarantees are now free

The 2024-era caution about structured output — that constraining an LLM to JSON cost 5–15% overhead and still needed validate-retry loops — is obsolete. The JSONSchemaBench study (10K real-world schemas, six frameworks) found constrained decoding can *speed up* generation by up to 50% and consistently improves downstream task accuracy by up to 4%, because masking prunes invalid branches of the token space [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10868v1) . The engine choice matters more than the choice to constrain: XGrammar precomputes context-independent token validity offline, cutting mask computation to **under 40 microseconds** per token and delivering up to 14× end-to-end speedups on JSON tasks versus earlier stacks; it is now the default structured-output backend in both vLLM and SGLang [(tianpan.co)](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-16-grammar-constrained-generation-output-reliability) . Microsoft's Guidance (llguidance) goes further — when the grammar uniquely determines the next token it skips sampling entirely, landing at ~6–9 ms per token versus ~15–16 ms unconstrained — while llama.cpp's built-in GBNF grammars (with automatic JSON-Schema-to-GBNF conversion) are the right tool for a local, single-process deployment [(tianpan.co)](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-16-grammar-constrained-generation-output-reliability) . The one real cost left is grammar **compilation** for complex schemas: Outlines can take 3–12 seconds per unique schema (prohibitive if schemas vary per request), XGrammar 0.12–0.30 s, Guidance under 60 ms — so a fixed, versioned set of belief-update schemas compiled once at startup eliminates the issue entirely [(tianpan.co)](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-16-grammar-constrained-generation-output-reliability) .

A subtlety worth internalizing: constrained decoding guarantees **format**, not **semantics** — a grammar-constrained 4B model will emit perfectly parseable nonsense if the underlying judgment is wrong, so schema validation must be paired with content validation (provenance checks, referent existence checks against the NPC roster, confidence bounds) in the symbolic layer [(tianpan.co)](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-16-grammar-constrained-generation-output-reliability) . This maps cleanly onto the tier contract: tier 3's dialogue model emits `belief_delta` JSON constrained by GBNF; the belief store rejects deltas whose subject/object aren't known entities, whose claimed source event doesn't exist in the event log, or whose confidence exceeds what the evidence type permits. The Reddit practitioner consensus on small local agents converges on the same point — GBNF/XGrammar pinning means "the model physically cannot" emit malformed structure, which is what makes 3B–8B models viable as state-writing components at all [(reddit.com)](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLM/comments/1txny6k/the_wall_for_small_local_models_as_agents_isnt/) .

### 3.2 Architectural patterns for belief write-back

Four write-back patterns recur across the surveyed systems, ordered here from most to least conservative. **(1) Score sidecar**: the LLM produces narrative text, and a separate extraction step (stance classifier, sentiment scorer, or a second constrained LLM call) converts the text to numeric deltas — HiSim and RumorSphere's seam; cheapest, and the extractor can be a 1B–3B model [(themoonlight.io)](https://www.themoonlight.io/fr/review/unveiling-the-truth-and-facilitating-change-towards-agent-based-large-scale-social-movement-simulation) . **(2) Dual-channel generation**: one constrained call emits both `utterance` (free text, player-facing) and `state_delta` (grammar-constrained JSON) fields in a single schema — the natural tier-3 pattern, used implicitly by Concordia's action specs that define JSON response formats inside the call-to-action [(Github)](https://github.com/google-deepmind/concordia/blob/main/CHEATSHEET.md) . **(3) Tool/function calling**: the dialogue model invokes typed functions (`record_rumor(subject, predicate, object, confidence, source)`) under the serving engine's tool-use machinery; reliability at small scale is now good — on the Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard, ToolACE-8B (≈91.5 overall) and FunRL-tuned Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (86.0) match or beat GPT-4-0125 (83.4), so a 7–8B function-calling specialist is a legitimate tier-3 option if the dialogue model is kept separate [(Emergent Mind)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/berkeley-function-calling-leaderboard-v4-bfclv4) . **(4) Game-master adjudication**: the LLM only *proposes* in natural language; a symbolic resolver interprets and commits — Vox Deorum's flavor-weight adjustment is the extreme, safest form [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18564) .

For persistent memory specifically, the Generative Agents memory stream remains the reference write-back discipline: every event stored as an atomic natural-language observation with timestamp, embedding, and an LLM-assigned poignancy score; retrieval scores recency × relevance × importance (implementation weights roughly 0.5/3.0/2.0 in modern reproductions, equal weights in the original); and reflection triggers when cumulative poignancy crosses a threshold, writing synthesized higher-level insights *back into the same stream* as retrievable thought nodes [(Agent Patterns)](https://agentpatterns.ai/agent-design/generative-agents-memory-stream/) . Two practitioner lessons from that lineage apply to the belief store: store observations as natural-language sentences rather than raw JSON (embeddings retrieve sentences far better), and treat reflection writes as first-class beliefs with provenance type "inference" so they can decay or be contradicted differently from witnessed events [(Agent Patterns)](https://agentpatterns.ai/agent-design/generative-agents-memory-stream/) . Project Sid's addition is the anti-corruption rule: concurrent modules may read shared state freely, but writes should funnel through one bottleneck component that enforces coherence, because concurrent LLM writers produce mutually contradictory state (agents saying one thing while doing another) [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1) .

### 3.3 Small models at structured output: what to expect

The honest expectation-setting for 1B–8B writers: format compliance is guaranteed by the grammar, but **semantic reliability degrades with schema complexity** faster than with frontier models, so schemas should be flat, few-fielded, and enum-heavy. The BFCL numbers show fine-tuned 7–8B specialists can match GPT-4-class tool use, but off-the-shelf 3–4B instruct models sit well below that; the gap is largely closed by constraining the output space (small enums, bounded integers) rather than by prompting harder [(Emergent Mind)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/berkeley-function-calling-leaderboard-v4-bfclv4) . Where the task is genuinely generative — the tier-2 rumor mutation — the constraint should apply only to the metadata fields (mutation type, confidence adjustment, target), leaving the mutated text itself free-form within a grammar-delimited string field.

The other small-model trap documented in the sources is **reasoning-token blowup**: compact reasoning models that emit thousands of hidden tokens can cost more latency than a larger non-reasoning model answering directly, so tier-2/tier-3 local models should run in non-thinking mode with explicit short-output instructions (Qwen3's instruct variants support exactly this split) [(turingpost.com)](https://www.turingpost.com/p/slmslist) . Finally, SGLang's own documentation carries the still-valid caveat that even with grammar backends enabled, prompts should explicitly describe the desired format — constraint and instruction are complements, not substitutes [(sglang.ai)](https://docs.sglang.ai/advanced_features/structured_outputs.html) .

## 4. Local small-model practicality in 2026

### 4.1 The 1B–8B landscape for persona-conditioned rewriting

The 2026 small-model field is crowded, but the tier-2 job description — *persona-conditioned rewriting of short texts, plus motivated-reasoning judgment, in continuous batches, on a GPU shared with a game* — narrows it quickly. Current roundups converge on a short list: **Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507** (Apache 2.0, 256K context, strongest agentic/tool-calling posture in class), **Phi-4-mini-instruct** (3.8B, MIT, 128K, strong structured-workflow behavior), **SmolLM3-3B** (Apache 2.0, fully open training pipeline, attractive if the mod ever redistributes fine-tunes), **Gemma-3n-E2B** (~2B effective, Gemma license terms need a close read before shipping), and **Llama-3.2-1B/3B** (weakest license, most mature tooling) [(Intuz)](https://www.intuz.com/blog/best-small-language-models/) . At the top of the band, Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.3-8B (~5.2–5.5 GB at Q4_K_M) remain the quality ceiling that still fits beside a game on an 8 GB card [(PromptQuorum)](https://www.promptquorum.com/local-llms/best-beginner-local-llm-models) . Newer 2026 releases (Qwen3.5-4B/9B, Gemma 4's small tiers, Ministral 3) are appearing on self-hosted leaderboards with materially better scores — Qwen3.5-4B already sits in tier-A territory on one 2026 ranking — but the Apache-2.0 Qwen line remains the safest default for a redistributable free mod [(Onyx AI)](https://onyx.app/self-hosted-llm-leaderboard) .

For the specific task of *rumor mutation*, generic benchmarks are the wrong selector — MF-LLM's warning that token-level likelihood does not predict rollout fidelity applies directly [(Nanyang Technological University)](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/boan/papers/NeurIPS25_MFLLM.pdf) . The right selector is a task-level harness: take 50 seed rumors, run each candidate model through a mutation chain, and score semantic fidelity drift and persona distinctiveness (Section 5). Expect a 4B-class instruct model to be the sweet spot; the persona-drift literature warns that smaller models actually drift *less* in some long-horizon settings, and MF-LLM found small backbones preserve behavioral variance better — both unusual cases where the cheap option is also the more faithful one [(substack.com)](https://neuralhorizons.substack.com/p/robo-psychology-13-the-ai-persona) . Where a hub NPC needs an especially distinct voice, a per-persona LoRA adapter (a few hundred MB, hot-swappable in llama.cpp/vLLM) over one shared 4B base is the standard 2026 pattern and keeps VRAM flat as hub count grows.

### 4.2 Quantization, VRAM, and latency on consumer GPUs

The memory arithmetic is settled and should drive provisioning: at **Q4_K_M (~4.5 bits/param) a model needs ≈0.56 GB per billion parameters** for weights; Q5_K_M ≈0.69 GB/B; Q8_0 ≈1.0 GB/B — plus 1–2 GB for a 4K-token KV cache and runtime overhead [(Kunal Ganglani)](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/running-local-llms-2026-hardware-setup-guide) . That puts the whole tier-2 candidate set at 0.7–5.3 GB of weights (Figure 3). Against that, Skyrim itself occupies roughly **2 GB of VRAM** in the Mantella community's measurements, whose practitioners report local LLMs becoming practical at ~8 GB total VRAM with ~6 GB free for the model — meaning a 4B Q4 model (~3 GB + ~1 GB KV) coexists comfortably on a single 8 GB card, and an 8B Q4 is feasible on 12 GB [(reddit.com)](https://www.reddit.com/r/skyrimmods/comments/1cn7wbt/my_current_experience_with_mantella_im_excited/) . Speed: single-stream 8B-class inference on an RTX 4090-class card sits at ~62–71 tok/s regardless of engine once quantization is equalized, and smaller models scale roughly inversely with size — a 4B model should sustain well over 100 tok/s, so a 60-token mutated rumor costs well under a second of GPU time [(Sesame Disk)](https://sesamedisk.com/llamacpp-vs-vllm-vs-sglang-vs-ollama-2026/) .

Two quantization rules of thumb from the 2026 practitioner literature belong in the build notes. First, **a larger Q4 model usually beats a smaller Q8 model in the same memory budget** — spend VRAM on parameters, not precision [(daily.dev)](https://daily.dev/blog/best-local-llm-models-run/) . Second, quality-sensitive sub-tasks can mix precisions: run the mutation model at Q4_K_M but keep a Q6_K variant for the player-facing extraction if artifacts appear. The CPU fallback also matters for low-end users of a distributed mod: 2–3B models run at 40–60 tok/s on an 8-core laptop CPU, so a degraded "tier-2-on-CPU" mode is a legitimate accessibility option, with the trade-off that background inference then contends with the game for CPU instead of GPU [(PromptQuorum)](https://www.promptquorum.com/local-llms/best-beginner-local-llm-models) .

### 4.3 Batching strategies and co-residency with a running game

The engine divide that matters is single-stream versus concurrent. For one request at a time, llama.cpp, Ollama, and vLLM are within ~10% of each other; the moment many NPCs need background inference, the architectures diverge — at ~50 concurrent requests, vLLM's PagedAttention plus continuous batching sustains roughly **920 tok/s aggregate** where queue-based engines plateau near 155 tok/s [(Sesame Disk)](https://sesamedisk.com/llamacpp-vs-vllm-vs-sglang-vs-ollama-2026/) . A gossip-hub tier generates exactly this workload: dozens of short, independent mutation requests per game tick. The practical serving shape is a local vLLM (or SGLang) server holding the 4B model with continuous batching, a per-tick budget (e.g., ≤32 mutations/tick), and XGrammar-constrained metadata; llama.cpp with GBNF is the lighter alternative if one process and minimal dependencies matter more than aggregate throughput [(sglang.ai)](https://docs.sglang.ai/advanced_features/structured_outputs.html) .

Co-residency with the game has three documented levers. **Prefix caching**: hub persona prompts share long static prefixes (personality card, world rules), and prefix caching removes that prefill cost per request — it is the single highest-value optimization for this workload [(Jarvislabs.ai)](https://jarvislabs.ai/blog/vllm-optimization-techniques) . **Sleep/wake cycling**: vLLM's sleep mode offloads weights to CPU RAM and discards KV cache, freeing 90%+ of GPU memory with ~2–3 s (L1) or ~7–8 s (L2) wake times — designed for RL colocation but equally usable to yield VRAM during graphically heavy scenes, or to alternate the tier-2 and tier-3 models on one GPU [(vllm.ai)](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/sleep_mode/) . **Turn-aligned scheduling**: Vox Deorum's pattern of running inference while the human (or other agents) takes their turn generalizes — trigger rumor-mutation batches on game events (cell changes, time waits, dialogue endings) rather than on a free-running loop, so GPU contention never lands mid-frame [(themoonlight.io)](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/vox-deorum-a-hybrid-llm-architecture-for-4x-grand-strategy-game-ai-lessons-from-civilization-v) . If contention still shows up as frame-time spikes, `gpu_memory_utilization` caps plus process priority are the crude-but-effective fallback; the co-located RL literature notes colocation "works best when GPU memory is carefully managed... not ideal for models with tight memory budgets" [(Hugging Face)](https://huggingface.co/blog/vllm-colocate) .

### 4.4 Rumor mutation and motivated reasoning on small models: what the evidence says

The tier-2 fantasy — gossip that semantically mutates as it passes through biased retellers — has direct experimental grounding in the "LLM telephone game" literature, and the grounding is double-edged. Perez et al. (ICLR 2025) ran transmission chains and found LLMs exhibit strong **cultural attractors**: chains converge toward model-specific orbits (more positive, simpler text), with open-ended instructions producing far stronger attraction than constrained rephrasing [(Joshua8.AI)](https://joshua8.ai/llm-telephone-game-semantic-drift/) . Mohamed et al. (ACL 2025, *LLM as a Broken Telephone*) showed iterative rephrasing/translation chains accumulate factual distortion rapidly — a lorry accident becoming a bus explosion within 50 iterations — and, critically for design, that distortion is **mitigable but not eliminable**: temperature 0 and restrictive "preserve every fact" prompting slow the drift, meaning the knob between "rumors mutate believably" and "rumors rot into noise" is literally temperature plus prompt constraint [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20258v1) . Multi-model chains degrade faster than single-model chains, so all hubs should share one backbone with persona LoRAs rather than different models per hub [(ACL Anthology)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.371.pdf) . A 17-model local replication in 2026 confirms the effect across open-weight models and points to Rath's *Agent Drift* taxonomy (semantic, coordination, behavioral drift; drift only measurable after 20–100+ interactions) as the monitoring vocabulary [(Joshua8.AI)](https://joshua8.ai/llm-telephone-game-semantic-drift/) .

For **motivated reasoning** — hubs that disbelieve what contradicts their grudges — the evidence says LLMs do not do it by default and must be made to. Chuang et al. found LLM agents have a strong fact-oriented bias that erodes assigned misinformation personas mid-conversation, and introduced the **confirmation-bias prompt** ("you will be more likely to believe information that supports your ideology…") to stabilize belief maintenance; a 2025 partisan-polarization replication confirmed the prompt works (agents hold stances against the model's inherent pull toward moderation) though a residual convergence tendency remains [(MDPI)](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4591/107/1/2) . Taubenfeld et al.'s underlying finding — partisan LLM agents fail to polarize in conversation as humans do, dragged instead by training-data biases — means an un-prompted rumor tier will *under*-dramatize motivated distortion [(MDPI)](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4591/107/1/2) . Persona-assigned models do show strong identity-congruency effects on contentious judgments (up to 90% accuracy swings in Dash et al.), but base models without personas under-produce the variance and direction of human motivated reasoning [(Emergent Mind)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/political-motivated-reasoning-studies) . Design consequence: the symbolic tier should own the *direction* of bias (grudge → discount evidence from the grudge target, with a numeric credibility multiplier), and the LLM should only own the *rationalization text* — asking the model to spontaneously generate the bias itself will produce fact-tending mush.

## 5. Evaluation: how anyone validates that a social sim "behaves plausibly"

### 5.1 Micro-level: replication tests, believability interviews, and social-intelligence batteries

The micro-evaluation canon has three layers. The oldest is Aher, Arriaga & Kalai's **Turing Experiment** (ICML 2023): replicate canonical human-subject findings (Ultimatum Game, garden-path sentences, Milgram, wisdom of crowds) with a population of simulated participants and compare effect patterns — with the methodological discipline of tuning prompts *without* looking at outcomes to avoid p-hacking, and the cautionary "hyper-accuracy distortion" they found in wisdom-of-crowds (models' crowd averages are suspiciously better than human crowds) [(Github)](https://github.com/microsoft/turing-experiments) . The second is the **believability interview** from Generative Agents — interrogate agents about self-knowledge, memory, plans, and relationships and have raters score whether responses cohere with stored state, run as an ablation (full architecture vs. no-memory/no-reflection variants) so the score measures the architecture rather than the base model [(Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-025-11412-6) . The third and most developed is **SOTOPIA-EVAL**: seven dimensions (goal completion, believability, knowledge acquisition, secret-keeping, relationship, social rules, financial outcomes) scored per episode by human or LLM raters, with published human-agreement statistics per dimension and open annotation protocols [(Emergent Mind)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/sotopia-interactive-social-evaluation-benchmark) .

Each layer carries a documented caveat that should shape how much weight it gets. Believability ratings from crowd-workers correlate only modestly across dimensions (SOTOPIA's own agreement table ranges from ~0.2 to ~0.95 depending on dimension), and **LLM-as-judge is circular and drifts**: SOTOPIA-π found GPT-4-based evaluation progressively *overestimated* trained agents' social intelligence relative to human raters as the agents were optimized against GPT-4 feedback — if you train or tune against an LLM judge, you cannot then validate with the same judge [(alphaxiv.org)](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2403.08715) . The strongest micro-instrument available is the Stanford 1,052-person study's benchmark: agents grounded in two-hour interviews replicated participants' General Social Survey answers at **83–86% of the participants' own two-week test-retest consistency**, versus 74% for demographics-only agents — both a calibration target (test-retest reliability is the ceiling, not 100%) and a demonstration that rich per-agent grounding data beats demographic stubs [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109) . For the Skyrim system, the practical micro-suite is: canned interview probes per NPC ("what do you think of X? what did you hear about Y?") scored against the symbolic belief store, plus a SOTOPIA-style believability rubric applied by human raters on a small dialogue sample.

### 5.2 Macro-level: distribution and time-series alignment against ground truth

Macro validation compares aggregate simulation trajectories to reference data, and the rumor-simulation literature has standardized the metric set. RumorSphere scores opinion-distribution alignment (ΔBias on means, ΔDiv on variance) and opinion-series alignment (**Dynamic Time Warping distance and Pearson correlation** against real event time series) [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02172v1) . SocioVerse uses accuracy/RMSE for election prediction and NRMSE plus **KL divergence** for attitude-distribution alignment against real polls [(MarkTechPost)](https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/04/26/llms-can-now-simulate-massive-societies-researchers-from-fudan-university-introduce-socioverse-an-llm-agent-driven-world-model-for-social-simulation-with-a-user-pool-of-10-million-real-individuals/) . OASIS reports normalized RMSE (~30%) on information-propagation curves against real X data [(MarkTechPost)](https://www.marktechpost.com/2024/12/27/camel-ai-open-sourced-oasis-a-next-generation-simulator-for-realistic-social-media-dynamics-with-one-million-agents/) . HiSim adds micro-consistency checks: stance-prediction accuracy above 70%, action-type prediction above 75%, content cosine similarity near 0.7 against real user behavior [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16333) . For a game world there is no real-world ground truth, but the transfer is direct: pick in-world reference phenomena with known desired dynamics (a murder rumor should saturate a hold in N days; a debunked rumor should decay with a half-life) and run the same DTW/distribution-alignment comparisons against the *designer's* target curves — this converts vibes-based tuning into a regression-testable artifact.

One structural warning from the validation literature belongs here: macro alignment is necessary but not sufficient, because multiple micro-mechanisms can produce the same macro curve — RumorSphere's own ablations show a wrong core-selection strategy can still land near the right aggregate in some metrics while being mechanistically off [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02172v1) . The 2025 *Artificial Intelligence Review* critical survey makes the general case bluntly: validation is "the central challenge" of generative social simulation, profile-alignment ground truth is usually unattainable, and the field's three dominant strategies (expert judgment, crowd believability, LLM judges) each carry known reliability problems, with LLM-judge circularity called out as increasingly common and methodologically problematic [(Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-025-11412-6) . The defense is triangulation: at least one macro metric, one micro instrument, and one human-rated believability sample, each sensitive to a different failure.

### 5.3 Regression scenarios for a persistent game world

The games-specific evaluation gap is that nothing in the literature addresses save/load persistence over hundreds of hours, so the regression harness has to be built — but its components all exist. **Scenario batteries**: scripted event sequences (NPC dies publicly; NPC dies secretly with one witness; player falsely accused) with invariant assertions on the symbolic state (provenance chains terminate at real events; belief strengths stay in bounds; grudge ledgers stay symmetric where the model requires it). **Determinism windows**: with temperature 0 and fixed seeds, the math tier is exactly replayable and the LLM tiers are replayable enough for smoke tests — Concordia v2.0's built-in serialization/checkpointing and AgentSociety 2's JSONL replay exist precisely for this [(cooperativeai.com)](https://www.cooperativeai.com/post/google-deepmind-releases-concordia-library-v2-0) . **Drift dashboards**: Rath's Agent Stability Index framing — semantic, coordination, and behavioral drift scored over 20–100+ interaction windows — gives the metric vocabulary for catching slow degradation that per-scenario tests miss [(Joshua8.AI)](https://joshua8.ai/llm-telephone-game-semantic-drift/) .

Survey-instrument calibration is the last piece: the GSS-style approach of administering a fixed questionnaire to a sample of NPCs at intervals and checking the *distribution* of answers against its own baseline (not against humans) catches population-level drift toward homogeneity or attractor states — the failure mode MF-LLM and the telephone-game studies both predict [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109) . Expert rating retains a role for dialogue quality: SOTOPIA's believability dimension operationalizes "would this character say this" with published annotation standards, and the role-play survey literature adds the NPC-specific check of **knowledge hallucination** — whether dialogue asserts lore inconsistent with the game world — which is exactly what a provenance-checked belief store can score automatically by string-matching utterances against known-false beliefs [(jenova.ai)](https://www.jenova.ai/en/resources/how-do-you-keep-ai-roleplay-characters-from-breaking-character-or-contradicting-lore) .

## 6. Failure modes in persistent LLM-agent simulations, and their mitigations

### 6.1 Persona and tone drift

Persona drift — agents sliding out of assigned character over long horizons — is now a measured phenomenon rather than anecdote. A late-2024 nine-model study found styles and self-consistency drift noticeably from initial persona assignments over extended conversations, with the counterintuitive result that **larger, more capable models drifted more** than smaller ones [(substack.com)](https://neuralhorizons.substack.com/p/robo-psychology-13-the-ai-persona) . Mechanism analyses attribute drift to loose post-training tethering (the "assistant" persona is weakly anchored and meta-reflective or emotionally charged prompts knock the model into other archetypes) and to contextual dilution, where long episodic histories wash out the identity tokens [(Emergent Mind)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/persona-drift) . Lifelong-SOTOPIA operationalized the consequence: across multi-episode interaction chains, **goal achievement and believability decline monotonically for every tested model**, and even agents with an advanced ~200–300-word memory-chunk method stayed significantly below humans on history-dependent scenarios [(Hugging Face)](https://huggingface.co/papers/2506.12666) . For a Skyrim playthrough measured in hundreds of hours, this is the tier-3 tier's primary risk: the innkeeper slowly becoming a generic helpful assistant — the "formality collapse" the modding community reports — is the same phenomenon under a different name.

Mitigations with published effect sizes: multi-turn RL against persona-consistency rewards (prompt-to-line, line-to-line, and Q&A consistency metrics) **reduced inconsistency by over 55%** in the NeurIPS 2025 work that introduced those metrics [(NeurIPS)](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/119491) ; SPASM's egocentric context projection — storing dialogue in a perspective-agnostic memory and re-rendering each agent's view at generation time — measurably slowed drift growth on concerns/emotion/motivation probes at temperature 0 [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09212v1) . The practitioner stack aligns: keep persona in a structured, always-in-context card; keep world facts in a retrievable lorebook rather than the rolling context; re-inject tone directives periodically (constant memory entries); and regenerate rather than accept drifted outputs so drift never becomes the new baseline [(jenova.ai)](https://www.jenova.ai/en/resources/how-do-you-keep-ai-roleplay-characters-from-breaking-character-or-contradicting-lore) . All four are cheap and should be considered baseline engineering, with periodic probe-based drift scoring (Section 5.3) as the tripwire.

### 6.2 Memory fabrication and error cascades

The second family is factual: agents inventing events, and those inventions propagating. Project Sid documented groundedness failures at the single-agent level (claiming to eat nonexistent food) cascading into dysfunctional behavior, plus cross-agent miscommunication where agreed actions diverge from executed ones [(andlukyane.com)](https://andlukyane.com/blog/paper-review-piano) . A 2026 claim-level study of multi-agent cascades quantified the dynamics: hallucination behaves as a stochastic process through agent chains — later agents on average *attenuate* hallucination (−0.072 per transition, p<0.001) but at the cost of factual decay and semantic drift, and stability depends on a per-hop propagation coefficient staying below 1; injected hallucinations can persist, soften, amplify, or be "corrected" into new errors [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07937v1) . The security literature has independently codified the same anatomy as OWASP **ASI08 (Cascading Failures)**: hallucination, memory poisoning, and tool corruption propagate via shared memory and agent-to-agent trust, amplify through feedback loops and memory persistence, and manifest as fan-out, oscillation, or state-corruption patterns — with the pointed observation that most pipeline validation is syntactic (valid JSON) rather than semantic (true claims) [(Adversa AI)](https://adversa.ai/blog/cascading-failures-in-agentic-ai-complete-owasp-asi08-security-guide-2026/) . Industry postmortems put production failure rates of unorchestrated multi-agent systems above 40%, with specification failures (~42%), coordination breakdowns (~37%), and verification gaps (~21%) as the split [(galileo.ai)](https://galileo.ai/blog/multi-agent-ai-failures-prevention) .

Mitigations follow from the mechanism. **Provenance as a hard type constraint**: every belief carries its source event ID, and the symbolic store rejects claims referencing nonexistent events — this converts memory fabrication from a silent poison into a validation error, and is the single highest-leverage defense for this design. **Trust-weighted propagation**: the math tier already supports per-source credibility multipliers; the cascade literature shows uncorrected errors persist when downstream agents treat upstream text as confirmed fact, so rumor text passed between tiers should carry its confidence and mutation count, and the telephone-game finding that longer chains degrade monotonically argues for capping chain depth or re-grounding long chains against the original event record [(TFSF Ventures)](https://www.tfsfventures.com/blog/hallucination-propagation-and-cascade-failures-in-multi-agent-systems) . **Write bottleneck**: one adjudicating component commits belief writes (Project Sid's Cognitive Controller pattern), preventing concurrent LLM writers from interleaving contradictory state [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1) . **Periodic re-grounding**: scheduled jobs that diff high-confidence beliefs against the event log and flag orphan beliefs catch slow poisoning before it compounds — the observability/circuit-breaker role OWASP assigns to cascade defense [(Adversa AI)](https://adversa.ai/blog/cascading-failures-in-agentic-ai-complete-owasp-asi08-security-guide-2026/) .

### 6.3 Homogeneity, sycophancy, and attractor collapse

The third family is distributional: simulations that quietly stop being a population. MF-LLM's central negative result — large backbones produce homogeneous agent responses that destroy behavioral diversity and long-horizon fidelity — is the headline [(Nanyang Technological University)](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/boan/papers/NeurIPS25_MFLLM.pdf) . The telephone-game attractors are the temporal version: iterated generation converges to model-specific orbits (simpler, more positive text), so a rumor ecology left to iterate will drift toward the model's stylistic attractor regardless of game events [(Joshua8.AI)](https://joshua8.ai/llm-telephone-game-semantic-drift/) . RumorSphere's 100-agent condition shows the small-population version: premature consensus, lowest polarization, highest cocooning — dynamics that look tidy and are wrong [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02172v1) . The motivated-reasoning studies add the social version: unprompted LLM partisans *moderate* toward each other instead of polarizing, pulled by training-data biases [(MDPI)](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4591/107/1/2) . And Aher's hyper-accuracy distortion — simulated crowds outperforming real ones — is the measurement version: the sim can look *better* than reality in ways that signal artifact [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10264) .

Mitigations are about injecting and protecting variance. Temperature and constraint tuning per role (higher temperature for mutation, lower for extraction) with the diversity metrics of Section 5.2 as the guardrail; small backbones for the masses-touching tier, per MF-LLM; persona-grounding rich enough to matter — the Stanford study's 74%-vs-86% gap between demographics-only and interview-grounded agents quantifies what thin personas cost [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109) ; and explicit bias ownership in the symbolic layer (Section 4.4) so that divergence is driven by modeled grudges rather than model whim. Where homogeneity is detected, the correction is structural (more distinct persona conditioning, re-grounding against event data), not prompt exhortation — debiasing prompts against identity-driven distortion showed minimal efficacy in the motivated-reasoning literature [(Emergent Mind)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/political-motivated-reasoning-studies) .

## 7. Synthesis: concrete recommendations for the three-tier build

**Architecture.** Adopt the four-seam interface contract (Section 2.5): the symbolic belief store is the only authority on state; tier 1 speaks numeric scores only; tier 2 consumes a compact context package (mean-field social summary + persona card + rumor object) and returns free-text mutation plus grammar-constrained metadata; tier 3 consumes belief snapshots rendered as structured Markdown and returns dual-channel output (utterance + constrained `belief_delta`), with every write adjudicated against provenance rules before commit. Dynamic promotion of hub status — RumorSphere's conflict-boundary criterion — is worth prototyping even at 30 hubs, because static top-degree selection measurably distorted RumorSphere's dynamics [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02172v1) . The transport layer can follow Vox Deorum's named-pipe → REST → MCP chain, which was built for exactly this game-to-Python-service geometry [(themoonlight.io)](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/vox-deorum-a-hybrid-llm-architecture-for-4x-grand-strategy-game-ai-lessons-from-civilization-v) .

**Models and serving.** Tier 2: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 at Q4_K_M (~3 GB) on a local vLLM server with continuous batching, prefix caching, XGrammar/GBNF-constrained metadata, temperature ≈0.7–0.9 for mutation creativity, per-persona LoRAs for the most distinctive hubs, and per-tick mutation budgets aligned to game events; Phi-4-mini (MIT) and SmolLM3-3B (Apache 2.0, cleanest redistribution story) as fallback and low-end options, with a CPU 2–3B degraded mode for minimum-spec users [(Intuz)](https://www.intuz.com/blog/best-small-language-models/) . Do not select the model on generic benchmarks — build the 50-seed-rumor mutation harness and select on measured drift and persona distinctiveness, per MF-LLM's NLL≠fidelity warning [(Nanyang Technological University)](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/boan/papers/NeurIPS25_MFLLM.pdf) . Tier 3: if the conversation model is local, a function-calling specialist (ToolACE-class 8B) or a general 8B with GBNF-constrained sidecar JSON covers write-back; if it is API-based, schema-constrained outputs are native [(Emergent Mind)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/berkeley-function-calling-leaderboard-v4-bfclv4) . Keep tier 2 and tier 3 as separate processes/models so the dialogue tier's long context never contends with mutation throughput; vLLM sleep mode can timeshare one GPU if hardware forces co-residency [(vllm.ai)](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/sleep_mode/) .

**Evaluation and operations.** Ship the harness with the mod: macro alignment of rumor curves against designer target dynamics (DTW/Pearson), micro interview probes scored against the belief store, a fixed NPC questionnaire tracked over playtime for homogeneity drift, per-hop rumor fidelity scoring to keep mutation in the believable band, and a small human-rated believability sample per release using SOTOPIA-style rubrics — never an LLM judge that any component was tuned against [(alphaxiv.org)](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2403.08715) . Treat the known failure budget as design input: believability *will* decay over long sessions (Lifelong-SOTOPIA), rumors *will* drift toward model attractors (telephone-game studies), and fabricated memories *will* attempt to enter the store (cascade literature) — the architecture above is specifically the set of seams at which each of those is caught [(Hugging Face)](https://huggingface.co/papers/2506.12666) .

**What to build on vs. study.** Build directly on: vLLM/SGLang serving with XGrammar (or llama.cpp GBNF for minimal footprint), the Concordia game-master output-type loop as the adjudication reference, HiSim's Mesa-based ABM menu for the tier-1 propagation models, and AgentSociety 2's replay/serialization approach for the regression harness [(sglang.ai)](https://docs.sglang.ai/advanced_features/structured_outputs.html) . Study but do not adopt wholesale: RumorSphere (no official code; take the DIS idea and the text→score seam), OASIS (right infrastructure ideas at 100× your needed scale), MF-LLM (mean-field conditioning and its small-model finding), and Project Sid (the concurrency/bottleneck pattern; its Minecraft stack is irrelevant to you) [(arXiv.org)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02172) . The one genuinely missing piece in the literature — a hybrid belief system validated over *hundreds of real-time hours with a human in the loop* — is the thing this project would contribute back.
