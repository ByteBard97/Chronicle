# Chronicle: Vision (v3.0)

**Status:** A rewrite, not a surgical revision. This is the first time this document's core bet has changed rather than been extended. Supersedes vision-v2.2.md. The trigger: v2.2's headline pitch was subjective belief/rumor/grudge tracking between the player and individual NPCs, personal-scale, slow to notice, player-centric. Direct owner verdict on that framing: "boring... nobody will install a mod because grudges between you and NPCs gets tracked." What follows isn't a patch on that pitch. It's a different headline, arrived at through the same research discipline v2.2 itself established (nothing asserted without a research base, primary sources checked directly rather than trusted secondhand), applied to a single question: if not grudge-tracking, what would actually make a Skyrim player feel the world is alive and in motion?

**What changed from v2.2:** the headline moves from "NPCs remember what you did to them" to **the world has its own agenda, and you can watch it move.** The civil war and dragon attacks become living, multi-phase conflicts with real casualties and real consequences, delivered through Bethesda's own Radiant Story engine rather than a competing quest system, cast with real named NPCs whose grievances and loyalties the player actually shaped. Belief, rumor, and grudge (everything v2.2 called Bet 1) don't disappear. They become the epistemology *underneath* the headline instead of the headline itself: the data the world-event layer draws on to decide who gets cast, what a rumor says by the time it reaches the third county, and why a given NPC broke down instead of shrugged. This reframing is backed by an unusually large research base for a single pivot: an exhaustive ~100-item catalog of comparative-game social mechanics (Crusader Kings, Kenshi, Shadows of Doubt, the Nemesis System, RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress), five independent AI agents' Skyrim-implementation brainstorms against it, a dedicated Sims mechanics investigation (verdict: weak precedent for this specific goal, despite being the most iconic reference), primary-source verification of Chronicle's own foundational academic citation (Talk of the Town, confirmed accurate), and, the finding that makes this buildable rather than aspirational, direct confirmation that **Bethesda's own Radiant Story engine already implements the exact casting mechanism this pitch needs**, across three shipped titles, with a documented integration surface. All of it is filed under `docs/research/comparative-systems/`, entry point `00-master-feature-synthesis-2026-09-06.md`.

---

## 1. The dream

Skyrim's civil war is a fixed slider: Whiterun falls when the quest says so, to whichever side the player picked, with the same three barks either way. Skyrim's dragons are a random-encounter table: a beast drops from the sky, gets killed, and the town it razed resumes its exact prior state by the next load screen. Nothing about either system has a memory, a trajectory, or a reason.

Chronicle makes the war and the crisis *mean* something, because it gives Skyrim's world an actual belief-driven momentum underneath the events players already recognize. The civil war becomes a **multi-phase conflict** (Uneasy Peace, Agitation, Crackdown or Concession, Open Hostility, Aftermath) that a hold moves through based on what real NPCs actually believe and do: a Stormcloak recruiter's success in one town, a player's own informant work in another, a dragon's granary raid blamed on whichever side the rumor mill decides was negligent. Dragon attacks get the same escalating treatment regionally: Rumored, Sighted, Predatory, Under Siege. And when the moment the player has been building toward for weeks finally arrives, when Balgruuf commits and the battle for Whiterun starts, Chronicle doesn't hand the player a static battle map. It hands them a town whose sides they watched form: the smith who refused Grey-Mane custom for a month, the priest whose crowds got bigger every week, the arrest that generated real grievance in the crowd that watched it happen. When it's over, the Aftermath isn't a texture swap. It's a funeral with the actual casualty list, a memorial stone with real names, and a month of visibly altered schedules before the hold decays back toward peace.

The mechanism that makes this affordable rather than a hand-authored fantasy is **storylet role-casting**: Chronicle authors quest and scene *shapes* with role slots and preconditions, and its belief engine casts real, specific NPCs into those slots based on what actually happened in the playthrough. The specific person with the specific grievance, not a random unvisited dungeon. The thing that makes this buildable now instead of a research aspiration is a finding this session confirmed directly against primary sources: **Bethesda's own Radiant Story engine already does this.** Its "Find Matching Reference" alias-fill mechanism is a near-verbatim match to the academic definition of a parametrized storylet, the same category of thing King of Dragon Pass's own creator uses the term for. Vanilla radiant quests pick "a dungeon you haven't visited." Chronicle's belief graph picks who and where, and Radiant Story's existing, shipped, community-trusted casting-and-delivery machinery does the rest. Chronicle isn't inventing a parallel storytelling engine to compete with Bethesda's. It's the first mod to notice that the engine's memoryless casting was always waiting for a memory to drive it.

Underneath all of it sits the machinery v2.2 called the whole dream: NPCs **witness** events and form **beliefs with provenance**. They know things because they saw them or were told, and the system never forgets which. Beliefs travel as **rumors that mutate in transit**. This session's research validated a specific upgrade against every comparative system studied, including the academic system (Talk of the Town) that comes closest: Chronicle adds the one thing no shipped game combines with mutation, **hearing a rumor moves the listener's own disposition toward its subject**, not just their knowledge of it. Beliefs crystallize into **named relationship states** (Friend, Rival, Sworn Friend, the self-directed Guilty) that carry their founding memory and drive visible proximity reactions — this is a new module (`social-mechanics-v3-foundation.md`'s Crystallization), not yet built, ruled in lane 61. The witness/belief/rumor/grudge machinery underneath it, unlike Crystallization, is still true, still built the way v2.2 specified, still regression-tested headless before anything richer sits on it. It's just no longer the headline. It's the reason the headline is honest instead of decorative: a war that "reacts to the player" without a real belief graph underneath it is just a slider with more steps.

## 2. The two north-star tests

**Test A: the world moves on its own agenda (new).** The player spends a week doing something else entirely: enchanting, running errands, ignoring politics. Over that week, Whiterun's civil-war phase advances anyway. Agitation visibly rises (bigger crowds at the shrine of Talos, a shop that stops serving one house's members), a named NPC is arrested in a scene Chronicle casts with a real grievance, and by the time the scripted Battle for Whiterun fires, the town the player fights through is legibly the product of choices, theirs and the war's, made over real time, not a flag flip. When it's over: a memorial stone with the actual dead, a widow's arc instead of a despawn, schedules that take a month to normalize. Pass condition: every element of that week (who got cast, what a bark references, why a shop stopped serving someone) traces back to a real, citable belief or event, and the player can find that citation by asking.

**Test B: the epistemology holds up under pressure (retained from v2.2, unchanged).** The player assassinates Jarl Balgruuf. Succession resolves through the court's actual relationship and faction state, not a scripted replacement. His household mourns on their own calendars; his children hold grudges with the killing as their evidence. News travels at the speed people move, mutating hop by hop. A Stormcloak blacksmith retells it as an Imperial plot; a Markarth merchant three weeks later confidently repeats a version that's wrong, and the sim can show every hop that made it so. Guard cohesion and faction posture shift as aggregates over what individuals actually believe, never as a global flag.

Both tests have to pass headless, with an intact evidence chain behind every assertion, before either layer is considered done. Test A is the new headline; Test B is why Test A is trustworthy rather than theatrical.

## 3. The bets

**Bet 1 (new headline): a belief-driven world-event layer, delivered through Bethesda's own engine, is a genuinely open competitive position.** Every LLM-NPC mod surveyed (SkyrimNet, Mantella, CHIM) does textual gossip and dialogue with no mechanical consequence on schedules, quests, or world state, confirmed again this session, unchanged from v2.2's finding. Separately, no civil-war overhaul mod ties the war's actual progress to distributed political action rather than a fixed questline, except CWORR's narrow reinforcement-coupling mechanic (already surveyed, `docs/research/50`). What's never been combined, anywhere in the comparative research this project has done (six games, five independent brainstorm passes, a dedicated academic-engine investigation), is a live belief graph driving Bethesda's own shipped casting mechanism to make a civil war and a dragon crisis feel like they're actually happening to *this* playthrough's people. The mitigation for the bet: both halves of the mechanism (Radiant Story's alias-fill, CK3's Struggle system) are independently, primary-source-verified real and shipped, not invented from research suggestion. See `docs/research/comparative-systems/radiant-ai-radiant-story-synthesis-2026-09-06.md` and `misc-game-mechanics-gaps-synthesis-2026-09-06.md`.

**Bet 2 (was Bet 1, now infrastructure): subjective belief with provenance is what makes the casting honest.** Unchanged in substance from v2.2: every precedent surveyed still gives up one half (Crusader Kings has consequence without subjectivity, Shadows of Doubt has subjectivity without action, Kenshi collapses the witness to the faction, Nemesis does per-NPC memory but routes it into a patented rank loop Chronicle still avoids). What's new this session: Chronicle's own foundational citation for this bet (Talk of the Town's belief facets, evidence chains, predecessor lineage) was verified directly against James Ryan's actual dissertation and papers rather than trusted secondhand, and holds up as accurate, arguably an understatement (`docs/research/comparative-systems/talk-of-the-town-primary-source-verification-2026-09-06.md`). The repositioning isn't a demotion of rigor. It's an honest statement of what players are actually buying: they're not buying a grudge ledger, they're buying a world whose grudges are the reason its wars and crises make sense.

**Bet 3 (was Bet 2, unchanged): showing the player how a story changed as it traveled.** No shipped game precedent still, per the original research. Still the dashboard's signature, still built first as a debugging instrument so the player-facing bet rides on infrastructure that pays for itself either way.

**A fourth, smaller bet, new this session: the reaction layer between belief and voice is worth building as its own explicit thing.** Every AI-director failure this session's research catalogued is an LLM deciding outcomes; every success is a deterministic core deciding and language rendering after. Chronicle's production-rule reaction layer, a ranked, most-specific-wins table mapping (event, beliefs, sentiment, temperament, mood) to a reaction class, shipped as moddable external data, is the concrete, buildable form of that doctrine, and it's the layer ADR-0011's conversation tier needs underneath it once voice arrives. Not a separate track from the LLM work; the deterministic floor it stands on.

## 4. The observation thesis

Unchanged from v2.2 in substance, extended by one thing: the dashboard is still not tooling for the mod, it's the other half of the product, and everything about legible observation, the derivation trace, and the four-way gap (ABM inspection, Smallville persistence, game legibility, replay-system time-travel; nothing surveyed combines all four) still holds. What's added: the world-event pacing director (§6) produces its own watchable timeline, a hold's phase track, a dragon-crisis escalation ladder, a queue of telegraphed-but-not-yet-fired events, which is exactly the kind of thing a second-screen or alt-tab dashboard view exists to make legible. The Jarl's-death north star was always "watch the news crawl across the map." Test A's north star adds: watch the *war* crawl across the map, weeks before it becomes a battle.

## 5. The constitution

Same three documents as v2.2 (`design-doctrines.md`, `ui-doctrines.md`, the ADRs), same load-bearing doctrines (expected randomness, every derived state answers "why," the honest-mechanic/named-fake pair). Two new doctrines this session's research earned the right to be load-bearing:

- **Radiant Story is the delivery mechanism, not a competitor.** Chronicle does not build a parallel quest-authoring system. The belief engine decides *who* and *where*; `SendStoryEvent`, `ForceRefTo`/`ForceRefIfEmpty`, and `OnStory*` observation events are the entire integration surface for getting that decision into the game, confirmed real and sufficient by three independent research passes. Anything that reaches for a bigger hammer than this needs a specific, named reason.
- **Group-level flags never cascade through individuals automatically.** Named directly after Dwarf Fortress's documented "loyalty cascade" (a forced execution recursively making the executioners traitors to their own civilization, self-terminating in civil war). A phase transition changes what's legal/common/possible in a hold; it never directly rewrites an individual NPC's faction membership. The same doctrine that already excludes Nemesis-style automatic rank-rewriting extends here: succession and allegiance change through story-cast decisions, never through a propagating flag.
- Carried forward, now stated in these exact terms because this session's research gave them sharper names: **never trust a launderable director input** (RimWorld's wealth-gaming exploit: use deaths and completed events, never raw player-visible resources) and **CK2's "opinion-modifier soup"** (uncapped additive stacking becomes unreadable, every scalar Chronicle ships needs a cap and an itemized cause from day one, not precision-tuned later).

## 6. The shape of the road

**Build order** is still the scenario ladder for the belief/rumor/grudge tiers underneath everything (unchanged from v2.2's own ordering: claims-layer mechanics, one-hop transmission plus the derivation trace, multi-hop propagation with mutation, social accumulation and the tell-decision gate, schedule writeback, roles and vacancy, mobile carriers, the Jarl composition test). What's different: **the GM/director layer is no longer held off the version road waiting for that ladder to finish producing state to sift.** It has a verified integration path, a concrete template for its hardest sub-problem (the civil war), and a fully worked-through design tour across all fifteen candidate systems (`docs/research/comparative-systems/worked-design-tour-2026-09-06.md`). The rough sequencing this session's research converged on:

1. **Pure-Python, zero-engine-risk items, buildable in parallel with the ladder**: gossip mutation + transitive writeback (kin-priority belief routing folds into this same lane, per lane 61 §7 — not a separate parallel system), the production-rule reaction layer, Secrets/Hooks leverage, the Dread axis, the world-event pacing director, the STC conversational ladder. None of these touch SKSE.
2. **Radiant Story integration: the architectural linchpin, done early.** Vanilla Papyrus, low engineering risk, and the headline mechanic depends on it. Should not wait behind the civil-war design pass.
3. **Civil-war-as-Struggle-system design pass**, adapting CK3's phase/catalyst model to Skyrim's actual questline, proceeds in parallel with #2, doesn't block it.
4. **Named technical spikes before committing full design budget**: the visible hold/settlement world-state swap (safe respawn-table override, actor/cell-swap-across-saves), persistent dynamic-text object injection (physical/cultural provenance objects, specifically safe bard-performance injection).
5. **Academic-engines follow-up reads, not blocking anything**: `ianhorswill/BotL` (City of Gangsters) and the Praxish/RePraxis repos, before committing engineering budget to any group-level deontic "Social Practices" layer.
6. **The unified stress/mental-break subsystem and the witness-gated crime fix**, both ready, both non-trivial, reasonable second-wave candidates once #2 and the reaction layer prove the pattern.

**Version road**: v0.1/v0.2 as before (headless ladder, then the Skyrim seam). What v2.2 called a GM/director layer with "no scheduled tier, no design started" is now **scheduled as v0.3's actual content**, ahead of the economy tier. The research base is no longer ahead-of-need, it's ready-to-plan. The LLM layers (conversation tier, procedural voice) still come strictly last and strictly as renderers: the production-rule reaction layer and the STC ladder are the deterministic floor ADR-0011 stands on when its time comes, not a competing pillar to sequence against.

**Phases, stated explicitly (2026-09-07 addendum, same session, not a version bump).** The version road above maps onto three phases, and the line between them is exactly where the LLM enters:

- **Phase 1: no LLM anywhere.** Everything above: the belief/rumor/grudge/obligation/role ladder (already shipped, Tiers 0-6), and the entire new world-event layer this document adds (storylet role-casting on Radiant Story, the civil-war/dragon-crisis phased struggles, Crystallization, the production-rule reaction layer, Dread, Secrets/Hooks leverage, the pacing director, the STC conversational ladder, kin-priority routing). All of it is deterministic, headless-testable, and moddable as external data. A player could run the entire headline pitch (§1's dream) with zero language models installed. This is the actual v0.1-v0.3 scope above; nothing in it waits on Phase 2.
- **Phase 2: the simulation gets a voice, scoped narrowly.** Two things only: NPC dialogue rendering (a local LLM speaks an NPC's already-computed belief state out loud, it never decides anything, per this document's Bet 4) and the player's own side of the conversation (ADR-0011: an authored voice card compiled from a trait profile, an intent taxonomy, 3-5 candidate lines generated in the player's character's voice per turn, committed lines re-entering the sim as ordinary claims). Deliberately **not** in Phase 2: any LLM authoring or deciding story content. The STC ladder and the reaction layer (both Phase 1) are what keep this scoped and safe. They're the deterministic floor this phase renders on top of, never a second decision-maker.
- **Phase 3+: the LLM storyteller/GM, and the harness that makes it safe.** An LLM-authored director layered on top of Phase 1's world-event machinery, generating storylet content and scene framing beyond template interpolation, not just casting real NPCs into pre-authored shapes, plus the harder infrastructure problem: a **hierarchy of GM agents operating at different timescales** (a slow campaign-architect tier holding the loose evolving premise, a mid tier advancing hold-by-hold state, a fast per-scene renderer), per the multi-timescale-architecture research (`docs/research/49-56`: HAMLET, StoryVerse, Dramatron, Story2Game). Building that harness, keeping the tiers synchronized without prose-replanning drift per report 55/56's findings, is the bulk of the engineering work here, not the LLM calls themselves.

**This is deliberately not "Phase 3" singular. It's an open-ended arc, owner-flagged as likely spanning Phase 3 through 5 or 6 once actually built,** not one clean milestone. Candidate shape, explicitly not a committed plan: one phase to get a single timescale working end to end (probably the mid, hold-advancing tier first, since Phase 1 already hands it real state to advance), a following phase adding the slow campaign-architect tier once that's proven, another for the fast per-scene renderer, and likely a phase on its own purely for keeping the tiers synchronized without drift. Each phase gets scoped for real only once the phase before it has shipped and shown what the next one actually needs. Same "ready-to-plan, not ahead-of-need" discipline this document applies everywhere else, applied to itself.

Phase 2 was kept narrow on purpose, rather than bundling any of this in with it: shipping NPC voice and player dialogue first gives the eventual multi-timescale GM harness a fully-reactive, fully-tested world to sit on top of, instead of standing it up against a moving foundation.

**Non-goals, carried forward and one added:**
- Everything v2.2 already excluded: no economy simulation before its tier, no LLM before the symbolic tiers are green, no in-engine code before the headless suite passes, no nemesis-style rank-rewriting chains, no global reputation ever, no belief without an evidence chain ever, no behavior threshold without hysteresis and a reason ever. Not a chatbot mod. Mouths still come last.
- **New**: no group-level flag write ever bypasses story-cast NPC decisions to propagate automatically (the loyalty-cascade doctrine, §5). This is now a first-class non-goal, not an implicit consequence of the Nemesis exclusion.

## 7. What success looks like

Near: both north-star tests pass headless, and a stranger can open the dashboard, watch a hold's agitation phase rise over a scrubbed week of ticks, click the arrest scene that pushed it toward Crackdown, and walk the belief chain back to the specific rumor that motivated it, without anyone explaining the tool to them.

Far: a player three weeks into a modded playthrough realizes the war they're fighting is one they watched build, hold by hold, from choices that felt small at the time (an informant protected here, a recruiter ignored there), and that the battle they just won has a memorial with names they recognize, in a town whose grief they watched arrive on schedule. That moment, *the war has a biography, and you were already in it before the battle started*, is the product. Everything the belief/rumor/grudge engine does exists to make that moment true instead of staged.
