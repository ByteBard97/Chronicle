# Chronicle Mod Feasibility: Overhaul NPC Relocation Conflicts and forcePersist Save-Bloat Risk

## TL;DR
- **Spawning persistent objects at the live runtime position of the 19 named Whiterun NPCs is workable, but three of them (Nazeem, Lillith Maiden-Loom, and Brenuin) are physically relocated to entirely different places by two nearly ubiquitous mods (Cutting Room Floor and AI Overhaul), so "evidence" placed at their live position will land at those new spots — and several others (Anoriath, Nazeem, Saffir) roam far outside Whiterun on new AI packages.** City *layout* overhauls like JK's Skyrim and Populated Cities are largely safe because they do not edit vanilla NPCs, but they can leave a spawned object clipping into new geometry.
- **Yes, forcePersist / 0xFF created-reference save bloat is a real, documented, player-visible problem.** Bethesda's own Cell Reset wiki confirms `PlaceAtMe` refs are never cleaned by normal cell resets, the UESP/CK wiki explicitly warns overuse "may result in savegame bloating," and Skyrim's persistent references are "baked in" for the life of the save unless you explicitly `Disable()` + `Delete()` them.
- **The established best practice is unambiguous: create the reference, and when done clean it up with `Disable()` then `Delete()` (or `MarkForDelete`); avoid `forcePersist=true` unless the object must survive cell unloads; and store as few permanent script references to spawned objects as possible.** For a mod that intentionally accumulates permanent world "evidence," you must budget the reference count deliberately and provide a cleanup/uninstall path, or you will grow the save over a long playthrough.

## Key Findings

### (1) NPC relocation conflicts

The critical distinction is between mods that edit **the NPC** (their home, bed, sandbox, or "go home" package — which changes *where the NPC actually is*) versus mods that only edit **the city geometry** (which can leave a spawned object clipping but do not move the NPC).

**Mods that physically relocate NPCs on the list:**

- **Cutting Room Floor (CRF)** — one of the most widely installed mods in Skyrim (CRF - SSE, Nexus mod 276 by Arthmoor, holds 106,851 endorsements as of version 3.1.23a, updated 02 December 2025). It relocates **Nazeem** to a restored home ("Wintersand Manor — Nazeem & Ahlam's house, restored to the game at Chillfurrow Farm"), which is *outside* Whiterun's walls. It also gives **Lillith Maiden-Loom** a restored home ("Maiden-Loom Manor — Lilith Maiden-Loom's house, restored to the game across from Whiterun Stables"). With CRF installed, an object spawned at Nazeem's live position at night/off-hours will appear at Chillfurrow Farm, not in Whiterun's Cloud District; for Lillith it will appear across from the stables.
- **AI Overhaul (LE mod 50978 by SpiderAkiraC → SSE port mod 21654 by mnikjom)** — gives **Brenuin** (a homeless beggar with no vanilla bed) a new sleep package: "Brenuin finally sleeps (by Whiterun gate)." An object spawned at his live position while he sleeps will appear at the gate area rather than wherever vanilla left him.

**Mods that change NPC daytime position via new AI packages (no new home, but the live position moves):**

- **AI Overhaul SSE** sends **Nazeem** to Dragonsreach/the Cloud District on weekdays, gives **Anoriath** a hunting package (he roams the wilds outside Whiterun to hunt), and adds travel/idle behaviors for **Saffir** (goes to the temple; follows Amren), **Carlotta**, **Lucia**, **Ysolda**, **Lars Battle-Born**, **Danica**, and **Olfina**. These are schedule/package edits, not new homes, but they still change where the NPC physically is at a given time, so a "live position" spawn can land far from vanilla expectations.
- **Immersive Citizens - AI Overhaul** overhauls the AI of many Whiterun citizens (Ysolda, Carlotta, Olava, Idolaf, Fralia, Nazeem, Lillith, Adrianne, and others) but its changelogs do not assign any of the 19 a new named home; effects are schedule/behavior. Its "Go Home" feature can move homeless/mod-added NPCs to shelters, but none of the 19 is specifically named.

**Mods that do NOT edit vanilla NPCs (geometry only — lower risk, but clipping possible):**

- **JK's Skyrim / JK's Whiterun**: The author states plainly, "JK's Skyrim doesn't edit ANY vanilla NPC's, doors or interior cells. It simply adds exterior objects." So it will not move the 19 NPCs. However, it heavily rearranges Whiterun's exterior clutter, market stalls, walkways, and adds structures — meaning an object spawned at an NPC's live position could clip into or land inside new JK geometry. JK also relies on L-Navcut collision markers and makes minimal navmesh edits.
- **Populated Cities Towns Villages (PCTV)**: Adds 28 *new generic* NPCs to Whiterun with their own AI packages; "this mod doesn't affect vanilla Skyrim" and uses no scripts. It does not touch the 19 named NPCs.
- **Alternate Start - Live Another Life (LAL)**: Does not relocate the 19. It changes only the game's opening; using the default Helgen sequence "will play out the same as the vanilla game," and it edits the Abandoned Prison cell and start quests. The one relevant interaction: because the vanilla intro can be skipped, the timing of some Whiterun scenes/quests can differ, so an NPC may not yet be at their "post-intro" position early on.

**Immersive Citizens is fundamentally incompatible with town overhauls that move static objects**, per its own author: "A town overhaul is incompatible because... the plugin moves static objects such as houses, bridges, market stalls, archery targets, counters, tables, or walls. Therefore, the placement of some (or all) of my invisible markers... [is broken]." This matters for Chronicle indirectly: idle markers and NPC destinations shift between IC and JK, so an NPC's live position is genuinely load-order-dependent.

**Net assessment for Chronicle:** Because it reads the *live* position at runtime (not a hardcoded coordinate), Chronicle is inherently more robust than a mod that hardcodes placements — it will follow the NPC wherever the overhaul put them. The real failure modes are: (a) the object spawns *outside* Whiterun (Nazeem at Chillfurrow, Brenuin at the gate, Anoriath in the wilds) if the spawn fires while the NPC is at a relocated/roaming spot, which may not be the "Whiterun evidence" the design intends; (b) the object clips into new geometry from JK/Populated/Dawn of Skyrim; and (c) if the NPC is dead/removed (some are killable), `PlaceObjectAtMe` on that reference will fail or place at the last known location.

### (2) Is forcePersist / 0xFF created-reference save bloat a real, documented problem?

Yes, and it is well documented across primary modding sources.

- **Temporary vs. persistent references**: Per the AFK Mods "Levels of Persistence" knowledge base (authored by Arthmoor), a reference "was either temporary, which means it was only loaded if it was within the grid surrounding the player, or it was persistent, which meant it was always loaded in memory at all times." The "Persistent Reference and Object Mover" resource adds that persistent refs are "baked in — Once they're in, they're baked in. This is because they're referenced by something specific — an NPC's package AI, a Quest, a Script, SOMETHING." Setting `abForcePersist=true` forces exactly this baked-in state.
- **0xFF-prefixed created references**: Per DynDOLOD's documentation, "The game uses the load order form ID 0xFF for save game and temporary data." Every object created at runtime (via `PlaceAtMe`, spawns, dropped items, spell effects) receives a 0xFF-prefixed FormID that lives *in the save file*, not in any plugin. There is a hard ceiling: per aers' widely-reposted PSA (mirrored on Step Mods by administrator DoubleYou, Aug 27 2023), "There's a cap of 2²⁰, or 1048576, active reference handles at any time. If you hit the cap, the game will either get stuck loading or just outright CTD." Steam moderator smr1957 corroborates the practical effect (Feb 12 2019): "the number of references being used by the game is getting close to the limit (which is just over 1 million)... if that number reaches or exceeds the limit, the game will just shut down."
- **PlaceAtMe are NOT auto-cleaned**: This is the key technical point for Chronicle. Bethesda's own Cell Reset wiki lists "Created refs (i.e. refs placed via PlaceAtMe)" among objects that "intentionally do not reset/respawn." A Bethesda CK-beta developer (relayed on the gamesas.com forum) stated "any placeatme object is automatically treated as persistent. It doesn't trigger OnReset events and similar, and isn't cleaned up." So even *without* `forcePersist=true`, a `PlaceAtMe` object survives cell resets; `forcePersist=true` additionally keeps it loaded in memory globally.
- **The CK/UESP wiki explicitly warns**: The `PlaceAtMe` page states, "Objects no longer used should be disabled (through DisableNoWait or a similar function) and marked for deletion (through Delete) to prevent them from bloating the user's savegame." The parallel console-command warning (quoted in the gamesas.com "PlaceAtMe & savegame bloat" thread) reads verbatim: "WARNING: Overuse of the placeatme command may result in savegame bloating, which can lead to savegame corruption."
- **Player-visible symptoms**: Steam and Nexus threads document large, slow saves. Steam user NoctVirtuousIX (Aug 1 2018): "My Save File is outright 130MB.. wth lmao"; Steam user Thylacoleo (Jul 31 2015): "my save file is bloated to 305mb, I only have two saved characters in game and run less than 90 mods," accompanied by multi-minute save times, NPCs vanishing, and eventually unloadable saves. On the Nexus persistence thread, one modder documented "over 13000 unloaded persistant refs" causing unloadable saves. FallrimTools/ReSaver (the standard cleaner) is explicitly a "bandage" — its author warns, "Resaver will bandage the wound, but your game may still bleed to death anyway. If you can avoid being in that situation in the first place, you should." ReSaver's main function is "Remove Unattached Instances" (orphaned script instances from removed mods), and it can filter created forms by "initial/base_object," but cleaning persistent created references by hand is impractical at scale.

**Important nuance for Chronicle's design**: 19 objects is trivially small — even placing one per NPC is negligible against a ~1,048,576-handle cap. The bloat risk becomes real only if Chronicle spawns *repeatedly over time* (e.g., new evidence on a timer or per-event) and never deletes the old ones. A one-time set of 19 persistent objects is safe; an accumulating chronicle of hundreds or thousands over a long playthrough is exactly the documented failure mode.

### (3) Best practices and models for spawning world objects without bloat

- **Clean up with Disable + Delete / MarkForDelete**: The CK wiki is explicit — disable then delete unused created references. Per the MarkForDelete documentation, "References created during gameplay, such as those dropped by an actor or something created with the PlaceAtMe function, are truly deleted. They no longer exist in any way" — i.e., runtime-created refs *can* be fully removed from the save, unlike editor-placed refs which can only be disabled. Note: disabling alone is not enough — per the gamesas.com console thread, "You need to markfordelete it as well, just disabling the object doesn't help."
- **Prefer non-persistent creation when possible**: Only pass `forcePersist=true` if the object genuinely must persist across cell unloads and be referenced globally. If Chronicle's evidence only needs to exist while the player is in the cell, a non-persistent ref plus a self-cleanup script is lighter. But note the caveat above: even non-persistent `PlaceAtMe` refs are not auto-cleaned by cell reset, so explicit deletion is still required for true safety.
- **Minimize stored script references**: Per markdf (FallrimTools/ReSaver author), "As long as any papyrus variable points to a form, the system won't automatically delete the changeform." If Chronicle holds every spawned object in a persistent array/property, none will ever be garbage-collected. Use a bounded collection (e.g., a `RefCollectionAlias`) and release references when evidence is retired.
- **Use lightweight base objects**: Community scripters note markers and simple statics carry fewer subrecords than actors or complex objects; "if you must use a specific reference... try using whatever has the least amount of subrecords."
- **Model mods**: Chesko's **Frostfall/Campfire** is the canonical example of a mod that spawns many world objects (tents, campfire rings, placed gear) cleanly — its CampUtil API explicitly exposes whether "this placeable object does not spawn persistently (i.e. won't be cleaned up by a cell reset)," showing the author deliberately manages persistence per-object. **Interesting NPCs - Persistent Objects Restoration** demonstrates careful handling of persistent vs. temporary objects to avoid clipping and bloat.

## Details

**Load-order reality:** The three highest-impact mods for Chronicle are CRF, AI Overhaul (or Immersive Citizens), and a city overhaul (JK's/Dawn of Skyrim/Populated). CRF is nearly universal in modern load orders and it is the one that moves Nazeem *out of the walled city entirely*. If Chronicle's design intent is "evidence appears somewhere in Whiterun," the Nazeem/Chillfurrow, Brenuin/gate, and Anoriath/wilderness cases will violate that intent depending on the in-game time when the spawn fires.

**Dead/removed NPCs:** Several of the 19 can die (Amren, Carlotta, Anoriath, Nazeem, etc.). `PlaceObjectAtMe` called on a cleaned-up corpse reference will fail or misplace the object. Chronicle should null-check the actor and its 3D-loaded state before spawning.

**forcePersist consequences specifically:** With `forcePersist=true`, each evidence object is (a) permanently written to the save's 0xFF change-form table, (b) kept loaded in memory regardless of player location, and (c) never removed by cell reset. This is fine for a small fixed set but compounds if evidence is generated repeatedly.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — If the design is a fixed, one-time set of ~19 evidence objects:** Ship as-is with `forcePersist=true`. The reference count is negligible against the ~1,048,576 active-handle cap. Add: (a) an actor null/3D-loaded check before each `PlaceObjectAtMe`; (b) an MCM or console "cleanup" function that iterates the stored refs and calls `Disable()` then `Delete()` on uninstall. Benchmark: if total spawned refs stay under a few hundred, save-bloat is a non-issue.

**Stage 2 — If evidence accumulates over time:** Do NOT leave every object persistent forever. Implement a bounded ring buffer (e.g., cap at N most-recent evidence objects), and `Disable()`+`Delete()` the oldest when the cap is exceeded. Store refs in a `RefCollectionAlias`, not individual persistent properties. Threshold to change approach: if playtesting a 100+ hour save shows the Chronicle-attributable created-ref count climbing past ~1,000, switch to aggressive cleanup or non-persistent spawns.

**Stage 3 — Handle overhaul relocations explicitly:** For Nazeem, Lillith, Brenuin (and roamers Anoriath/Saffir), decide whether "evidence" should track the NPC anywhere (current behavior) or be constrained to Whiterun. If the latter, gate the spawn on the NPC's current cell/worldspace (only spawn if `GetCurrentLocation`/parent cell is within Whiterun) and defer otherwise. This neutralizes the CRF/AI Overhaul relocation problem entirely.

**Stage 4 — Geometry safety:** Because JK's/Dawn of Skyrim/Populated rearrange Whiterun clutter, spawn slightly above ground and let havok settle, or use the CK-documented physics workaround: objects created via `PlaceAtMe` "are not hooked up to physics events... until the cell reloads if the object's base form does not already exist in the cell on cell load. This can be worked around by calling Disable and then Enable on the object." (Classic Skyrim's `PlaceAtMe` lacks the `abSnapToNavmesh` argument that FO4/Starfield added, so use a small Z-offset plus the Disable/Enable trick.) Test with JK's Whiterun active.

**Always ship:** A documented uninstall/cleanup path, and a note in the mod description that removing Chronicle mid-save leaves its persistent refs unless the cleanup function is run first (mirroring standard modding guidance that persistent data is otherwise baked into the save).

## Caveats
- The "Brenuin sleeps by Whiterun gate" line is documented on the LE AI Overhaul page (mod 50978, SpiderAkiraC); the SSE port's (mod 21654, mnikjom) own changelog only says "Fixed Brenuins dialogue while wandering" and generally "beggars now sleep on the street, or in a tavern basement." The behavior carries over via the port but is not restated verbatim on the SSE page.
- Immersive Citizens' changelogs do not name a new home for any of the 19; its relocation risk is limited to schedule/idle-marker shifts and its "Go Home" shelter logic for homeless/mod NPCs.
- The reference-handle cap is 2²⁰ = 1,048,576 active handles (per aers' PSA), not the total 2²⁴ FormID address space; these figures are community-documented rather than officially published by Bethesda, but are widely corroborated.
- Whether an even smaller number of persistent refs causes user-visible bloat depends heavily on the rest of the load order; poorly-scripted mods and orphaned instances are the more common culprits per FallrimTools' author.
- I did not locate a mod page that specifically documents `PlaceObjectAtMe forcePersist` bloat by name for a Whiterun-evidence-style mod; the guidance here is synthesized from the CK/UESP wiki, Bethesda's Cell Reset wiki, AFK Mods, DynDOLOD, and FallrimTools documentation, which collectively and directly address the mechanism.
