# Chronicle Mod-Conflict Research — Prompts 3 & 4

**Scope:** This report covers research prompts 3 and 4 from the Chronicle mod-conflict brief: (3) runtime writes to the vanilla NPC **relationship-rank** field for 19 named Whiterun NPCs versus dialogue, marriage, and follower mods; and (4) spawning persistent physical "evidence" objects at those NPCs' live positions via `PlaceObjectAtMe(..., forcePersist=true)` versus city/settlement overhauls and long-playthrough save bloat. All findings are drawn from citable mod pages, wikis, forum threads, and tool documentation, with the conflict classification (true race condition vs. one-directional dependency) made explicit for each system.

---

## Executive Summary

**Prompt 3 — relationship-rank writes.** The relationship-rank field turns out to be a surprisingly *quiet* battlefield. Most of the popular systems that "use" relationships — Relationship Dialogue Overhaul (RDO), Immersive Speechcraft, Nether's Follower Framework's Regard system, and Amorous Adventures — either **only read** the vanilla rank or maintain their **own parallel disposition value** and never touch the vanilla field. The genuinely dangerous interactions are: **(a)** vanilla favor/quest rewards themselves, which perform one-off rank writes for several of the 19 NPCs (Ysolda, Amren, Carlotta, Danica, Lars, Braith) that a continuously re-simulating Chronicle would silently stomp on every sync tick; **(b)** the vanilla marriage system, since Ysolda is a marriage candidate and rank 4 (Lover) is load-bearing for spouse behavior; and **(c)** one true periodic writer found — the "Follower Dismissal – Immersive Relationship System" mod, which syncs its custom rank *into* the vanilla rank **every 24 in-game hours** for registered followers, creating the only documented flapping-risk pairing if a Chronicle-tracked NPC is ever recruited as a follower.

**Prompt 4 — persistent object spawning.** Spawning evidence at an NPC's *live* position is structurally robust against **schedule/location reassignment** (Olfina moving into Dragonsreach, Ysolda taking over the Bannered Mare, city overhauls reshuffling routines), because the object always lands where the NPC actually is. The real exposure is twofold: **(a)** Immersive Citizens — which fully overhauls all of Whiterun's NPC AI — routinely moves citizens far outside their "normal" context (fleeing to random hideouts, sheltering indoors during rain, rambling into the wilderness), so evidence will spawn in narratively incoherent places even though the coordinates are technically correct; and **(b)** `forcePersist=true` created references are a **documented save-bloat vector**: persistent references are never deleted by the engine, are exempt from cell-reset cleanup, and every one is baked into the save permanently — the same accumulation pattern the Unofficial Patch historically fixed for dropped weapons, and the same category of cruft FallrimTools/ReSaver exists to excise. Both are avoidable with bounded spawn counts and a lifecycle policy.

## 1. Prompt 3 — Relationship-Rank Writes vs. Dialogue, Marriage, and Follower Mods

### 1.1 The vanilla baseline: the field is already written by quests, favors, and marriage

Before examining mods, it is essential to understand that the vanilla game itself is the most prolific *writer* of the field Chronicle plans to own. Relationship rank in Skyrim ranges from **-4 (Archnemesis) to +4 (Lover)**, defaults to 0 (Acquaintance), and is read by dialogue conditions, by the theft/trespass tolerance system (rank gates the value of items you may take for free: 25 gold at Friend, 100 at Ally, 500 at Lover), and by inheritance/corpse-handling systems ([UESP: Disposition](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Disposition)). Critically, UESP documents that **quest and favor rewards set the rank absolutely** — the wiki's notation "=1" means "the rank is set to friend, no matter what it was before," and "=-1" means "the rank is set to rival, no matter what it was before" ([UESP: Disposition](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Disposition)). These are unconditional overwrites, exactly the write semantics ChronicleBridge implements.

This matters concretely because several of Chronicle's 19 target NPCs carry rank-writing favors in the unmodified game. Ysolda's radiant favor "Rare Gifts" (fetch a mammoth tusk) is a standard disposition-raising favor and doubles as her marriage-candidacy gate ([UESP: Rare Gifts](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Rare_Gifts), [UESP: Ysolda](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Ysolda)). Amren's "Dungeon Delving" favor (recover his family sword) is another rank-writing favor, and its completion is entangled with his wife Saffir's scripted arguments — also one of the 19 ([UESP: Dungeon Delving (Bandits)](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Dungeon_Delving_(Bandits)), [UESP: Amren](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Amren)). Carlotta Valentia's favor (persuade Mikael to leave her alone) raises her disposition ([UESP: Carlotta Valentia](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Carlotta_Valentia)). Danica Pure-Spring's "The Blessings of Nature" is a full quest with favor semantics ([UESP/IGN: The Blessings of Nature](https://www.ign.com/wikis/the-elder-scrolls-5-skyrim/The_Blessings_of_Nature)). The "Bullying Braith" mini-quest is the clearest documented case: after completion, "Braith becomes friendly to you… you can freely enter and take low-value items from the Battle-Born house" — the observable signature of a rank write to Friend for both Braith and Lars Battle-Born ([UESP: Bullying Braith](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Bullying_Braith)).

Ysolda additionally carries a scripted rank *decrease*: if Mikael is killed, "Ysolda's disposition toward the Dragonborn will decrease" ([Elder Scrolls Fandom: Ysolda](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Ysolda)). And Ysolda is one of the game's standard marriage candidates, meaning the Bonds of Matrimony quest will write rank 4 (Lover) to her ([UESP: Ysolda](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Ysolda)). The practical consequence: Chronicle's simulation layer must decide, per NPC, whether it *respects* quest-written ranks (treating favor completion as a simulation input) or *overrides* them (treating its simulation as the sole source of truth). If it overrides unconditionally, players will experience favors that visibly "did nothing" — the post-quest gift-giving, house access, and friendly greetings disappear on the next sync. If it respects them, the simulation's grudge outcomes can be silently vetoed by a two-septim errand for Lars Battle-Born. Neither is a crash; both are player-visible logic conflicts, and they exist even in a load order containing **zero** other mods.

**Relationship Dialogue Overhaul** is the most popular relationship-aware mod and the one the brief flags first, and the finding is reassuring: RDO's core mechanism is *conditional dialogue selection*. Its 5,000+ added lines are conditioned on the NPC's existing relationship with the player — "99.9% of the new dialogue added by RDO is not restricted to specific NPCs" and works "based on the relationship the NPC has with player" ([RDO SE](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/1187)). The author explicitly states the mod is not script-heavy, that "there's nothing that's actively checking around the player or constantly polling for updates all the time," and that the heaviest script is the MCM itself, which "only runs when you open RDO's MCM" ([RDO SE](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/1187)). In other words, RDO is a pure *consumer* of the rank field: when Chronicle's simulation marks Nazeem an Enemy, RDO is precisely the mod that will surface that state to the player through hostile greetings and refused services ("NPCs that offer services… will not offer those services if the NPC dislikes the player"). This is the opposite of a conflict — Chronicle's writes become *visible* through RDO. The only write path is the optional MCM (restored for SE via the separate "RDO – Update and MCM" patch), which lets the player manually set any unique NPC's rank ([YouTube: RDO MCM demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kVA60XrwrG4), [RDO Update and MCM](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/44601)). A manual player action overwriting a simulated value once is not a race condition; at worst it is a deliberate user override that Chronicle's next sync will revert, which is arguably correct behavior.

**The follower frameworks (EFF, AFT, NFF)** interact with the field at exactly one moment: recruitment. EFF's own documentation states the mechanic plainly — "Followers tie in with favor, and favor is actually your relationship rank" ([EFF](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrim/mods/12933)). Recruiting an NPC through vanilla favor mechanics sets their rank (typically to Ally); the frameworks then manage the follower relationship through their own quests, factions, and aliases rather than through repeated rank writes. AFT can additionally "make an npc a follower" outside the vanilla favor path, which users note "tends to affect some npcs that cheat room doesn't" ([GameFAQs AFT discussion](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/191476-the-elder-scrolls-v-skyrim-special-edition/74996098)) — meaning any of the 19 (Amren and Adrianne are plausible targets) can be pulled into follower management, with a one-time rank adjustment at that boundary. NFF's headline "Regard System" is explicitly a separate construct — "a relationship system that is more of their respect for you as an adventuring partner" — layered on top of rather than inside the vanilla rank ([NFF](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/55653)). For all three, the conflict shape is identical: a single overwrite at recruitment time, after which the systems do not contend for the field. The design implication for Chronicle is that rank is a poor channel for representing simulated grudges *against current followers* — follower status itself asserts a rank floor — but for non-follower townsfolk there is no contention at all.

**The one genuine periodic writer** found in this research is a smaller, newer mod: "Follower Dismissal – Immersive Relationship System." Its description states that a follower's "custom relationship rank… is synced with the vanilla relationship rank every day," that registered followers' values "update every 24 hours," and that the sync writes back into the in-game rank ("npc's can only be followers above 1 relationship rank… their relationshiprank in game will be synced to 1") ([Follower Dismissal](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/147191)). Two systems that each treat the vanilla rank as a write target on independent timers are the textbook precondition for "flapping": Chronicle writes a simulated Rival, the daily sync rewrites the custom value into the vanilla field, Chronicle's next event rewrites it back. The conditionality is important, though: the mod only manages NPCs the player has explicitly registered through its dialogue, which in practice means followers, hirelings, and housecarls. None of the 19 Whiterun townsfolk is a default follower, so the conflict requires a specific player action (recruiting, say, Amren via AFT's make-follower feature and then registering him). The risk is real but opt-in and narrow, and it argues for Chronicle detecting CurrentFollowerFaction membership and suspending rank writes for enlisted NPCs rather than for any general reconciliation protocol.

**Amorous Adventures** deserves specific mention because four of Chronicle's 19 — Adrianne Avenicci, Carlotta Valentia, Danica Pure-Spring, and Ysolda — are on its romance-target list ([TV Tropes: Amorous Adventures](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/AmorousAdventures)). However, per a community statement on the related disposition-display mod's page, "Amorous Adventures [is] not using skyrim relationship rank, its using its own custom disposition" ([Nexus: Show NPC Disposition, posts](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/102144?tab=posts)). This is consistent with RDO's compatibility note that "Lover dialogue from AA may mix with dialogue from RDO but they do not conflict" ([RDO SE](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/1187)). So there is no field-level race; the exposure is narrative incoherence — Chronicle's simulation can declare Carlotta the player's Archnemesis while AA's questline romances her — which no technical layer can reconcile and which is best handled by a content-level note in Chronicle's documentation.

**Marriage systems** (vanilla plus "Marriage Mod – To Have And To Hold," which enables remarriage and multiple spouses ([Currently/Yahoo: 57 Best Skyrim Mods](https://currently.att.yahoo.com/att/55-best-skyrim-mods-2025-034900547.html))) write rank 4 (Lover) and the spouse faction at the wedding and then leave the field alone — but the value remains *load-bearing* for the spouse's home behavior, store income, Lover's Comfort, and spouse dialogue conditions. Because Ysolda is both a Chronicle target and a vanilla marriage candidate ([UESP: Ysolda](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Ysolda)), Chronicle can silently demote the player's wife to Enemy. Nothing writes the rank back, so there is no flapping — there is simply a broken spouse until the player notices. This is a policy question, not an engineering one: Chronicle should probably treat rank 4 (or spouse-faction membership) as a hard exclusion, the same way Immersive Citizens excludes followers from Survival Instinct.

### 1.3 Race condition or one-directional? The classification

Across everything investigated, **no popular mod continuously re-derives and rewrites vanilla relationship rank for generic townsfolk**. The periodic-writer set contains exactly one entry (Follower Dismissal, daily, followers only). The one-off-writer set is large — vanilla favors, marriage, follower recruitment, RDO's MCM — but one-off writes cannot produce a race condition by themselves; they produce *lost updates*, and the direction of the loss depends entirely on Chronicle's write policy. If Chronicle rewrites rank on every simulation event, it wins every collision and the visible symptom is favors and marriages that appear to "not take." If Chronicle writes only on significant simulated state transitions, quest rewards will stick until the next grudge event, and the visible symptom is simulated enemies who remain oddly friendly after a fetch quest. The dominant design recommendation that falls out of the research is therefore not a compatibility patch — there is nothing to patch, since no ESP records are involved — but a **priority policy**: define which events (favor completion, marriage, follower recruitment) are allowed to override simulated rank, and treat the vanilla field as shared state with explicit conflict rules rather than as Chronicle's private output channel.

The read-only consumers, meanwhile, are pure upside. RDO will voice Chronicle's grudges, Immersive Speechcraft's persuasion odds will respond to them ([Immersive Speechcraft SE](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/21296)), and Relationship Change Notifications will announce every simulated shift as it lands ([Relationship Change Notifications](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/34670)) — useful for Chronicle's own playtesting, if occasionally spammy. The one residual annoyance class is that rank writes are *silent* in vanilla; Chronicle's writes will surface through these mods without attribution, which may generate confused bug reports ("RDO made Nazeem hate me") unless Chronicle ships its own notification or an MCM page showing simulated standings.

## 2. Prompt 4 — Persistent Object Spawning vs. City Overhauls and Save Bloat

### 2.1 What `forcePersist=true` actually does to a save

The spawning API Chronicle uses has well-documented semantics. `PlaceAtMe`/`PlaceObjectAtMe` takes an `abForcePersist` flag ("True to force the created reference to be persistent") and, importantly, a separate `abDeleteWhenAble` flag defaulting to true, which "force[s] the reference to be deleted as soon as it is no longer persisted by a property or quest and is no longer in the loaded area" ([Fallout Wiki / Creation Kit: PlaceAtMe](https://fallout.wiki/wiki/Resource:Creation_Kit/PlaceAtMe_-_ObjectReference)). Chronicle's combination — forcePersist=true — is the maximally durable configuration: the created reference is persistent from birth, which in this engine means it is **never garbage-collected**. The AFK Mods technical thread on save structure states it directly: "persistent references are never actually deleted"; when their cell resets they are physically removed from the world but the engine *relocates the record* back to its persistence cell rather than destroying it, and the entry remains in the save ([AFK Mods: GetParentCell on persistent references](https://www.afkmods.com/index.php?/topic/3717-skyrim-the-papyrus-function-getparentcell-and-what-may-happen-when-it-runs-on-a-persistent-reference/)). UESP's console documentation corroborates the flip side of this: a cell reset deletes "any references created in-game (refID starting with FF), such as items dropped by the Player" — i.e., *non-persistent* created refs are cleaned, persistent ones survive ([UESP: Console, resetinterior](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Console)).

Whether this constitutes "save bloat" as a player-visible issue is well-established community knowledge rather than theory. The canonical precedent is the Unofficial Patch's fix for NPCs dropping weapons and shields as separate objects, "as these are never cleaned up which greatly adds to save bloating," alongside fixes for ash piles and civil-war flag activators "that have been accumulating" ([Arqade: minimizing Skyrim save size, quoting USKP changelog](https://gaming.stackexchange.com/questions/48939/what-can-i-do-to-minimize-my-skyrim-save-game-size)). A Nexus Mods scripting-practices thread advises that "PlaceAtMe and PlaceLeveledActorAtMe should be used sparingly," noting that even temporary created references "remain in the save" once unloaded, that dropped items and lootable-item state are baked into saves, and that reference removal should use `MarkForDelete` rather than `Disable` ([Nexus Forums: practices that cause save bloat](https://forums.nexusmods.com/topic/7459531-what-scriptinggeneral-practices-can-cause-save-game-bloat-and-related-issues/)). And the existence and popularity of FallrimTools/ReSaver — a dedicated savefile editor whose entire purpose is removing unattached script instances and undefined elements from bloated saves, with its own author cautioning that cleaning "will bandage the wound, but your game may still bleed to death" — demonstrates that the community treats this accumulation class as a real, playthrough-ending problem worth building tooling against ([FallrimTools on Nexus](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/5031)).

Two nuances keep this in proportion. First, per-object cost is small: an evidence item is a few hundred bytes of change-form data, and a belief system touching 19 NPCs will generate orders of magnitude fewer objects than, say, an archer's dropped arrows. The bloat concern scales with *spawn frequency over a long playthrough*, not with the feature's existence. Second, the cost is *irreversible in-game*: because persistent refs survive cell resets and are never deleted, there is no natural mechanism by which a Chronicle evidence trail "heals" over time — every rumor that spawns a physical token adds permanent save mass unless Chronicle itself tracks and deletes the references (the engine supports this: non-persistent placement with the default `abDeleteWhenAble=true` self-cleans once unloaded and unpersisted ([Fallout Wiki / Creation Kit: PlaceAtMe](https://fallout.wiki/wiki/Resource:Creation_Kit/PlaceAtMe_-_ObjectReference))). The research therefore supports a concrete design rule rather than vague caution: spawn non-persistent by default, reserve `forcePersist` for objects that must survive cell unload for gameplay reasons, cap live evidence objects per NPC, and keep the created refIDs so Chronicle can `Delete()` stale evidence itself.

### 2.2 Relocation behavior of the named city/settlement mods

The four mods the brief asks about differ enormously in what they do to NPCs, and the differences flip the expected risk ordering.

**Immersive Citizens – AI Overhaul** is by far the most consequential, and its own documentation is the best source. Whiterun is in the mod's "fully overhauled" tier — one of 601 named NPCs whose AI it rebuilds, with Whiterun explicitly listed among the fully covered cities ([Immersive Citizens official site](http://ai4egames.com/skyrim/ic/)). Three features move the 19 NPCs out of their vanilla context routinely: **Survival Instinct** makes attacked NPCs flee to a randomly chosen hideout ("an NPC's home, the local temple, the Jarl's stronghold, a mine, the local barrack") selected per city size; **Take Shelter** sends NPCs indoors during rain or snow into randomly chosen buildings; and the **Rambler** behavior makes some NPCs "stroll outside their city/village," loot ingredients, or "travel to another village/city" ([Immersive Citizens official site](http://ai4egames.com/skyrim/ic/)). Veteran commentary documents Whiterun citizens ending up in unexpected places under this mod — the AFK Mods thread records "NAZEEM GOES TO THE CLOUD DISTRICT!" and NPCs showing up in the Hall of the Dead, and notes IC's use of permanent quest aliases that lock NPCs into high-priority AI at all times ([AFK Mods: Immersive Citizens discussion](https://www.afkmods.com/index.php?/topic/4215-immersive-citizens-ai-overhaul-by-shurah/)). For Chronicle, the conclusion is precise: evidence spawning at the NPC's *live* position will never be in the "wrong place" relative to the NPC, but IC guarantees that the NPC is frequently in a place that makes no narrative sense for evidence — a grudge token materializing next to Nazeem while he hides from a dragon inside the guard barracks, or beside Ysolda mid-ramble on the tundra road. The failure mode is **coherence, not correctness**. A sensible mitigation is to defer spawning while the NPC is in combat/fleeing or outside their home city location, rather than tying spawn position to sim-event timing.

**Populated Cities Towns Villages (PCTV)** is structurally inert for this question. It adds 189 new respawning generic NPCs — 28 of them in Whiterun — each "with own AI package," but its documentation states it "doesn't affect vanilla Skyrim," uses no scripts, and can be placed anywhere in the load order ([PCTV SE](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/2005)). It does not relocate, re-schedule, or replace any of the 19. The only interaction is crowding: more NPCs around the market means more witnesses around a spawned evidence object, which is arguably a feature for a rumor system.

**JK's Whiterun** changes the city's physical layout, not its people. The relevant evidence is indirect but consistent: the JK's Skyrim Patch Collection notes patches that "shift the initial placement of a few NPCs" and adjust navmesh ([JK's Skyrim Patch Collection](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/154077)), and Immersive Citizens' compatibility list treats JK's Whiterun (v1.4+) as *compatible* while listing full-layout overhauls like ETaC and JK's Skyrim AIO as AI-breaking — because they move or delete markers and statics that AI packages reference, producing NPCs who "permanently collide themselves against walls" or stand inanimate ([Immersive Citizens compatibility list](https://ai4egames.com/skyrim/ic-se-support/)). For Chronicle the exposure is limited to edge geometry: an NPC sandboxing against a newly added wall or planter could receive an evidence spawn half-clipped into that static. Since spawn position derives from the NPC's feet on navmesh, this is rare and cosmetic; the sharper version of the same risk appears when players stack *incompatible* layout mods (the IC incompatible list is long — Ultimate Whiterun, 7K Better Whiterun, Whiterun Outskirts Market, Real Cities-Whiterun, Dawn of Skyrim Director's Cut among them ([Immersive Citizens compatibility list](https://ai4egames.com/skyrim/ic-se-support/))) and NPCs get stuck in broken navigation, in which case evidence accumulates wherever the NPC is stuck.

**Alternate Start – Live Another Life** can be dismissed for this subsystem. Its changes are concentrated at game start — the Helgen sequence, the Abandoned Prison cell, MQ101/MQ102 and associated scripts — and its incompatibility list is entirely about start-quest edits, not city populations ([AFK Mods: Live Another Life](https://www.afkmods.com/index.php?/files/file/270-alternate-start-live-another-life/)). The one Whiterun touchpoint, the gate-guard scene (DialogueWhiterunGuardGateStop), is a start-gating mechanism, not an NPC relocation ([AFK Mods: Live Another Life](https://www.afkmods.com/index.php?/files/file/270-alternate-start-live-another-life/)). Nothing in LAL moves any of the 19 NPCs or alters where they live.

One additional, unasked-for finding is worth recording because it dominates the "wrong cell" scenario the brief raises: **vanilla itself relocates two of the 19**. Olfina Gray-Mane moves into Dragonsreach as housecarl if Vignar becomes Jarl after a Stormcloak victory ([Elder Scrolls Fandom: Olfina Gray-Mane](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Olfina_Gray-Mane)), and Ysolda takes over the Bannered Mare (or Anoriath's stall, or Belethor's shop) if the relevant proprietor dies ([Elder Scrolls Fandom: Ysolda](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Ysolda)). Because Chronicle spawns at live position, these moves are handled correctly by construction — this is the strongest argument *for* the live-position design. The stale-coordinate failure the brief hypothesizes ("a cell the NPC no longer regularly occupies") can only occur if Chronicle caches positions, or during the documented engine gaps where positions don't update: Immersive Citizens' FAQ notes the engine cannot update NPC positions during fast travel and that NPCs don't move while the player sleeps or waits ([Immersive Citizens official site](http://ai4egames.com/skyrim/ic/)). If Chronicle's position poller reads coordinates immediately after a fast-travel or wait, it will place evidence where the NPC *was*, and with forcePersist=true that misplaced object is permanent.

### 2.3 Failure-mode synthesis and recommendations

Pulling the two halves together, Prompt 4's risk decomposes into three distinct, independently fixable problems. **Placement coherence** is the IC-driven issue: live-position spawning is always technically correct, so the fix is contextual gating (don't spawn while the target is fleeing, in combat, trespassing in a random hideout, or outside their home location) rather than positional correction. **Placement staleness** is the engine-timing issue: the post-fast-travel and post-sleep windows return stale coordinates from any position read, so Chronicle should delay evidence spawning until the NPC has been observed at a stable position across two poll cycles. **Save mass** is the persistence issue: `forcePersist=true` objects are exempt from every automatic cleanup mechanism the engine has (cell reset deletes only FF-prefixed *non-persistent* created refs ([UESP: Console](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Console)); persistent refs are relocated, never destroyed ([AFK Mods: persistent references](https://www.afkmods.com/index.php?/topic/3717-skyrim-the-papyrus-function-getparentcell-and-what-may-happen-when-it-runs-on-a-persistent-reference/))), so the fix must be lifecycle management inside ChronicleBridge itself — tracked refIDs, caps, and explicit deletion of superseded evidence.

The "do any community guides describe this as a real issue worth avoiding" sub-question resolves to an unqualified yes. The Unofficial Patch treats accumulating created references as save bloat worth patching ([Arqade/USKP changelog](https://gaming.stackexchange.com/questions/48939/what-can-i-do-to-minimize-my-skyrim-save-game-size)); the modding-practices literature explicitly names PlaceAtMe as a use-sparingly API and prescribes MarkForDelete for reference removal ([Nexus Forums: save bloat practices](https://forums.nexusmods.com/topic/7459531-what-scriptinggeneral-practices-can-cause-save-game-bloat-and-related-issues/)); the AFK Mods save-format analysis explains the underlying never-deleted mechanism ([AFK Mods: Information Baked Into Saves](https://www.afkmods.com/index.php?/topic/3676-skyrim-information-baked-into-saves/)); and ReSaver's existence and popularity operationalize the cleanup side ([FallrimTools on Nexus](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/5031)). None of these sources describe a *catastrophe* at Chronicle's likely spawn rates — the horror stories involve hundreds of thousands of accumulated objects — but all of them describe exactly Chronicle's chosen pattern (persistent created references with no cleanup path) as the thing to avoid designing into a long-playthrough mod.

## 3. Consolidated Conclusions

**Prompt 3 verdict:** Chronicle's relationship-rank writes face **no true race condition against any popular dialogue, marriage, or follower mod as installed by default**; the ecosystem reads the field (RDO, Immersive Speechcraft, notification mods) or maintains parallel custom values (Amorous Adventures, NFF Regard) far more than it writes it. The three real exposures are, in order of likelihood: vanilla favor/quest/marriage writes being stomped by Chronicle's sync (affects at least Ysolda, Amren, Saffir's household, Carlotta, Danica, Lars, and Braith — over a third of the target list); spouse-state corruption through Ysolda's marriage candidacy; and a conditional daily-sync race with the Follower Dismissal mod that only materializes if the player recruits and registers one of the 19 as a follower. All three are policy problems solvable in Chronicle's write logic (respect quest-written ranks as simulation inputs; exclude spouses and active followers), not patch problems.

**Prompt 4 verdict:** The spawn-at-live-position design is sound against NPC relocation — vanilla residence changes (Olfina, Ysolda) and overhaul-driven schedule changes are handled correctly by construction. The residual risks are narrative-incoherent placement during Immersive Citizens' flee/shelter/ramble behaviors, stale-coordinate placement in the engine's fast-travel/sleep windows, and permanent save growth from `forcePersist=true` references, which are never deleted by any automatic mechanism and constitute a community-documented bloat vector. Recommended engineering controls: spawn non-persistent with default `abDeleteWhenAble` wherever possible, gate spawning on NPC context (not fleeing, in home location, stable position), track created refIDs for explicit cleanup, and cap per-NPC evidence counts.
