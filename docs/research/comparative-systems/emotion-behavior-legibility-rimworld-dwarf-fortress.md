# Legible Emotion→Behavior Systems: Dwarf Fortress and RimWorld as Models for "Chronicle" (Skyrim SE/AE)

## TL;DR
- **Use a hybrid, but lean RimWorld.** Adopt RimWorld's *threshold-banded, weighted-random-with-gating* model for deciding *which* behavior fires, but borrow Dwarf Fortress's *state-machine takeover* (a distinct "afflicted" unit-state that suppresses normal job-seeking) for the *mechanics* of interrupting and replacing an NPC's current behavior — because Skyrim's AI package override system (`ActorUtil.AddPackageOverride`, priority 0–100) is already a priority-injection state machine.
- **Legibility is the whole ballgame in a co-present 3D game.** Neither DF's pause-and-recenter announcements nor RimWorld's mood-bar/tooltip layer exists in Skyrim. You must externalize cause through *diegetic* channels: pre-break "tells" (idle animations, wandering, staring), NPC self-dialogue/greetings that name the cause, ambient NPC-to-NPC gossip, letters/journal entries, and staged escalation (a minor visible change days before the major behavior change). Bethesda themselves deliberately scaled back Radiant AI precisely so emergent behavior wouldn't read as a bug.
- **The engine can do it, but only as eventual-consistency, not real-time.** Papyrus gets ~1.2 ms/frame of VM budget, a ~0.1 s realistic minimum poll, and NPCs in unloaded cells don't tick. Push state from your headless Python service via an HTTP-loopback SKSE bridge (Mantella v0.12+ model) or a native C++ DLL (SkyrimNet model); apply package/faction changes on cell-attach, batch a few swaps per frame, and budget ~1–3 s of latency for live NPCs.

---

## Key Findings

1. **DF and RimWorld solve two different problems.** DF models a *continuous internal variable* (stress) that occasionally trips into discrete, mostly-irreversible states; RimWorld models a *continuous mood* that trips into *temporary* states chosen by weighted-random selection gated by traits and situation, with a hard anti-spiral "catharsis" buffer. RimWorld's design is explicitly built for legibility and player agency; DF's is built for emergent tragedy.
2. **Both games interrupt current behavior via a state transition, not a job-queue edit.** In DF the unit enters a `mood_type` state (Berserk, Melancholy, etc.) that suppresses normal job-seeking; a strange-mood dwarf *claims a workshop, evicts others, and ignores all other labor until fulfilled or dead*. In RimWorld the pawn enters a `MentalStateDef` and control is removed. Both map cleanly onto Skyrim's package-override model.
3. **RimWorld's selection algorithm is a documented weighted-random over eligible breaks.** `MentalBreaker` picks from `CurrentPossibleMoodBreaks` using `TryRandomElementByWeight(d => d.Worker.CommonalityFor(pawn))`, filtered by `d.Worker.BreakCanOccur(pawn)` and by intensity band. Traits gate eligibility and re-weight; thresholds are 35% / 20% / 5% by default (minor/major/extreme = base, 4/7 base, 1/7 base).
4. **Legibility in the source games is delivered by UI affordances Skyrim lacks.** DF pauses, recenters the camera, and prints colored announcements ("has gone stark raving mad!"); RimWorld shows a mood bar with threshold markers, "About to break" alerts, a per-thought tooltip breakdown, and colored colonist-bar icons. Chronicle must replace all of this with in-world signals.
5. **Existing external-service Skyrim mods (Mantella, SkyrimNet) prove the plumbing works** and already grapple with the legibility problem — they halt the NPC visibly, and inject behavior through a Papyrus/SKSE action layer. Their round-trip latency (~2–6 s in practice) sets a realistic expectation.

## Details

### 1. Dwarf Fortress: stress → behavior mapping

**The variables (post-v0.47 / v50 model).** Stress is tracked internally as two hidden numbers, visible only via DFHack/Dwarf Therapist:
- **Short-term stress:** range −100,000 to +100,000; correlates directly with the visible mood emoji (ecstatic → miserable). Driven by *emotions*, each generated by an experience or a revisited long-term memory.
- **Long-term stress:** range −50,000 to +120,000; a gradual accumulation of short-term stress. Per the Dwarf Fortress Wiki (DF2014:Stress), negative effects begin appearing at the status thresholds **+10,000, +25,000, and +50,000** — surfaced in-game only as the statuses **stressed, haggard, and harrowed**.
- **Emotional breakdowns only occur when *both* short- and long-term stress are high.** Under "ideal" conditions long-term stress rises at most ~20,160/year and falls at most ~43,564/year, so becoming haggard takes years and recovery takes years.

**Personality facets that modulate the mapping** (wiki reverse-engineering; corroborated by DF Steam-forum testing by "SalfordSal"): **bravery** determines how fast a dwarf *gains* stress; **stress vulnerability / capacity** determines how much stress they can cope with before breaking; and **anxiety/calmness** determines how fast stress *dissipates*. Plus **[DEPRESSION_PROPENSITY]** (→ melancholy) and **[ANGER_PROPENSITY]** (→ berserk).

**The outcome tree.** Short-term emotional breakdowns (temporary; cancel current job, then recover):
- **Tantrum** — throws objects, starts fights, destroys furniture (anger-prone dwarves).
- **Depression** — mopes, cancels jobs (depression-prone). Repeats → **melancholy**.
- **Obliviousness** — stumbles around aimlessly (anxiety-prone). Repeats → **stark raving mad**.

Long-term: prolonged stress → **Harrowed**; a harrowed dwarf who then experiences a stressful event (e.g. sees a corpse) tips into permanent **insanity**, which comes in four flavors, all terminal:
- **Melancholy** — wanders slowly, stops eating/drinking, dies of thirst/starvation (high depression propensity).
- **Stark raving mad** — babbles, wanders, ignores needs, starves.
- **Berserk** — attacks friend and foe; becomes an enemy of the civilization, trips traps (high anger propensity).
- **Catatonic** — stares into space until death (extreme anxiety/trauma).

**Strange moods** are a separate, positive-flavored takeover triggered independently of stress: the dwarf enters a `mood_type` (Fey, Secretive, Possessed, Fell, Macabre), **claims a workshop, evicts the current user, refuses all other labor, and hunts for materials**; if it can't obtain them within a couple of months it goes insane and dies. On success it produces an artifact and a huge skill gain.

**Mechanically, how the game injects the new behavior.** DFHack source (`showmood.cpp`, `strangemood.cpp`) confirms the model is a **state-machine transition into a distinct unit state** — `unit.mood` set to a `mood_type` enum (Fey/Secretive/Possessed/Fell/Macabre for moods; Berserk/Melancholy/Insane etc. for breakdowns) — that **suppresses normal job-seeking**, combined with hard cancellation of the current job. The wiki notes "Most events that would ordinarily interrupt a dwarf will not deter a moody dwarf" — the state overrides the job scheduler entirely rather than merely inserting a high-priority job. This is option (c) in the task's taxonomy (a state-machine transition that suppresses job-seeking), with elements of (b) (hard cancellation with a cancellation announcement).

**Announcements as legibility devices.** The game **pauses, centers the camera on the dwarf, and prints a colored announcement** ("has been consumed by melancholy," "has gone stark raving mad!"; a strange mood pauses/centers twice — once on claiming the workshop, once on beginning construction). These are the legibility layer: they attribute the state change to a named individual at a specific moment, converting an invisible variable crossing a threshold into a discrete, attributable narrative beat.

**Resolution / recovery.** Temporary breakdowns end on their own after some time ("regain their composure") *if* stress falls; otherwise they recur and escalate. Insanity is **permanent and terminal** — "there is no cure." Recovery of *stress itself* is via fulfilling needs and generating happy memories: quality bedrooms, a legendary dining room, meaningful (skill-increasing) work, praying at temples, socializing/drinking at taverns, and — the wiki's most reliable trick — **military training** (a "Therapy Squad" scheduled to train with no barracks, which drives idle dwarves to satisfy their own needs). This is a deliberate, slow **decay-toward-baseline** system.

**Tantrum spirals and Tarn Adams's dampening.** The classic failure cascade: a death → unhappy friends → tantrum → destroyed property / more deaths → more unhappy dwarves → berserk/melancholy → suicide contaminating the water → more deaths. The pre-v0.47 "unhappiness" model produced runaway spirals easily. Tarn Adams rewrote the whole system into the current *emotion + long-term-memory + personality-facet* model (v0.44 era, shipped in the v50 Steam release) specifically to make breakdowns rarer, more individualized, and slower — the dual short/long-term stress requirement, the years-long accumulation, and needs-based recovery all act as spiral dampeners.

### 2. RimWorld: mental break selection algorithm

**Thresholds (RimWorld Wiki + decompiled `MentalBreaker.cs`).** Mental Break Threshold is a stat, default **35%** (min 1%, max 50%). Per the RimWorld Wiki (Mood), the mood-bar risk lines are placed "at 5% for an extreme break risk, 20% for a major break risk, and 35% for a minor break risk, although these thresholds can be changed by a colonist's traits." Derived bands:
- **Minor break:** mood below 35% → mean time-to-break ~10 days; mood label "**Stressed**."
- **Major break:** below **4/7 × threshold = 20%** → MTB ~3 days; label "**On edge**."
- **Extreme break:** below **1/7 × threshold ≈ 5%** → MTB ~0.7 days; label "**About to break**."

**Threshold modifiers (traits):** Iron-Willed and Steadfast raise resilience (effectively lower threshold); Nervous/Volatile lower it; Sanguine, Too Smart, Psychically Sensitive, Depressive, and mood-flat traits shift it. Multiple traits are additive, capped at the 1%–50% minor threshold.

**The selection algorithm — hybrid weighted-random gated by eligibility.** From the decompiled `Verse.AI.MentalBreaker`:
- The VM checks on `pawn.IsHashIntervalTick(150)` (every 150 ticks) whether the pawn `CanDoRandomMentalBreaks`, is not `Downed`, is `Awake`, and not already `InMentalState`.
- It tracks `ticksBelowMinor`, `ticksBelowMajor`, `ticksBelowExtreme` and `ticksUntilCanDoMentalBreak`.
- It builds `CurrentPossibleMoodBreaks` = all `MentalBreakDef`s `where d.intensity == intensity && d.Worker.BreakCanOccur(pawn)`.
- It selects: `CurrentPossibleMoodBreaks.TryRandomElementByWeight(d => d.Worker.CommonalityFor(pawn))`.
- Non-player factions can only do Extreme breaks (`if pawn.Faction != Faction.OfPlayer && CurrentDesiredMoodBreakIntensity != Extreme` → suppressed).

So: **the intensity band is deterministic (set by how far below threshold), but which specific break fires within that band is a weighted random draw** over `baseCommonality` (via `CommonalityFor`), *after* filtering by `BreakCanOccur`. Traits, situation, and capability gate and re-weight:
- **Pyromaniac** → Fire starting spree is always eligible even at good mood.
- **Bloodlust / Psychopath / violent traits** bias toward violent/macabre breaks.
- Situation gating: per the RimWorld Wiki, Murderous Rage "is ten times less likely to occur in a colony with a population of two, and it increases linearly to the normal level at a population of seven or more"; Jailbreaker requires a reachable prisoner; ideology-based breaks (Crisis of belief) require the Ideology DLC and eligible ideoligions; bonded-animal and caravan states gate others.

**Break catalogue by intensity** (illustrative): Minor — Sad wander, Food binge, Hide in room, Insulting spree, Crying, Giggling. Major — Tantrum, Bedroom tantrum, Psychotic (daze) wandering, Sadistic rage, Corpse obsession, Insane ramblings. Extreme — Berserk, Fire starting spree, Murderous rage, Slaughterer, Jailbreaker, Catatonic breakdown, Run wild, Given up and leaving.

**Telegraphing / legibility (deliberate design).** RimWorld gives the player substantial lead time and attribution:
- The **mood bar** on the Needs tab with **threshold markers** at 5% / 20% / 35%.
- **Alerts** ("Colonist mood: Stressed / On edge / About to break") with escalating alert sounds (minor = normal alert, major/extreme = critical/red alert).
- A **mood tooltip** that itemizes *every individual thought* with its numeric offset ("Slept in the cold −5, Ate without table −3, Barracks −4"), so cause is fully legible.
- During a break the colonist's **name turns green** in the colonist bar and a **lightning-bolt icon** appears (yellow = non-aggressive, red = aggressive).

**Tynan Sylvester's stated design philosophy.** In his 2017 GDC talk "RimWorld: Contrarian, Ridiculous, and Impossible Game Design Methods," Sylvester's design-pillar slide reads "**Not a game – a story generator**," and argues that in a story generator "loss is an essential part of a story, not its conclusion," so "your mechanics must include loss *and recovery*." Mental breaks are the archetypal loss-and-recovery beat, and the legibility of *why* a pawn broke is central to the story generator working — the player must be able to author the causal narrative. He also authored *Designing Games: A Guide to Engineering Experiences*.

**Resolution / recovery.** Breaks are **timed with per-def recovery windows** (e.g. Murderous rage = fixed 100,000 ticks / 40 h; Tantrum = min 8,000 / max 12,000 ticks; daze = MTB 10,000 ticks, min 2,500 / max 90,000). They can be ended early by: **arrest** (chance-based; ends the break but downgrades to prisoner and, if released, replaces catharsis with a −8 "was imprisoned" thought); **downing/beating** (blunt melee to pain-incap — the only way to stop Berserk); **Word of Serenity** psycast (forces a 6-hour coma); or mods like SnapOut that add warden "calm down" attempts scaled by Social skill. **The anti-spiral mechanic is the "Catharsis" thought.** Per the RimWorld Wiki (Mental break): "Pawns get the 'catharsis' thought, granting a +40 mood for the next 2.5 days. This will generally prevent... a spiral of mental breakdowns in all but the worst scenarios" (it can stack to +70/+93; a Void catharsis variant grants +30). Some outcomes are effectively permanent (Given up and leaving = the pawn walks off the map).

### 3. Comparative resolution design

| | Dwarf Fortress | RimWorld |
|---|---|---|
| Recovery model | **Decay-to-baseline** (stress slowly normalizes if needs met) | **Timeout + intervention** (fixed recovery windows; arrest/beat/psycast shortcut) |
| Anti-spiral device | Dual short/long-term gate; years-long accumulation; needs-based recovery | **Catharsis buffer (+40 mood / 2.5 days)** post-break |
| Terminal states | Yes — insanity is permanent and fatal | Few (leave map, suicide-adjacent); most are temporary |
| Player agency | Indirect (fix environment, wall them in) | Direct (arrest, beat, psycast, calm) + indirect (fix mood) |

**Design consequence:** DF's decay-to-baseline + terminal states produce *tragedy* and force environmental problem-solving, but frustrate players who feel powerless. RimWorld's timeout + catharsis + direct intervention keeps the player *in the driver's seat* and prevents death spirals, at the cost of some drama. **For Chronicle, RimWorld's model is the better fit for player agency** — a player co-present with a grieving or vengeful NPC should have diegetic ways to intervene (console the NPC, complete a quest, remove the stressor), and the catharsis buffer prevents the same NPC from thrashing.

### 4. Skyrim implementation reality

**How Skyrim swaps AI behavior at runtime.** The canonical tool is PapyrusUtil's `ActorUtil.AddPackageOverride(Actor targetActor, Package targetPackage, int priority = 30, int flags = 0)` — a global native SKSE function. Priority runs **0–100 (100 highest); on ties the last-added package wins; overrides persist through save games** and override packages from all other sources. Companions: `RemovePackageOverride`, `ClearPackageOverride`, `CountPackageOverride`. This is effectively a runtime priority-injection state machine — directly analogous to DF's mood-state takeover. Alternatives/complements: quest-alias package stacks (the Immersive Citizens / AI Overhaul approach), the Story Manager (event-node-driven quest triggers), faction/relationship rank changes (`SetRelationshipRank`, `ModFactionRank`) which persist on the actor record regardless of load state, and idle-animation injection via animation events.

**Latency and reliability constraints (from Papyrus/SKSE research):**
- Papyrus VM gets `fUpdateBudgetMS = 1.2` ms/frame for function dispatch (+`fExtraTaskletBudgetMS = 1.2`); stack memory `iMaxAllocatedMemoryBytes ≈ 76,800` bytes (Skyrim LE default). Raising these causes stack thrashing, stutter, and CTDs (visible as "Suspended stack count is over our warning threshold… VM is freezing" in the Papyrus log).
- Realistic **minimum reliable poll is ~0.1 s** (`RegisterForSingleUpdate(0.1)`); if a handler overruns its interval, event calls stack → suspended-stack dumps and save bloat. Prefer game events over polling.
- **NPCs in unloaded cells do not tick.** With default `uGridsToLoad=5`, actor AI runs in a **3×3 cell grid** around the player. Package/faction changes pushed to off-screen NPCs *persist* but only take *behavioral* effect on cell-attach. Design for **eventual consistency**, with apply-on-load hooks.
- Same-engine data (Fallout 4, SKK tools) shows ~130 actors active in a dense cell and a hard refusal above ~300; realistically you can affect **tens of loaded actors live**. Bulk package swaps should be **batched across frames (single-digit to low-tens per second)** or offloaded to native C++ to avoid stack dumps.
- **Bridge patterns:** (a) **HTTP loopback** — a game-side SKSE HTTP plugin polls/calls your Python service; this is Mantella's v0.12+ model (a separate Python 3.11 exe; game acts as HTTP client). (b) **Native C++ SKSE DLL** with named pipe / WebSocket / embedded HTTP — SkyrimNet's model, which runs entirely in-process (embedded server on localhost:8080, MCP tools on 8889) and explicitly avoids external-bridge latency; its companion **SeverActions** ships, per its README, an SKSE plugin whose "276 native functions replace slow Papyrus operations with native C++ implementations, providing 100-5000x performance improvements." (c) **File bridge** via PapyrusUtil `JsonUtil` (external Python writes JSON, Papyrus polls) — simplest but reintroduces the ~0.1 s poll floor and has no push/notify.
- **Real-world latency:** Mantella/SkyrimNet report NPC responses in ~2–6 s, dominated by LLM/TTS, not the bridge; a native loopback bridge itself is sub-frame. For Chronicle's non-conversational behavior pushes, target **~1–3 s** for live NPCs.
- **A documented ActorUtil failure mode:** applying the *same* package form across many NPCs causes "timeshare" behavior (NPCs take turns executing it) rather than all running it. Use per-archetype package instances or quest-alias stacks.

**Radiant AI failure modes as a cautionary tale.** Skyrim's Radiant AI gives NPCs general goals ("eat here at 2pm") and lets them path to them; package conflicts, simultaneous procedures, and stuck scenes produce behavior that *reads as bugs* (NPCs looping on wall-lean markers, walking off mid-combat, standing idle). Per paavohtl's researched history "What was Radiant AI, anyway?", Bethesda's Pete Hines said in PC Zone #165 (March 2006) that "while complex AI was important, they had to scale things back (like NPCs stealing from the player) to keep the game understandable; otherwise a player might assume their items disappeared due to a bug"; Todd Howard separately recounted an NPC leaving a fight mid-combat to buy a dagger. **The lesson for Chronicle is explicit: unattributed emergent behavior is indistinguishable from a bug. Legibility is not polish — it is the core requirement.**

### 5. Lessons from other legible-AI systems

- **The Sims — motive + advertisement + telegraphing.** Objects "advertise" motive-satisfaction scores (with adjustable attenuation/range); Sims path to the highest-advertised need. Crucially the *internal* state is externalized by the **plumbbob** (green/yellow/red) and **thought bubbles** showing the specific low motive with a matching animation (clutching the stomach = hungry). A masterclass in making an invisible utility system legible with a single overhead glyph + an animation tell — but it relies on overhead UI Skyrim lacks.
- **Shadow of Mordor — the Nemesis System** makes NPC *memory* legible through **dialogue callbacks** ("Remember me?") and **visual scarring** (burn marks reflecting how you fought them). Design director Michael de Plater built it as a "villain simulator" where "scarring and memory and that relationship" are surfaced every encounter. This is the single most transferable model for Chronicle: **make the belief legible through the NPC's own dialogue and appearance**, tied to the specific triggering event, every time the player meets them.
- **Crusader Kings** externalizes its opinion/stress numbers through **event pop-ups** with named causes and consequences — the abstract number becomes a narrated incident.
- **Academic / believable-agents work** (Prom Week / Comme il Faut by Josh McCoy & Michael Mateas; Versu; the BDI and "emotionally believable agents" literature) shows that social state becomes legible when the system exposes the *rule that fired* (the social exchange, the influence rule) to the player rather than just the outcome — attribution is a first-class design output, not an afterthought.

## Recommendations: a threshold→behavior→legibility architecture for Chronicle

**Adopt this staged model.** For each of ~150 named NPCs, Chronicle's Python service maintains continuous belief variables (grudge, grief, fear, obligation, plus per-target memories). Map them like RimWorld's banded thresholds, drive behavior like DF's state takeover, and *always* precede a behavior change with a diegetic tell.

**Stage 1 — Selection (RimWorld model).**
- Define **three intensity bands per belief axis** with hysteresis (separate trip-up and reset thresholds to prevent flicker). Using the task's example numbers as the *major* band: grief > 80, grudge > 60, fear > 40. Set a lower *minor* band (e.g. grief 50, grudge 40, fear 25) and a higher *extreme* band.
- Within a band, choose the specific behavior by **weighted random over eligible behaviors** — a direct analogue of `TryRandomElementByWeight(CommonalityFor)` filtered by `BreakCanOccur`. Gate eligibility by NPC archetype/personality (a timid NPC can't go "berserk"; a guard can), location, time of day, cell-load state, and relationship to the target of the belief. This yields variety and replayability while staying in-character.
- **Keep a per-NPC cooldown and a "catharsis" buffer:** after any visible behavior episode ends, suppress re-triggering for an in-game period (RimWorld's +40/2.5-day analogue). This is your anti-spiral device and prevents the Radiant-AI "thrashing NPC = bug" perception.

**Stage 2 — Behavior injection (DF/Skyrim state-machine model).**
- Implement each behavior as an **AI package applied via `AddPackageOverride`** at a reserved Chronicle priority band (e.g. 40–60, above vanilla sandbox ~30 but below combat/quest-critical) so it visibly takes over — swap schedule (a grieving NPC skips work and visits a grave), sandbox location (a fearful NPC hides indoors), or idle animations (a vengeful NPC glares, cleans a weapon).
- **Do not share one package form across many NPCs** (the documented ActorUtil timeshare bug); use per-archetype package instances or quest-alias stacks.
- **Apply changes on cell-attach for off-screen NPCs** (eventual consistency). Batch swaps at single-digit-to-low-tens per second; offload bulk work to a native SKSE DLL if you exceed that.

**Stage 3 — Legibility (the part Skyrim forces you to invent).** Because there is no mood bar, tooltip, or pause-inspect, layer *at least three* redundant diegetic channels per behavior change, staged over time:

1. **Pre-break tells (hours-to-days of lead time).** Before the major package swap, apply a *minor* visible change: a new idle animation (staring, pacing, head-in-hands), wandering to a meaningful location (the grave, the tavern, the home of the grudge's target), or a posture/clothing change. This is the Sims "thought-bubble tell" translated into 3D body language. **Lead time matters more in 3D than in a top-down game** — a player who is physically co-present but sees the NPC only intermittently needs the tell to persist long enough (target *at least a full in-game day of intermittent exposure*) to register cause before the consequence.
2. **First-person dialogue and greetings that name the cause** (the Nemesis callback). The NPC's greeting/idle barks should reference the specific triggering belief: "You let my brother die at Helgen and you dare speak to me?" This is the highest-value legibility channel in a first/third-person game — unambiguous, diegetic, and attributing cause to a named event. Existing external-service mods (Mantella/SkyrimNet) already inject NPC dialogue from an external process; reuse that pipe.
3. **Ambient NPC-to-NPC gossip.** Third-party NPCs discuss the cause ("Have you seen Sigrid since her husband passed? She hasn't left the house"). This lets the player learn the cause even without interacting with the afflicted NPC — the Crusader Kings "narrated incident" pattern.
4. **Persistent artifacts:** a **journal/quest-log entry** or a **letter/courier note** when a belief crosses the major threshold ("A grief has taken hold of Sigrid of Whiterun"), and optional **visual signifiers** (mourning clothes, carrying a memento, a lighting/particle cue near the NPC). These replace DF's colored announcement and RimWorld's alert.
5. **Escalation staging:** minor tell → dialogue reference → (if unresolved) major behavior change → (extreme) the dramatic act. Never jump straight to the extreme act with no prior signal — that is precisely the Radiant-AI-reads-as-bug failure.

**Stage 4 — Resolution (RimWorld model, for agency).** Give the player *diegetic* intervention: a dialogue "console/reassure" option (Social-skill-gated success like SnapOut wardens), completing a related quest, removing the stressor, or time-based decay if left alone. Reserve *permanent* outcomes (an NPC who leaves town, a grudge that becomes a permanent hostile faction relationship) for beliefs left unresolved past an extreme threshold — the DF terminal-state analogue, used sparingly for drama.

**Benchmarks that change the plan:**
- If **playtesters attribute a behavior change to a bug**, add an earlier/longer tell and a dialogue line; treat any "is this NPC broken?" report as a legibility defect, not a balance one.
- If **script lag / stack dumps appear** (check the Papyrus log for "suspended stack count"), reduce swaps-per-frame, raise the poll interval above 0.1 s, and move injection to a native DLL.
- If **NPCs thrash between states**, widen hysteresis and lengthen the catharsis cooldown.
- If **latency exceeds ~3 s for live NPCs**, move from a JsonUtil file-poll bridge to HTTP loopback or a native C++ SKSE bridge.

## Caveats
- **Exact DF numeric thresholds are community reverse-engineering, not official.** The +10,000/+25,000/+50,000 long-term-stress status bands and the propensity-facet mappings come from the Dwarf Fortress Wiki's testing (a 300-dwarf/3-fortress sample) and DFHack source, not from Tarn Adams's published spec; the raw stress numbers are not viewable in-game without utilities.
- **RimWorld internals are from a community decompile** (`josh-m/RW-Decompile`) and the RimWorld Wiki; field names (`MentalBreaker`, `CommonalityFor`, `BreakCanOccur`, `TryRandomElementByWeight`) are accurate to the decompiled build, but exact `baseCommonality` values per break and recovery-tick windows vary by version and DLC. Treat specific tick counts as version-approximate.
- **Skyrim actor-count and throughput ceilings** (~130 active, ~300 hard cap, packages-per-second) are inferred largely from same-engine Fallout 4 data (SKK) and documented frame/stack budgets, not from a published Skyrim benchmark. Prototype and measure on your target load order before committing to a 150-NPC live population.
- **The Nemesis System is patent-encumbered** (Warner Bros., until ~2036). Chronicle should implement legible NPC memory through *dialogue and appearance* (generic techniques) without copying the Nemesis System's specific promotion/hierarchy mechanics.
- **LLM-driven dialogue (Mantella/SkyrimNet) adds nondeterminism.** If Chronicle uses an LLM for the cause-naming dialogue channel, constrain it to reference the *specific* triggering belief/event so attribution stays accurate; a hallucinated cause is itself a legibility failure.
