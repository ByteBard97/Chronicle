# The Sims Social-Simulation Mechanics: A Deep-Research Report for the Chronicle Project

*Research date: 2026-09-07. Prepared as a comparative-systems reference for Chronicle, the Skyrim SE/AE social-simulation mod (beliefs with provenance, mutating rumors, grudges/obligations that write back into behavior).*

---

## Executive Summary

The short verdict, stated up front and defended in detail below: **The Sims is weak precedent for Chronicle's core goal — "the whole town's social web evolves whether or not you're watching" — but it is the single best documented source of *player-scale* social mechanics, several of which transfer well to a walk-around RPG.** The specific claim your project inherited ("Richard Evans's AIIDE paper says Sims 3 only simulated the current lot") turns out to be **accurate in substance but misattributed in citation**: the citable statement comes from Zubek, Horswill, Robison & Viglione's AIIDE 2021 practitioner paper on *City of Gangsters*, which describes The Sims 3 as "an interesting transitional case, as it used both forward simulation and a simple rule-based system, although detailed social simulation was only performed on the characters in the current 'lot'" ([Zubek et al., AIIDE 2021](http://robert.zubek.net/publications/social-modeling-via-logic-programming-in-city-of-gangsters.pdf)). The same characterization also appears in the Versu paper co-authored by Richard Evans himself, so the claim carries a first-party fingerprint ([Evans & Short, IEEE T-CIAIG 2014](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6648395/)).

However, the claim needs one major nuance your internal notes lack: while **detailed moment-to-moment social simulation** was lot-scoped, Sims 3 *also* shipped "Story Progression," a coarse-grained background system that autonomously marries, impregnates, promotes, moves, and kills unplayed households — and The Sims 4 retrofitted an equivalent ("Neighborhood Stories") in 2021–2022 ([EA — Introducing Neighborhood Stories](https://www.ea.com/en/games/the-sims/the-sims-4/news/introducing-neighborhood-stories), [Rock Paper Shotgun](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/the-sims-4-neighborhood-stories)). So the franchise *did* attempt a world that changes without the player — but only as a **statistical, event-level demographic engine, never as a belief-level social simulation**. Off-screen Sims don't form opinions, hold grudges, or spread rumors; they accrue life events. That gap is exactly the space Chronicle occupies.

The three mechanics most worth adapting, in order: **(1) The Sims 4 Sentiments system** (patch 103, Nov 2020) — directed, event-provenanced, decaying relationship state ("Festering Grudge," "Betrayed by Cheating") that is almost a 1:1 design sketch of Chronicle's beliefs-with-provenance ([Sims Wiki — Sentiment](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Sentiment)); **(2) The Sims 2's gossip-memory system** — the franchise's only true secondhand-information mechanic, where invisible "gossip memories" propagate person-to-person through chat ([Sims Wiki — Memories](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Memories)); and **(3) the two-timescale relationship model** (daily/STR + lifetime/LTR scores with asymmetric decay), a cheap and proven way to make relationships feel alive without full belief simulation ([Sims Wiki — Relationship](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)).

## 1. Source Forensics: Verifying the Evans "Current Lot" Claim

### 1.1 The actual provenance of the claim

The project's internal note attributes the lot-scoping claim to "Richard Evans's AIIDE paper on Sims 3's AI." No such paper exists under that exact description. Richard Evans (AI lead on The Sims 3) has two relevant public artifacts: his **GDC 2010 talk "Modeling Individual Personalities in The Sims 3"** ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1012450/Modeling-Individual-Personalities-in-The)) and his co-authorship of the **Versu** simulationist-storytelling paper with Emily Short ([Evans & Short, IEEE T-CIAIG 2014](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6648395/)). The verbatim claim — "The Sims 3 (Evans, 2009) is an interesting transitional case, as it used both forward simulation and a simple rule-based system, although detailed social simulation was only performed on the characters in the current 'lot'" — appears in **Robert Zubek, Ian Horswill, Ethan Robison & Matthew Viglione, "Social Modeling via Logic Programming in City of Gangsters," AIIDE 2021**, a peer-reviewed practitioner paper ([Zubek et al., AIIDE 2021](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18912); [author PDF](http://robert.zubek.net/publications/social-modeling-via-logic-programming-in-city-of-gangsters.pdf)). The same sentence also appears in Evans & Short's Versu paper, which is why the claim has an "Evans said it" flavor in secondary citations — but for your library, the correct primary citation is Zubek et al. 2021, with the Versu paper as corroboration from Sims 3's own AI lead.

Zubek's paper is doubly useful because it situates The Sims in a taxonomy that maps directly onto Chronicle's design space: it divides recent social-simulation systems into **reputation systems**, **large-scale forward simulations with hundreds of NPCs**, and **smaller symbolic-reasoning systems with 5–20 NPCs**, and places Sims 3 in the awkward middle — large population, but shallow per-character social state ([Zubek et al., AIIDE 2021](http://robert.zubek.net/publications/social-modeling-via-logic-programming-in-city-of-gangsters.pdf)). City of Gangsters itself (≈1,200 NPCs whose opinions are computed by logic-programming inference over a relationship graph, including second-order propagation such as "you extorted my cousin, so my family won't deal with you") is arguably a much closer commercial precedent for Chronicle than The Sims ([AAAI — paper record](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18912); [UCSC thesis citing it](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4mr6c29j/qt4mr6c29j.pdf)).

### 1.2 What Evans's own talk actually describes

Evans's GDC 2010 presentation (slides and summaries preserved via academic secondary sources) describes Sims 3's character model as: each Sim carries **five traits drawn from a pool of roughly 80**, and those traits are **data-driven**, modifying *how* actions are performed (walk style, speech style, idle behavior) and which interactions the autonomy system prefers ([IGDA Japan SIG summary of the GDC talk](http://igda.sakura.ne.jp/sblo_files/ai-igdajp/sig_ai_7/YMiyake_IGDA_GameAI_seminar_7th_02_2010_6_12.pdf); [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1012450/Modeling-Individual-Personalities-in-The)). The underlying decision engine descends directly from The Sims 1's motive engine: objects **advertise** motive deltas (a fridge advertises Hunger +40; a bathtub advertises Hygiene +40, Comfort +30), and each Sim computes total expected utility — the projected Mood — across nearby advertisements and picks the maximum ([SimsTek Wiki — SimAntics/Edith](https://simstek.fandom.com/wiki/SimAntics); [Ken Forbus's Northwestern lecture notes, "Under the Hood of The Sims"](https://simstek.fandom.com/wiki/SimAntics)). This is the "simple rule-based system" half of Zubek's "transitional case" characterization; the "forward simulation" half is the open-neighborhood clock in which all Sims age and story-progression events fire.

So the verified, nuanced picture is: **Sims 3 simulates every Sim's *demographic life* continuously but simulates any Sim's *social cognition* only when that Sim is instantiated on the active lot.** The moment a Sim leaves the lot, its relationships, moods, and opinions stop evolving in any fine-grained sense; what continues is the coarse story-progression dice-rolling (Section 8). For Chronicle's purposes, this is confirmation of your working hypothesis with an important refinement: the obstacle was not that Maxis never wanted off-screen life — Sims 3's story progression proves they did — but that **belief-level social state was architecturally fused to the rendered scene**. Your own "fake movement, honestly simulate belief" split is precisely the decomposition The Sims never made.

## 2. Relationship Representation

### 2.1 The two-timescale scalar model (Sims 1–2)

The franchise's foundational representation is a **pair of directed scalars per (Sim A → Sim B) pair**: a short-term ("daily," STR) score and a long-term ("lifetime," LTR) score, each nominally −100 to +100. In The Sims 1 (from the Hot Date expansion onward), merely co-existing on a lot converted daily points into lifetime points — daily dropped 2, lifetime gained 1, up to ~24–28 points — while off-lot Sims suffered a flat −2 daily decay each day at 4pm with no lifetime compensation ([Sims Wiki — Relationship](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)). The Sims 2 kept both scores but removed passive co-presence gain: instead, **the lifetime score continuously normalizes toward the daily score** in 3-point steps three times per day (midnight, 8am, 4pm), while the daily score decays −2 every day at 4pm ([Sims Wiki — Relationship](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)). Discrete **relationship levels** (Acquaintance, Friend, Best Friend, Crush, Love, Enemy) are thresholded projections of these scalars — e.g., Friend requires mutual daily ≥ 50; Best Friend requires mutual lifetime ≥ 50 plus a confirming positive interaction ([Sims Wiki — Relationship](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)).

Two design observations matter for Chronicle. First, the representation is **purely accumulative: the numbers carry no provenance**. A Sim with lifetime −80 toward another Sim does not "know why"; the state is just a depth of well that interactions dig or fill. Community modders documented this as a flaw worth fixing — the popular simlogical "More Realistic Relationship Decay" hack exists precisely because players disliked that "if your sims hadn't seen each other for ages the relationship could settle back to 0,0," i.e., that strong impressions would evaporate for want of a reason-anchored representation ([simlogical — Sims 2 relationship hacks](https://simlogical.com/sl/Sims2Pages/Sims2_Relating.htm)). Second, the two-timescale structure is the franchise's cleverest cheap trick: **STR is volatile and fast-decaying, LTR is a sluggish trailing integral**, which produces psychologically plausible behavior (a single fight wrecks an evening but not a marriage; rebuilding trust takes many interactions) out of two floats and three cron ticks. The Sims 2: Nightlife layered "Fury" on top — a timed state that slams both scores below −50 and tints the offender's portrait red, with the red intensity indicating remaining duration — an early, crude grudge mechanic with provenance implicit in its trigger ([Sims Wiki — Relationship](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)).

### 2.2 Sims 3 consolidation and Sims 4's dual-bar split

Sims 3 collapsed the model to effectively a single relationship bar per pair (0–100) with named stages (Stranger → Acquaintance → Distant Friend → Friend → Good Friend → Best Friend → BFF, plus Enemy/Old Enemy on the negative side and a separate romantic ladder: Romantic Interest → Partner → Fiancé(e) → Spouse, with "Ex-" states persisting after breakups) ([The Sims Wiki — Relationship](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/Relationship)). Neglect decays the bar toward "Distant Friend" — a mechanic players can only fully disable via the "Long Distance Friend" lifetime reward, which exists because decay-through-neglect is otherwise unconditional ([Sims Wiki — Lifetime reward](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Lifetime_reward)). The Sims 4 restored duality in a different axis: **separate Friendship (green) and Romance (pink) bars**, where the Friendship bar gates friendly/funny interaction availability and also lowers romantic-rejection probability, and both bars decay with days of non-contact ([Carl's Sims 4 Guide — Relationships](https://www.carls-sims-4-guide.com/relationships/); [Sims Wiki — Relationship](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)). Relationship labels in Sims 4 are combinatorial projections of the two bars — "Bad Romance," "It's Very Awkward," "Terrible Match," "Total Opposites" — a small but instructive case of deriving readable social *states* from continuous scores ([Sims Wiki — Relationship](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)).

The honest summary across all four generations: **relationship state is always a small vector of accumulated scalars with thresholded labels — never a structure with causal content.** Nothing in the base representation records *why* two Sims feel as they do; the franchise bolted provenance on later via memories (Sims 2) and sentiments (Sims 4), covered in Sections 4 and 5. For Chronicle, the transferable asset is the **two-timescale decay architecture** and the **labels-as-threshold-projections** pattern (cheap, legible to the player, easy to surface in dialogue conditions); the non-transferable lesson is that provenance cannot be retrofitted into a scalar — both attempts to do so (memories, sentiments) had to live as *parallel* data structures alongside the bars.

| Generation | Relationship storage | Decay rule | Provenance? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sims 1 (Hot Date+) | Daily + Lifetime scalars, directed per pair | Daily −2/day at 4pm for off-lot Sims; on-lot converts daily→lifetime ([Sims Wiki](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)) | None |
| Sims 2 | Daily (STR) + Lifetime (LTR), directed | STR −2/day at 4pm; LTR normalizes toward STR by ±3, 3×/day ([Sims Wiki](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)) | None (Fury state has implicit trigger) |
| Sims 3 | Single bar 0–100 + named stages, incl. persistent "Ex-" states | Neglect decays bar; "Long Distance Friend" reward disables ([Sims Wiki](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Lifetime_reward)) | None |
| Sims 4 | Dual bars: Friendship + Romance | Both decay with days of no contact ([Carl's Guide](https://www.carls-sims-4-guide.com/relationships/)) | **Yes — via parallel Sentiments** (Section 4.3) |

## 3. The Social Interaction Catalog and Autonomous Selection

### 3.1 What an "interaction" actually is

A Sims social interaction is not generated; it is a **hand-authored, scripted unit** (a SimAntics behavior tree in Sims 1–2, a tuned "mixer" XML interaction in Sims 4) with preconditions (relationship thresholds, traits, age, mood), an animation/dialogue payload, and outcome effects on the participants' state. The "menu" the player sees is the subset of the authored catalog whose precondition tests pass in the current context. Sims 4's tuning files expose this directly: each interaction declares `allow_autonomous`, target-type, commodity-based advertisement scores ("static commodities" and "false advertisements" that nudge the autonomy score of nearby objects/Sims), and outcome branches — e.g., a joke interaction that randomly runs either an "excited" or "horrified" social response animation pair ([Zero's Sims 4 Mods — Interactions tutorial](https://www.patreon.com/zer0_Mods_/posts/tutorial-8-57351933); [Sims 4 Studio forum — autonomy on objects](https://sims4studio.com/thread/25008/decreasing-autonomy-on-object)). Nothing in the chain is generative in the LLM sense; variety comes from a large authored catalog crossed with a rich precondition space.

Autonomous NPC-to-NPC selection is real and well-documented. When free will is enabled, a Sim continuously re-scores available interactions using the advertisement/utility machinery inherited from Sims 1 — motive deficits, trait pushes (toddlers are trait-pushed toward "Make a Mess"), current emotion/moodlets, relationship state, and distance all feed the score ([Sims Wiki — Free will](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Free_will); [Sims4Studio — advertisement mechanics](https://sims4studio.com/thread/25008/decreasing-autonomy-on-object)). MC Command Center's "Automation Scan" feature — which lets players list and disable autonomous interactions currently running on every Sim on the lot — is practical proof that **autonomous social behavior is a first-class, enumerable subsystem**, not an emergent accident ([MCCC — Automation Scan documentation](https://deaderpool-mccc.com/automationscan.html)).

### 3.2 Autonomy tuning as evidence of the scoring function's shape

The modding ecosystem around autonomy is effectively a crowdsourced reverse-engineering of EA's scoring function, and its shape is instructive. The "Deeper Social Autonomy" mod rewrites the autonomy tests for Friendly/Funny/Mean/Mischief/Romance interactions so that Sims "socialize in accordance with their traits, moods, relationships, preferences, skills" — which is only necessary because the **vanilla tests are famously permissive**, letting Sims flirt with enemies or chat while bladder-critical ([CurseForge — Deeper Social Autonomy](https://www.curseforge.com/sims4/mods/deeper-social-autonomy)). Its modules read like a bug list for utility-AI social selection: "Mean Limiter" (cap consecutive mean interactions per target), "Social Reach" (scale initiation distance by mood, relationship, and traits), "Discourage Romance If Rejected," "Motives Not Red" (suppress socializing while needs are critical) ([CurseForge — Deeper Social Autonomy](https://www.curseforge.com/sims4/mods/deeper-social-autonomy)).

Two Chronicle-relevant lessons fall out. One: **utility scoring over a scripted catalog is sufficient for believable moment-to-moment social behavior at room scale** — nobody has ever needed more than this for a dinner party to look alive. Two: the failure modes are all *contextual* (wrong target, wrong timing, wrong repetition cadence), not *generative* — the answer was never more content, always more precondition dimensions. Sims 3's free-will documentation shows the trait-coupling explicitly: high free will "will choose interactions based on the Sim's traits, favorites, and how they are compatible with other Sims in socialization," while romance is never initiated autonomously without traits like Flirty or Hopeless Romantic — a design choice that **deliberately caps autonomous relationship formation** unless story progression or specific traits push it ([Sims Wiki — Free will](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Free_will)).

## 4. Memories, Moodlets, and Needs

### 4.1 Sims 2: the franchise's only true memory system

The Sims 2 stores **discrete, typed, per-Sim memory objects** ("Won a fight with $Subject," "Got married," "Relative died") in the Simology panel, each with a positive/negative valence that can depend on the Sim's aspiration — Knowledge Sims collect *positive* memories of alien abduction; Romance Sims collect *negative* memories of getting married ([Sims Wiki — Memories](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Memories)). Memories are durable (they persist indefinitely, survive death for relative-linked events, and can only be removed via external tools or the Apartment Life "Tabula Rasa" spell) and they have behavioral reach: **recent memories weight conversation-topic selection**, decaying in salience until they stop being topics ([Sims Wiki — Memories](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Memories)). The data model even carries a `$Subject` slot — a memory is explicitly about *another Sim* — which is the closest the franchise ever came to a structured event record with participants ([TheSimsWiki — Adding memories in SimPE](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/Game_guide:Adding_memories_in_SimPE)).

Critically for Chronicle, Sims 2 memories distinguish **witnessed from heard**: Section 5 details the gossip mechanism, but the memory system is its substrate — a Sim who "Spread the Gossip" tells a memory it owns, and the listener acquires an **invisible gossip memory** (not shown in the panel, visible only in SimPE) that can itself be re-told ([Sims Wiki — Memories](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Memories)). This is an evidence chain in embryo: first-person memory versus secondhand copy, differing only in visibility, not in kind. Notably, Sims 3 abandoned discrete memories almost entirely in favor of moodlets — the 2009 "memory" is a moodlet with a timer — and Sims 4 shipped with no memory panel at all, backfilling only via Sentiments (2020) and Milestones (Growing Together, 2023).

### 4.2 Sims 3–4: moodlets and the emotion stack

Sims 3 replaced the single mood bar with a **sum-of-moodlets** model: each moodlet is (name, ±mood effect, duration/termination condition, cause), and the Sim's Mood is the arithmetic total — e.g., "Sleepy" (no effect, 3h) → "Tired" (−40, 3h) → "Exhausted" (−50, until sleep) is a scripted escalation chain driven by the Energy need ([Sims Wiki — List of Moodlets (Sims 3)](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/List_of_Moodlets_(The_Sims_3))). Socially-caused moodlets carry provenance in their *cause string* but not in any queryable structure: "Offended" (−15, 3h) fires from insults and makes subsequent negative interactions hit harder (Hot-Headed Sims take 30% more effect) — the Sim is *simulated as aggrieved* without the game retaining *at whom* ([Sims Wiki — List of Moodlets (Sims 3)](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/List_of_Moodlets_(The_Sims_3))).

Sims 4 generalized this into the emotion system: moodlets are bucketed by emotion channel (Happy, Sad, Angry, Flirty, Focused, Playful…), each channel sums its moodlet strengths, and **the highest total becomes the dominant emotion**, with Happy uniquely converting 1:1 into whatever positive emotion leads, and +8 total producing the "Very X" tier ([Sims Wiki — Emotion](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Emotion); [Carl's Guide — Emotions](https://www.carls-sims-4-guide.com/emotions/)). The dominant emotion gates available interactions, biases autonomy, and drives Whims ([IGN — Emotions guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/the-sims-4/Emotions)). The community's documented complaint is a real design lesson for Chronicle: **flat point-summation lets stale trivia drown fresh drama** — a Sim whose spouse just cheated stays Happy because the house is nicely decorated — which is why the highest-rated community fix proposals are recency-weighting and allowing negative stacking ([EA Forums — emotion system discussion](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/the-sims-4-general-discussion-en/how-can-we-fix-the-emotion-system/1575354)). If Chronicle's belief system ever reduces to scalar mood, it will inherit exactly this failure mode; the fix is to keep emotional *tone* scalar but emotional *object* structured.

### 4.3 Sims 4 Sentiments: provenance bolted onto the bars

Sentiments (base-game patch 103, November 2020, shipped alongside Snowy Escape) are **directed, per-(Sim→Sim) records of emotionally significant shared events**, each with flavor text naming the cause, a short/long duration class, and behavioral effects (proximity-triggered moodlets, biased autonomy) ([Sims Wiki — Sentiment](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Sentiment); [Carl's Guide — Sentiments](https://www.carls-sims-4-guide.com/sentiments.php)). The design constraints are brutally economical: **at most four sentiments per target, weakest evicted on overflow; all fade over time** ([Carl's Guide — Sentiments](https://www.carls-sims-4-guide.com/sentiments.php)). They are explicitly **asymmetric** — "one Sim can be resentful about their recent divorce while the other Sim can be perfectly chill about it" — which makes them genuine per-agent belief state rather than a pair property ([SimsCommunity — Sentiments list](https://simscommunity.info/2020/11/10/list-of-all-the-sentiments-in-the-sims-4/)).

The catalog is the design lesson. "Festering Grudge" (long-term: deep-seated, hard to forgive), "Betrayed by Cheating" (long), "Grudging after a Fight" (short), "Guilty" (short — the *offender* carries a self-directed negative sentiment), "Awed by Lifesaving Hero," "Neglected by a Workaholic," "Dejected about Rejection" — this is a **vocabulary of social-debt and obligation states with named causes and differential half-lives**, conceptually isomorphic to Chronicle's grudges/obligations ([Sims Wiki — Sentiment](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Sentiment); [SimsCommunity — Sentiments list](https://simscommunity.info/2020/11/10/list-of-all-the-sentiments-in-the-sims-4/)). What sentiments lack relative to Chronicle: they do not propagate between Sims, they do not mutate, and they do not support third parties (a sentiment is strictly about the dyad that experienced the event). EA's own Neighborhood Stories wiring confirms sentiments are the designated attachment point for social consequences: patch 125 "added support for Sentiments" to autonomous life changes, and failed matchmaking attempts "backfire with a negative Sentiment" ([EA — Neighborhood Stories](https://www.ea.com/en/games/the-sims/the-sims-4/news/introducing-neighborhood-stories)).

## 5. Autonomous Relationship Formation and Gossip

### 5.1 Sims 2's gossip pipeline: the franchise's only secondhand channel

The Sims 2 implements genuine NPC→NPC information spread. Negative memories can be spread by gossip: "Sims who have heard a piece of gossip can pass it on to other Sims as well. This is because Sims accumulate gossip memories which do not appear in the memories panel" — gossip originates with Sims who *witnessed* a bad event, and propagates through the Gossip and Chat interactions to Sims who merely *heard* it ([Sims Wiki — Memories](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Memories)). The invisible accumulation is heavy enough that the community's FFS Lot Debugger ships an option to purge gossip memories, and adding memories via SimPE carries a documented corruption risk *because* gossip can spread references to unsafe NPCs ([Sims Wiki — Memories](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Memories); [TheSimsWiki — Adding memories in SimPE](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/Game_guide:Adding_memories_in_SimPE)). Nightlife added the complementary institutional channel: **Contacts** — Sims who "have heard of a Sim from a mutual friend" and cold-call that Sim for outings, with a temporary relationship boost — an explicit "friend-of-a-friend introduction" mechanic ([Sims Wiki — Relationship](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)).

Measured against Chronicle's rumor system, Sims 2 gossip is real but thin: **no mutation** (the gossip memory is a faithful copy of the source memory, $Subject intact), **no attenuation model** (no credibility decay by hop count), and **no belief revision** (hearing gossip does not visibly re-sculpt the hearer's relationship scores toward the subject beyond conversation effects). It is a copying channel, not a mutating one. Still, it refutes any claim that "The Sims never did secondhand information" — it did, in exactly one title, and the mechanic was effectively dropped in Sims 3 and never restored in Sims 4's base design.

### 5.2 Autonomous relationship formation: on-lot vs. off-lot

On the active lot, NPCs genuinely form and change relationships without player involvement: with free will on, background Sims chat, flirt, fight, and accrue relationship points with each other, and in Sims 2, enemies and furious Sims autonomously deploy negative socials ([Sims Wiki — Free will](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Free_will)). But the franchise consistently *throttles* autonomous relationship formation at the design level: in Sims 3, "romantic interactions won't be spontaneously initiated by Sims unless they have specific traits such as Flirty or Romantic," and WooHoo/Try-for-Baby are never autonomous — which the wiki notes directly causes townie stagnation unless story progression compensates ([Sims Wiki — Free will](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Free_will)). Sims 4's vanilla autonomy was so indiscriminate in the opposite direction (random flirting destroying scripted couples) that "Disable Random Flirting" and "Disable Mean Autonomy" are headline features of MCCC's tuner ([Hone.gg — MCCC settings guide](https://hone.gg/blog/mc-command-center-settings-in-sims-4/)).

Off-lot, autonomous relationship change exists only at the demographic level (story progression's marriages and breakups, Section 8) until Sims 4's Neighborhood Stories added *relationship-shaped* background events: neighbor Sims can autonomously "hang out with other Sims to form or deepen Friendly relationships," and the active Sim can "Trash talk one Sim to another, which will greatly reduce the relationship between those two Neighbor Sims" or play matchmaker — with dynamic guardrails (Sims won't pair with trait-conflicting partners, e.g., Good/Evil; trash-talking a friend backfires) ([EA — Neighborhood Stories](https://www.ea.com/en/games/the-sims/the-sims-4/news/introducing-neighborhood-stories)). Note the direction of flow: the player *injects* third-party influence ("Talk Up Another Sim," "Hook Up With") and the world executes it. The Sims 4 thus has, as of 2021–2022, a player-mediated gossip/advocacy channel — but **still no autonomous NPC-to-NPC rumor propagation**: no Sim ever tells another Sim what a third Sim did without the player's finger on the scale ([EA — Neighborhood Stories](https://www.ea.com/en/games/the-sims/the-sims-4/news/introducing-neighborhood-stories); [Rock Paper Shotgun](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/the-sims-4-neighborhood-stories)).

## 6. Personality and Trait Compatibility

### 6.1 From fixed vectors to computed chemistry

Sims 1 personality is five bipolar axes (Neat, Outgoing, Active, Playful, Nice), tied to zodiac signs ([SimsTek Wiki — SimAntics](https://simstek.fandom.com/wiki/SimAntics)). Sims 2's Nightlife expansion built the franchise's most fully documented compatibility function: each Sim has an **attraction score toward each other Sim**, computed from zodiac pairing (+35/±17.5/−35 tables), personality similarity, primary aspiration, and two turn-ons plus one turn-off (each matched turn-on +17.5, matched turn-off −22.5); the two directed scores are averaged into symmetric **chemistry**, displayed as −1 to 3 "bolts" ([Sims Wiki — Chemistry](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Chemistry); [StrategyWiki — Attraction and Chemistry](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/The_Sims_2:_Nightlife/Attraction_and_Chemistry)). Chemistry is not cosmetic: it "affects autonomous behaviour, can unlock socials, can trigger rivalry, [and] contributes to the start of date scores" ([StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/The_Sims_2:_Nightlife/Attraction_and_Chemistry)). The system is a **general compatibility function over shared feature vectors**, not authored per-pair — though it embeds authored per-pair *tables* (the zodiac matrix), a hybrid worth stealing: hand-authored cultural resonance layered on computed trait overlap.

### 6.2 Sims 3–4 and the modern revival

Sims 3 thinned compatibility back to implicit trait interaction — Sims "give positive reactions and interactions to Sims who are compatible in traits and the opposite with non-compatible traits," with Evans's design intent being that ~80 data-driven traits modulate *manner and preference* rather than a single pairwise score ([Sims Wiki — Free will](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Free_will); [IGDA Japan SIG summary](http://igda.sakura.ne.jp/sblo_files/ai-igdajp/sig_ai_7/YMiyake_IGDA_GameAI_seminar_7th_02_2010_6_12.pdf)). Sims 4 then rebuilt explicit compatibility twice: **Growing Together (2023)** added Social Compatibility — likes/dislikes of personality "characteristics" (e.g., liking Ambitionless Sims implies negative compatibility with Ambitious ones) driving platonic/familial ease — and **Lovestruck (2024)** added Attraction, a self-described "spiritual successor to chemistry," combining physical turn-ons/offs, skill-and-lifestyle "Way of Life" categories, characteristic preferences, and romance-style preferences, gating autonomous romantic behavior and interaction success ([Sims Wiki — Attraction](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Attraction); [SimsCommunity — Attraction guide](https://simscommunity.info/2025/09/17/sims-4-attraction-lovestruck-guide/)). Notably, EA's default implementation only computes attraction lazily — turn-ons are auto-assigned to unplayed-household NPCs "only WHEN they have none and need to compute attraction towards a played sim right that minute" — a telling instance of the franchise's simulate-only-what-the-player-can-see reflex ([Lumpinou — Lovestruck mod documentation](https://www.patreon.com/lumpinou/posts/mod-lovestruck-4-108890115)).

For Chronicle, the transferable insight is that **compatibility-as-function beats compatibility-as-data at RPG scale**: the zodiac/turn-on tables are a cheap authoring surface (a Skyrim equivalent might be faction/race/Hold cultural affinities as the authored matrix, with personality traits as the computed overlap). The failure to avoid is Sims 4's lazy instantiation — attraction that materializes only when the player looks is precisely the "player-scale" gravity well Chronicle is trying to escape.

## 7. Group, Household, and Family Dynamics

### 7.1 Households, outings, and clubs

Multi-Sim units in The Sims are historically *containers*, not social constructs: the household is a budget-and-residency bucket; relationships inside it remain strictly pairwise. Sims 2: Nightlife and Sims 3: Late Night/University Life experimented with **outings and social groups** (temporary gatherings, influence/reputation tracks), and Sims 4: Get Together (2015) made the franchise's fullest group construct: **clubs** of up to 8 Sims, with membership requirements (age, career, marital status, finances, traits, skill ≥2), up to 10 encouraged/discouraged activity rules, designated hangouts, club-vs-club hostility settings ("Be Mean To Renegades"), and autonomous in-gathering rule enforcement — rule-breakers get scolded by other members, losing friendship ([Sims Wiki — Club](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Club_(The_Sims_4:_Get_Together)); [SimsVIP — Get Together Q&A](https://simsvip.com/2015/11/28/the-sims-4-get-together-your-questions-answered/)). Clubs can even push Sims to act **against their traits** during gatherings — an explicit, temporary norm-override layer ([SimCitizens — Creating a Club](http://simcitizens.com/creating-a-club-in-the-sims-4s-get-together-expansion/)).

The instructive property for Chronicle is that club membership changes *behavioral affordances and normative expectations* without changing pairwise state: a club is a **reusable social context object** (roles: leader/member; rules: encouraged/discouraged behaviors; scope: during gathering at hangout) that autonomy consults as an extra precondition source. The limitation is equally instructive: clubs hold no internal social *state* — no cohesion score, no grudges between factions beyond a symmetric "rivals" flag, no memory of past gatherings. A club feud in The Sims is two clubs both configured "Be Mean To" each other; nothing accumulates ([SimsVIP — Get Together Q&A](https://simsvip.com/2015/11/28/the-sims-4-get-together-your-questions-answered/)).

### 7.2 Family dynamics as authored dyad-roles

Growing Together (2023) added **Family Dynamics**: seven pairwise relationship *types* (Close, Distant, Supportive, Permissive, Difficult, Strict, Jokesters), assignable in CAS or arising autonomously from compatibility, each biasing the dyad's autonomous interaction mix (Difficult pairs lean toward Complaints/Mean and form Furious sentiments; Strict elders autonomously discipline; Jokesters prank together) ([Sims Wiki — Family relationships](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Family_relationships); [TheGamer — Family Dynamics explained](https://www.thegamer.com/the-sims-4-family-dynamics-explained/)). Two constraints reveal the system's shallowness: dynamics are **symmetric by construction** ("Sims cannot have differing perceptions of each other" — a Strict parent cannot be experienced as Permissive by the child), and they apply only within kinship edges, with in-laws excluded ([GameRant — Family Dynamics guide](https://gamerant.com/sims-4-growing-together-family-dynamics-guide/)).

So: the franchise's answer to "more than the sum of pairwise relationships" is **typed pairwise edges plus context objects**, never a genuine group state. Family trees are pedigree data (MCCC even exports GEDCOM) ([MCCC documentation](https://deaderpool-mccc.com/documentation.html)); feuds don't exist as first-class entities anywhere in the shipped games. A Skyrim mod with faction-aware grudges (the Nords-vs-Thalmor texture of Chronicle's setting) is already past the frontier of what The Sims ever built.

## 8. The Off-Lot / Background-Simulation Question

### 8.1 Sims 1–2: the frozen world

In Sims 1 and Sims 2, **time does not pass for anyone outside the currently played lot** — neighborhoods are effectively serialized snapshots; even relationship decay for absent Sims is a bookkeeping tick (−2 STR/day at 4pm) rather than simulation ([Sims Wiki — Relationship](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)). Sims 2's design notoriously allows a player to keep a household in amber for generations while the played family ages. There is no abstraction layer at all: off-lot is not "lightly simulated," it is *not simulated*.

### 8.2 Sims 3: the open neighborhood and Story Progression

Sims 3's open world made the whole town technically co-present (Sims route around the neighborhood in real time), and on top of it sits **Story Progression**, enabled by default: "Neighbors may move away, new ones will move in, get promotions, get married, have children, make enemies, and even die… Sims' lives progress without the player's input" ([Sims Wiki — Story progression](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Story_progression)). This is the piece that complicates the Evans/Zubek claim, and it should be stated precisely: story progression is a **stochastic demographic event engine**, not a social simulation. Its documented pathologies give the game away — it "may cause Sims who are best friends to become rivals, or an adult to become friends with an unrelated baby," i.e., the events it fires are *not computed from the Sims' social state* ([Sims Wiki — Story progression](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Story_progression)). NRaas's documentation of EA's baseline behavior ("career changes… are pushed by EA Story Progression"; "if the sim is inactive and totally unemployed, the game itself may still place them into a job slot") confirms the mechanism: periodic scenario checks with random or lightly-trait-scored outcomes ([NRaas — StoryProgression FAQ](https://www.nraas.net/community/StoryProgression-FAQ-General); [NRaas — Interactions](https://www.nraas.net/community/StoryProgression-Interactions)).

So the correct, nuanced answer to Chronicle's core question is a **three-layer split**: (a) full social cognition — only on the active lot, per the verified claim; (b) coarse life-event simulation — town-wide, continuous, but state-free at the belief level; (c) relationship state — persistent bookkeeping that decays but never *evolves* off-lot (Sims 3's "Long Distance Friend" reward exists to exempt Sims from neglect-decay, confirming decay-not-evolution) ([Sims Wiki — Lifetime reward](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Lifetime_reward)). Nothing at layer (b) ever writes a *reason* into anyone's head; a Sims 3 town where everyone hates the player-character for something they did three towns away is mechanically impossible.

### 8.3 Sims 4: launch regression and the Neighborhood Stories retrofit

Sims 4 **launched in 2014 with no story progression at all** — inactive households were inert except for aging — a regression so unpopular that EA rebuilt the feature in public: patch 121 (Nov 30, 2021) added Neighborhood Stories (neighbor Sims autonomously consider career changes, babies, promotions, accepting proposals; active Sims can influence neighbors via new socials), and patch 125 (March 2022) made it fully autonomous and configurable per household category, with nine toggleable life events including adoption, moving, and accidental death ([EA — Introducing Neighborhood Stories](https://www.ea.com/en/games/the-sims/the-sims-4/news/introducing-neighborhood-stories); [Rock Paper Shotgun — how it works](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/the-sims-4-neighborhood-stories); [PCGamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-sims-4-is-giving-npc-households-the-ability-to-make-major-decisions/)). Design details worth noting: autonomous changes are trait-gated ("Neighbor Sims consider them depending on factors like Traits"), and — most revealingly about the franchise's player-scale gravity — the 2021 version had autonomous Sims **phone the player for permission** before acting ([EA — Neighborhood Stories](https://www.ea.com/en/games/the-sims/the-sims-4/news/introducing-neighborhood-stories)). As of patch 125, romance-related autonomous events (dating, marriage, divorce) remain absent from the nine supported events — the social web's edges are exactly what the background engine does *not* touch ([Rock Paper Shotgun](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/the-sims-4-neighborhood-stories)).

## 9. Community Overhaul Mods as Evidence

### 9.1 What the mods had to build

The mod ecosystem is the cleanest evidence of what the base games lack, because every major overhaul is a diff against EA's model. **NRaas StoryProgression** (Sims 3) replaces EA's engine with trait-scored, economically-grounded progression: inactive Sims get jobs matching traits and lifetime wants, pay taxes and child support, fall into debt and emigrate, and — crucially — perform **autonomous flirt interactions among themselves** "to simulate a life-like balance amongst the inactive members of town" ([NRaas — StoryProgression Interactions](https://www.nraas.net/community/StoryProgression-Interactions)). Its "caste" system exists because players needed to *protect* specific households from progression — evidence that the engine operates on the whole population indiscriminately ([NRaas — FAQ](https://www.nraas.net/community/StoryProgression-FAQ-General)). **MCCC** (Sims 4) recreates the same for Sims 4: its mc_pregnancy module "creates a basic story progression of neighborhood Sims in that they can get married and have children as they age-up without direct user control," via scheduled midnight checks scaled to lifespan settings, with move-in logic that avoids orphaning toddlers ([MCCC documentation](https://deaderpool-mccc.com/documentation.html); [TheSimsTree — MCCC guide](https://thesimstree.com/en/blog/the-sims-tips/mc-command-center-(mccc)-guide-for-the-sims-4-%E2%80%93-settings-and-features.html)).

Note what even these maximal overhauls do *not* do: they schedule **events** (marriages, pregnancies, moves), not **beliefs**. Nobody's story-progression mod propagates a rumor graph or computes an opinion; the state they mutate is the same demographic bookkeeping EA uses. The ceiling of twenty years of Sims modding is "the town's vital statistics churn without you," not "the town thinks without you."

### 9.2 The social-depth mods

The other mod family extends *on-lot* social depth. **WonderfulWhims** adds an Attractiveness system (per-Sim style and preference matching), **Impressions**, and nine computed **Personality Archetypes** (Sage, Hero, Jester, Caregiver, Lover, Innocent, Everyman, Greedy/Sinister) derived from traits, where archetype similarity raises compatibility and opposing archetypes resist relationship growth ([ModTheSims — WonderfulWhims](https://modthesims.info/d/647895/wonderfulwhims.html); [TheGamer — Wonderful Whims](https://www.thegamer.com/sims-4-turbodrivers-wonderful-whims-mod/)). **Deeper Social Autonomy** rewires autonomy tests so moods, sentiments, relationship bands, and compatibility bits actually gate NPC-initiated socials ([CurseForge — Deeper Social Autonomy](https://www.curseforge.com/sims4/mods/deeper-social-autonomy)). The pattern across both families: modders' energy goes into (a) making the background *demographically* alive and (b) making the foreground *contextually* sane. The middle — structured social cognition that persists and propagates — is empty in mods as in the base games. Chronicle is not retracing anyone's path.

## 10. Comparative Synthesis

### 10.1 Mechanics by generation

![Heatmap: social-simulation mechanics by Sims generation](sims_social_mechanics_heatmap.png)

The heatmap makes the franchise's actual trajectory visible: The Sims 2 was the high-water mark for *social cognition* (memories, gossip, chemistry); The Sims 3 traded cognition for *world continuity* (open neighborhood, story progression, moodlets); The Sims 4 spent a decade re-converging, adding the missing pieces one patch at a time — emotions at launch, sentiments in 2020, neighborhood stories in 2021–22, family dynamics in 2023, attraction in 2024 ([Sims Wiki — Sentiment](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Sentiment); [EA — Neighborhood Stories](https://www.ea.com/en/games/the-sims/the-sims-4/news/introducing-neighborhood-stories); [Sims Wiki — Family relationships](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Family_relationships); [Sims Wiki — Attraction](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Attraction)). No single title ever combined all of them, and the two Chronicle-relevant capabilities — secondhand information spread and world-scale belief evolution — never coexisted and never reached maturity in any title.

### 10.3 Where the real world-scale precedents live

If Chronicle's research library needs citations for "world-scale social web" systems, the academic/industrial lineage that explicitly positioned itself *against* The Sims' limitations is more useful than The Sims itself. Comme il Faut (UC Santa Cruz) was built expressly to exceed Sims 3: it supports **third-party social reasoning** ("x is cheating on y if x and y are dating and there is a character z also dating x" — which "The Sims 3 can only reference the two characters in an interaction") and a **social facts database** giving characters history-aware beliefs — "a feature completely missing from The Sims 3" ([McCoy et al., AIIDE 2011](http://www.ben-samuel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/CiF-AIIDE2011.pdf)). Evans & Short's Versu used a custom logic language (Praxis) for simulationist social worlds ([IEEE — Versu](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6648395/)); Zubek's City of Gangsters ran logic-programming social inference over ~1,200 NPCs with second-order propagation ([Zubek et al., AIIDE 2021](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18912)); and the academic line continues into recent work on social inference rules for vendettas and belief adoption ([UCSC eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4mr6c29j/qt4mr6c29j.pdf)). These systems, not The Sims, are the strong precedents for "the world reacts to the player and to big events, autonomously, whether or not the player is present."

## 11. Verdict

**The Sims is weak precedent for Chronicle's world-scale goal, and the corrected citation trail now shows exactly how weak and why.** The verified claim — detailed social simulation scoped to the current lot, per Zubek et al. 2021 and echoed by Evans himself in the Versu paper — holds up, with one correction and one nuance. The correction: it is not from "Richard Evans's AIIDE paper"; the citable source is Zubek, Horswill, Robison & Viglione, AIIDE 2021 ([AAAI record](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18912)). The nuance: Sims 3 and (post-2021) Sims 4 *do* run town-wide background simulation — but it is a **state-free demographic dice engine** whose documented failure mode (best friends randomly becoming rivals, adults befriending babies) is precisely the absence of the belief layer Chronicle is building ([Sims Wiki — Story progression](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Story_progression)). Even at its most autonomous, a Sims world never *thinks* off-screen; it merely *happens*.

**Within its actual competence — player-scale, on-screen social texture — The Sims remains the richest documented design catalog available**, and three mechanics are genuinely worth adapting despite the weak world-scale precedent. First and strongest: **Sentiments** (Sims 4, patch 103) — a shipped, playtested vocabulary of directed, cause-labeled, asymmetric, time-decaying dyadic states ("Festering Grudge," "Betrayed by Cheating," "Guilty") that is the closest commercial analogue to Chronicle's grudges-with-provenance; adapt the vocabulary and half-life classes, but drop the 4-slot cap and add the propagation and third-party reference Sims never attempted ([Sims Wiki — Sentiment](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Sentiment); [Carl's Guide](https://www.carls-sims-4-guide.com/sentiments.php)). Second: **Sims 2's memory/gossip pipeline** — the franchise's only secondhand channel proves that per-Sim event records with a subject slot can drive conversation and be copied person-to-person on consumer hardware from 2004; Chronicle's rumor mutation is a strict generalization of it ([Sims Wiki — Memories](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Memories)). Third: the **STR/LTR two-timescale relationship substrate** — two floats and a thrice-daily normalization tick produced twenty years of psychologically legible relationships ([Sims Wiki — Relationship](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)).

The final framing for the library: The Sims demonstrates that **believable social *performance* is a solved problem at room scale** (utility scoring over authored interactions, moodlet tone, compatibility functions), and that **no mainstream life-sim has ever shipped social *cognition* at world scale** — the space between those two facts is exactly Chronicle's contribution. Where the Sims games do touch world-scale machinery (story progression), they are a cautionary tale about event engines without belief state; where they touch belief state (memories, sentiments), they are a proof of concept trapped at dyad scale. Chronicle's "fake movement, honestly simulate belief" split is not a variation on The Sims' architecture — it is the inversion of it.
