# NPC Social-Mechanics Catalog (2026-09-06)

Exhaustive inventory of named social/world-reactivity mechanics from every
game/system covered in this directory (Crusader Kings II/III, Kenshi,
Shadows of Doubt, the Nemesis System, RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress, plus the
genre-neighbor and AI-director material folded into those files: King of
Dragon Pass, Wildermyth, Total War, Mount & Blade II, and a dozen-plus
AI-director/drama-management systems). Compiled as a brainstorming worklist
— **no Skyrim-fit judgments, no ranking, nothing pre-filtered**; that's the
next conversation's job, item by item. Grouped by game, then loosely by
category. Citations point to the source file (all in this directory unless
noted). The Sims is deliberately **not** covered here — no dedicated
research exists on it yet; see
`notes/sims-social-simulation-research-prompt-2026-09-06.md`.

## 1. Crusader Kings II / III

### 1.1 Relationship/opinion tracking

- **Directed pairwise opinion.** Every character holds a separate opinion value of every other character (A→B ≠ B→A), practically −100 to +100, computed as a sum of individually-named, itemized modifiers shown in a tooltip/ledger. *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md, ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **Opinion modifier fields (scripting primitives).** `opinion` (flat value), `days/months/years` (lifetime), `decay` (bool, linear decay toward zero), `multiplier` (stacking count), `revoke_reason`/`prison_reason`/`execute_reason`/`banish_reason` (grants a legal right tied to the modifier), `enemy`, `crime`, `disable_non_aggression_pacts`, `non_aggression_pact`, `obedient` (forces AI acceptance of all diplomacy). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **CK2 flat-then-drop decay vs. CK3 gradual decay.** CK2 temporary modifiers hold full value for their duration then vanish instantly; CK3 modifiers linearly decay toward zero over their duration (a community-requested, later-implemented change). *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Succession opinion inheritance.** On non-elective succession, the heir temporarily inherits a fraction of the predecessor's opinion held by vassals — CK2: a flat inherited fraction; CK3: 25% of *positive* opinion but 50% of *negative* opinion (deliberate asymmetry so grudges outlive their targets more than goodwill does), decaying itself over time. *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Typed opinion categories (CK3).** Opinion is broken into General, Attraction (sexual-orientation-gated, modified by Beauty/Disfigured traits), Same Dynasty (with Virtues & Sins and Faith Hostility sub-categories), Relations, Tyranny, and Opinion of Predecessor — each separately itemized in the tooltip. *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **Vassal Stance categories (CK3).** Vassals are typed into Courtly/Glory Hound/Parochial/Zealot/Belligerent/Minor Landholder/Minority stances; liege actions (feasts, raising crown authority) apply modifiers only to vassals subscribed to the matching stance. *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **Diplomacy-skill-scaled opinion.** Personal Diplomacy skill contributes a formula-scaled opinion bonus toward non-rulers/courtiers (CK2: `+1.5 × (Personal Diplomacy − 4)`; CK3: roughly −8 to +92 across the skill range). *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md, ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **Prestige/piety-derived opinion.** Positive prestige/piety grant small opinion bonuses capped by engine defines (CK2: `+1 per 200 prestige, max +10`; negative prestige penalizes 10× as fast per point). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **Tyranny as pooled vs. itemized.** CK2 tracks each tyrannical act (unjust imprisonment, execution, forced abdication, overriding a council vote) as its own separately-expiring opinion entry (30-year expiry each); CK3 pools all tyranny into one meter (0–1000) with its own monthly decay rate (~−0.25/month baseline, modifiable by traits/artifacts/perks). *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md, ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **Named gift/grant modifiers with fixed durations.** Granting a barony/county/duchy/kingdom/empire gives escalating opinion (+20/+40/+60/+80/+100) for a fixed duration (CK2: 10 years) and stacks; monetary/artifact gifts scale by recipient greed/artifact quality and last ~5 years; repeated monetary gifts extend duration rather than stacking. *ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **Explorable opinion UI.** An "Explore Character Opinions" interaction lets the player inspect any arbitrary A-to-B opinion, and a map mode visualizes realm-wide sentiment geographically. *ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*

### 1.2 Named relationships (relationship crystallization)

- **Discrete relationship flags overriding the scalar.** Extreme opinion states crystallize into named booleans that unlock unique mechanics rather than living only as a number: Friend, Best Friend, Lover, Soulmate, Rival, Nemesis (CK3 adds Childhood→Adulthood→Lifelong tiers; friendship and conflict are mutually exclusive with each other, love can coexist with either). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Explicit opinion values + secondary effects per relation (CK3).** Friend +60 (+20% councilor efficiency), Best Friend +120 (+30% councilor efficiency), Lover +60 (+25% fertility), Soulmate +120 (+25% fertility), Rival −60 (−20% councilor efficiency), Nemesis −120 (−30% councilor efficiency) — each also carries a **stress-on-death** value (+10 for love/friend relations dying, −35 for a rival/nemesis dying), meaning losing (or outliving) a bonded character is itself a mechanical event. *ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **Formation always records a reason (a "memory").** Named-relation formation surfaces a specific in-fiction sentence explaining why ("Kaiser Heinrich and King Philippe shared an excellent feast in Aachen"), viewable in a Relationships tab. *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Relations gate/protect AI behavior.** An AI liege's friend/lover cannot be recruited into a faction against them; lovers can foil assassination attempts (human-shield mechanic); rivals are the dominant source of AI-initiated murder-scheme motivation. *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Inheritance of named relations on death.** Best Friend/Nemesis can transfer to a closely-related heir (with a mutual +60 opinion nudge if underlying opinion is too low to inherit directly) instead of simply vanishing. *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **House-level relationship crystallization (CK3).** Beyond individuals, entire dynastic Houses accumulate a Feuding/Neutral/Faithful relation from repeated member actions (gift to house head +20%, becoming best friends +20%, murder discovered −20%, etc.); if no relevant action occurs for 50 years, house relations drift one level toward Neutral. Feuding houses get −30 opinion, −25 marriage acceptance, +15 hostile scheme success, and unlock "Eradicate Rival House"; Faithful houses get the positive mirror plus alliance negotiation rights. *ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*

### 1.3 Secrets, hooks, leverage

- **Secrets (typed, discoverable, provenance-tracked).** Characters acquire a Secret when committing an illicit/taboo act (Adultery, Witchcraft, Murder Attempt, Illegitimate Birth, Cannibal, Deviant, Sodomite, Incestuous, Non-Believer, embezzlement, cheating at examinations, etc.). Secrets are discovered via random event or a Spymaster's "Find Secrets" task (a periodic skill contest against the target's Intrigue/Secrecy). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md*
- **Expose vs. Blackmail choice.** Discoverer picks Expose (applies the secret as a permanent trait publicly, unlocking imprisonment/execution justification) or Blackmail (creates a Hook — Weak for a "shunned" secret, Strong for a "criminal" one; refusal auto-exposes the secret). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Hooks as spendable leverage currency.** One hook per character at a time (a stronger/longer hook overwrites a weaker one). Weak hooks are single-use, mostly add acceptance bonuses (10-year default expiry). Strong hooks are reusable (5-year cooldown) and can *force* acceptance on interactions, and passively block the target from joining factions/declaring war/imprisoning/demanding conversion against the holder. Perpetual hooks are permanent, reusable, acceptance-only. *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Explicit spend-value table.** Concrete acceptance-bonus/force values per interaction type (Arrange Marriage +100→+200, Demand Conversion +50→Forced, Invite to Scheme +100→Forced, Force to Join Faction strong-only, Demand Council Position forced + no removal for 25 years, Force Vote forced for 100 years). Hooks explicitly cannot be spent on some interactions (Call to War, Offer Peace, Hire Mercenaries). *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Forgiving-trait hook abandonment.** A Forgiving character can voluntarily give up a held hook to relieve their own stress and grant the target a decaying opinion bonus — leverage as a two-way emotional transaction, not just extraction. *ck-mechanics-inventory.md*

### 1.4 Stress / internal-friction engine

- **A psychological buffer gating out-of-character action.** Characters gain Stress (CK3: 0–400) when taking actions that conflict with their own personality traits (a Just character imprisoning without cause; a Compassionate character torturing; an Honest character blackmailing). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Threshold breakdown events.** Crossing 100/200/300 stress for the first time immediately fires a Mental Break event; reaching 400 triggers a level-3 break and resets stress down by 100. Breaks can injure/kill the character, force them to murder a courtier, force abdication, or change a personality trait. Break cooldown is 5 years (8 with a perk). *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Coping mechanisms.** Resolving a break can adopt a coping trait (Comfort Eater, Drunkard, Flagellant) that permanently alters attributes/adds opinion penalties but grants passive stress-relief multipliers during stress-reducing activities. *ck-mechanics-inventory.md*
- **A documented, itemized trait→stress-source table.** e.g., Ambitious gains stress granting independence/titles or signing White Peace during an offensive war; Compassionate gains stress from a long list of coercive/violent actions; Vengeful gains stress from *forgiving* or restoring inheritance (the opposite polarity from most traits). *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*

### 1.5 Memories (persistent life-event log)

- **Structured, tagged, participant-linked memory objects** attached to each character (patch 1.7, "Friends & Foes"): timestamp, type tag (`battle_won`, `relative_murdered`, `feast_attended`), participant references, visibility scope (Public/Private). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Retention-by-rank.** Ordinary characters' memories fade/are usually lost on death; player characters and high-tier rulers retain memories permanently. Private memories (took a lover, committed a murder) are hidden from other players/observers. *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **Memories are queried by other systems, not just displayed.** Dialogue generators, duel context checks, and scheme-execution logic pull the exact memory ID of a real grievance to populate narrative text (e.g., an assassin's stated motive cites the specific real slight that provoked it). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md*
- **Viewable in a dedicated Memory Viewer** as a discrete UI surface, separate from the opinion ledger. *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*

### 1.6 Faction / collective political pressure

- **Faction power as a ratio, gating discontent accrual.** CK2: faction strength = combined member levies ÷ liege's levies, with ultimatum-firing thresholds (75% minimum, big likelihood jumps at 100%/150%). CK3: discontent accrues monthly once member military power exceeds 80% of the liege's, and falls when below; ultimatum fires automatically at 100% discontent (or immediately after an unjust imprisonment). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Hard eligibility gates independent of the probabilistic layer.** A character cannot join/lead a faction while imprisoned, underage, a Ceremonial Monarch, bound by a hook/alliance/truce, terrified (unless discontent is already rising), or above a fixed opinion-of-liege ceiling (CK3: ≥80). AI vassals additionally won't join against a friend/lover liege, though player vassals can. *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Per-trait join/leave multipliers — a fully published propensity table.** CK2: Ambitious 4×, Envious/Greedy/Impaler/Deceitful 2×, Proud/Brave/Arbitrary 1.5×, Content/Imbecile 0.01×, Craven 0.1×, Kind/Charitable/Honest 0.5×. Distance and culture/religion mismatch add further multipliers. *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **A Strong Hook or a resource threshold can force faction membership** for a fixed period (CK3: 10 years). *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Powerful-vassal council-seat mechanics.** A fixed number of "powerful vassals" (scaling by liege rank) deterministically demand a council seat; denial applies a large fixed opinion penalty (CK3: −40); a hook can force the seat, after which the holder cannot be removed for a fixed period (25 years). *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md, ck-mechanics-inventory.md*

### 1.7 Council mechanics

- **Five discrete voting-stance archetypes (CK2 Conclave), each with its own scoring formula.** Loyalist (supports the liege unconditionally unless self-destructive), Pragmatist (stability/threat-balance focused), Glory Hound (military-prestige focused), Zealot (religious-expansion focused), Malcontent (obstructs the liege by default). Each stance's eligibility/weight is computed from named traits + opinion + realm-power ratios. *ck-mechanics-inventory.md*
- **Concrete vote thresholds** for revocation/imprisonment/banishment/execution/title-grant support, keyed to the voter's opinion of the target. *ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **Council obstruction can trigger firing penalties** (recently-fired-from-council and fired-from-council are separate, differently-sized opinion modifiers). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md*

### 1.8 Schemes / plots (hostile and personal social actions)

- **CK2 plot power is a levy-contribution ratio** with role-based contribution multipliers (vassals of the target contribute 2× their levy ratio, foreign conspirators 1/3, council members 2× against rulers) and named power breakpoints (50/100/150/200%) tied to event-opportunity pacing. *ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **CK3 schemes split into Personal (Sway/Befriend/Seduce/Romance) and Hostile (Murder/Abduct/etc.).** Personal schemes score off Diplomacy + target opinion + trait compatibility + rank difference, no agents/secrecy. Hostile schemes use four tracked metrics: Potential (max theoretical success, capped, mostly filled by the schemer's own skill + recruited Agents), Secrecy (governs monthly detection probability; a failed secrecy roll adds a Breach; 5 Breaches force scheme collapse+discovery), Advantage (a resource spent to trigger execution, gained from Intrigue above a threshold), and Agent Acceptance (scored off opinion delta, personality traits, and Hook coercion). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Agent recruitment is opinion/trait-scored, with a documented player exploit.** Most characters face a flat "nothing to gain" penalty unless they dislike the target; marrying an Envious/Deceitful/hostile-faith courtier into the target's court so they "hate everyone" reliably yields free agents. Rivals of the target get extra protection as murder targets; being at war applies large success penalties to Murder/Abduct. *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Discovery consequences are explicit and stacking.** A discovered murder attempt applies large stacking opinion penalties scaled by relation to the target (parents/children, siblings), and a successfully-discovered murder converts surviving relatives into Rivals who may themselves seek murder. *ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*
- **Scheme-specific success/outcome tables** (Sway 95% chance of +25 opinion, Seduce +20 opinion and can create a Lover/secret, Befriend success upgrades Friend→Best Friend and disbands/removes the target from any faction they lead, Befriend failure applies a cascade of opinion penalties across the whole court). *ck-plaintext-numeric-values-inventory.md*

### 1.9 AI decision-making

- **A documented `ai_chance`/`ai_will_do` scoring idiom**: base weight + additive situational modifiers (`modifier { add = X, trigger }`) + multiplicative vetoes (`modifier { factor = 0, trigger }`) + personality-scaled terms (`ai_value_modifier`). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **Twelve-to-thirteen exposed hidden personality parameters** (`ai_boldness`, `ai_compassion`, `ai_greed`, `ai_honor`, `ai_rationality`, `ai_sociability`, `ai_vengefulness`, `ai_zeal`, `ai_war_chance`, `ai_war_cooldown`, `ai_amenity_spending`, `ai_amenity_target_baseline`, `ai_energy`), each with a documented behavioral meaning (e.g., Honor governs alliance-keeping and willingness to become a scheme agent; Vengefulness drives punishing transgressions). *ck-mechanics-inventory.md, ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*
- **A two-layer visible/hidden state split** — visible traits/opinion the player can reason about, plus a hidden personality-value layer that produces behavioral variance the player can't fully predict (the "expected randomness" pattern: surprising in outcome, retroactively explainable). *ck-failure-modes-and-reactivity-loop.md*
- **AI archetype bucketing** (Dev Diary #104): characters are grouped into named behavioral archetypes (e.g., "Warlike" for bold+greedy characters who bank gold into a war chest) that shape spending/war-initiation patterns beyond the raw scoring formula. *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*

### 1.10 Dread / intimidation (a second scalar overriding opinion)

- **Dread vs. Boldness threshold gates.** A character becomes Intimidated when an actor's Dread exceeds their Boldness by a fixed margin (20), Terrified at a larger margin (45) — this includes negative Boldness (a Craven character is terrified of nearly everyone). Intimidated/Terrified give flat acceptance bonuses that bypass negative opinion entirely, and a terrified character can't join factions unless discontent is already rising. Dread drifts monthly toward a trait-derived baseline. *ck-opinion-decay-and-threshold-tables.md*

### 1.11 Event-engine architecture (design/failure-mode lessons, not a single mechanic)

- **MTTH (mean-time-to-happen) vs. `on_action` event-driven hooks** as CK's two structural event-triggering paradigms — continuous probabilistic polling vs. firing strictly on state-change. *ck-postmortems-and-design-lessons.md, ck-failure-modes-and-reactivity-loop.md*
- **"Opinion-modifier soup"** — CK2's documented failure mode where uncapped additive modifier stacking becomes an unreadable amalgam; CK3's fix was stance vectors + memory flags, not more numeric precision. *ck-postmortems-and-design-lessons.md, ck-failure-modes-and-reactivity-loop.md*
- **Sculptural Fiction vs. Generative Social Simulation** (Emily Short's framework): pre-authored storylet decks that get "carved away" by state as a narrative engine, vs. continuously-computed agent interactions from raw equations. Modern consensus favors a hybrid: continuous state simulation underneath, surfaced through a sculptural storylet layer at key thresholds. *ck-postmortems-and-design-lessons.md*
- **Emergence-detection / pattern-recognition thesis** — players infer coherent narrative from a persistence-linked chain of otherwise-unrelated algorithmic events (a vassal's child dies → the vassal later joins a faction → the player infers "grief-driven vengeance") — requires events to leave persistent, readable records for later decisions to cite. *ck-postmortems-and-design-lessons.md*

### 1.12 CK's genre neighbors (covered inside the same CK research files)

- **King of Dragon Pass / Six Ages** — social state tracked at the *clan* level (ancestral favor, advisor-team traits), not per-character; action execution is storylet selection during seasonal phases; legibility delivered entirely diegetically via an in-fiction Ring of Advisors offering contextual predictions; designer doctrine: author choices as value trade-offs with "no obviously best answer," not optimal-answer puzzles (immunizes events against being solved/farmed). *ck-postmortems-and-design-lessons.md, ck-failure-modes-and-reactivity-loop.md*
- **Wildermyth** — character-pair relationship bindings (Rivals/Lovers/Friends) plus emergent physical-transformation tags (mummified, fire-infused); a dynamic contextual-casting trigger engine searches the active party for characters whose history/traits/relationship level match a storylet's exact preconditions; legibility via a visual comic-panel interface that explicitly surfaces motivation; explicitly *discarded* a deeper simulationist overland map (growing/shrinking populations, dynamic threats) because "simulation depth that players cannot read is wasted depth." *ck-postmortems-and-design-lessons.md, ck-failure-modes-and-reactivity-loop.md, ai-directors-and-drama-management-v4.md*
- **Total War (Three Kingdoms/Attila)** — scalar Loyalty/Satisfaction meters with named trait-affinity sources (guanxi); hard-threshold-triggered defection (with the defector's *region*) and civil war; failure mode: decisive variables (inter-vassal relations) were invisible to the player, producing "green but seceded" mass-defection incidents; the character-driven design direction was itself abandoned commercially after Three Kingdoms' early cancellation. *ck-postmortems-and-design-lessons.md, ck-failure-modes-and-reactivity-loop.md*
- **Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord** — a single scalar −100..+100 relationship per lord with documented per-action deltas (rescue a prisoner +8) and named bands (indifferent→vengeful), but almost no NPC-initiated action reads the score and no provenance/decay design — cited as the "control group" proving a legible number alone, with no threshold→action catalog and no presentation layer, produces no social fantasy. *ck-postmortems-and-design-lessons.md, ck-failure-modes-and-reactivity-loop.md*
- **The "Twilight Bazaar" thesis (Aalto University)** — an independently-built-and-failed miniature of CK's lessons: (1) an OCEAN/Big-Five personality model failed playtest because its detail never surfaced behaviorally, replaced by a smaller, more expressive discrete-trait pool; (2) "bargaining chips" — social knowledge represented as discrete, presentable, lie-capable inventory *objects* rather than hidden NPC state, built because players "forgot what they knew about whom" when knowledge was invisible; (3) the interaction *verb* itself frames interpretation — a "Gossip" verb prompted a player to construct a coherent backstory from a state change, an otherwise-identical "Socialise" verb produced nothing. *ck-failure-modes-and-reactivity-loop.md*

## 2. Kenshi

### 2.1 World states / town overrides (the headline collective mechanic)

- **Boolean world-state flags driving two output channels only: town overrides and homeless (wilderness) spawns.** Nothing more granular exists in vanilla. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Single-leader vs. multi-leader collapse patterns.** Small factions are "one-switch": killing/imprisoning the sole named leader collapses squad spawns and can flip the hometown to Destroyed/taken-over. Large factions are multi-switch networks requiring multiple town-boss kills, with documented *ripple* logic beyond the leader's own hometown (destroying supply-chain towns like slave farms degrades a still-led capital; killing a strong defensive town's boss lets neighboring towns fall to invaders even though those towns' own bosses live). *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Kill/imprison equivalence, and release-rollback.** Where a condition needs a character "dead," imprisonment satisfies it; where it needs them "alive," it means alive-*and*-free (caged doesn't count). Releasing a captured, world-state-linked leader rolls the flag back and can revert town overrides — deliberate support for rescue-mission narratives. A few overrides require the capture-then-release *sequence itself* to persist, only flipping on a second visit. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **A small fixed override vocabulary, applied as a whole-record swap.** Malnourished, Half-destroyed, Civil War (internally = Malnourished+Half-destroyed for one town), Destroyed/Ruins, Prosperous, or a faction name (full takeover) — each swaps the entire town record: faction, resident roster, bar squads, building states, vendor tables. "Prosperous" is explicitly relative, not absolute (can still be worse than default in absolute terms). *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Priority-ranked override chains.** When multiple overrides' conditions are satisfied, the one gated by the *most* world states wins (ties break to list order); some towns chain through up to five ranked owners depending on the global alive/free vector of ~40 named characters. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **The swap only executes while the town is unloaded** (old town despawns, new one spawns) — nothing changes visibly if the player is present; the game silently waits for the player to leave. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Player-owned buildings are collateral damage** in an overridden town (contested in the wiki record, but broadly true) — a documented, unresolved pain point. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Faction headquarters can relocate** to a newly-acquired town. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **"Broken squad" degradation instead of faction deletion.** Beaten factions rarely disappear entirely — they persist as weaker remnant spawns (a leaderless Holy Nation spawns weaker "Strayed Paladins"; leaderless United Cities spawns "Samurai Rogue" bandits). Only a handful of minor factions can be reduced to true zero. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Power vacuums grow a rival faction's footprint.** Killing one faction's regional leader can cause a *neighboring, unrelated* faction's homeless spawns to colonize the vacated zones — one faction's collapse mechanically growing another's presence. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Zone-level spawn-table retuning**, independent of town overrides: a leader's death/capture shifts which squads spawn in which wilderness zones (a global spawn-table modifier keyed on the alive/free vector of named characters), not just a town-level swap. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **The system is the most-modded layer in the whole game**, with two rival community overhaul philosophies (Reactive World: many new triggers/factions, criticized for fragility from too many micro-leaders; Living World: fewer, restorative-direction changes, praised as "simple and effective") — direct evidence that "more world-state branches" is not automatically better; each branch must be discoverable and completable. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*

### 2.2 Faction relations scalar

- **A −100..+100 per-faction scalar with exactly two named thresholds, no intermediate bands.** −30 = hostile-on-sight (whole faction attacks); +50 = allied (a bundled behavior package: gate guards stop searching you, members gift food/medicine, assist in combat, bandage downed player characters, ignore your bounties except crimes they personally witness, and may send troops to defend your outpost). Between the thresholds: neutral. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Witnessed crime overrides the scalar entirely** — anyone who personally witnesses a crime against their faction/allies may attack even at alliance-level relations; the scalar gates *default* disposition only. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **A documented table of relation-changing actions** with concrete deltas: attacking members (scaled by target rank), imprisoning+interrogating (−1), a torture-device act (scaled to victim's standing), attacking a "Diplomat"-flagged plot-important NPC (severe drop, near-immune to ranged targeting, bodyguards retaliate one at a time), healing wounded (no witness needed, scaled to target importance, hard-capped so healing alone can never reach alliance), turning in a bounty (flat +2, more with specific factions), buying a slave's freedom (+4, repeatable at real cost), selling into slavery (+1–2 with slaver factions), and "Pacifiers" — a cash-for-relations NPC service with banded fees and a cooldown, who can refuse service (with a stated reason) if you've allied their enemy or hurt their leadership. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **No passive decay of faction relations** (community consensus; contrasts with bounty decay, a separate mechanism). *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Alliance-procedure knock-on costs.** Joining one faction via its leader typically carries an itemized relations penalty with named rival factions (e.g., joining the Flotsam Ninjas costs −75 with the Holy Nation) — allying is not free, and turning in a hostile faction's leader-bounty is generally an outright, irreversible break with that faction. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*

### 2.3 Crime, bounty, and delegated legal system

- **Crime is strictly non-telepathic**, gated by line-of-sight witness triggers in the event pipeline (`EV_WITNESS_GENERIC_ASSAULT`, `EV_WITNESS_THIEF_OR_LOCKPICK`, `EV_THIEF_CAUGHT_STEALING_FROM_ME`), which raise a local alarm. *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md*
- **A full published crime-severity table** (Trespassing, Looting, Assault, Burglary, Theft, Escaping prison, Terrorism, Kidnapping), each with its own expiry time, jail sentence (half the expiry), and bounty value. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Linear bounty-expiry formula** — a statute of limitations scaled directly to severity (roughly 4 hours of real-time expiry per ~100 bounty). A notoriety threshold (~10,000) makes a bounty never expire on its own; above a higher threshold only paying bail (2× the bounty) clears it. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Recognition scales with bounty size** — police/bounty-hunters spot wanted characters fastest, ordinary civilians only recognize the most infamous, and recognition itself takes time (a wanted character can dash through town before being spotted). *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Prison converts to slavery in pro-slavery jurisdictions** — a caged character can be enslaved and shipped to a faction's slave mines before their sentence completes; slaver NPCs will attempt to enslave *any* unconscious non-allied character they encounter, independent of any bounty. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Delegated legal systems** — a faction's data record can point its "Legal System" at another faction; every faction sharing that pointer shares one wanted-list namespace (crimes against a minor faction accrue as a bounty with the major umbrella faction, but only umbrella-faction settlements have police infrastructure to actually enforce arrest — the local minor faction just attacks). *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md, spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md*
- **Only specific occupation-typed characters can issue a bounty** (military/law-enforcement roles of a real, non-pseudo faction) — a documented non-functional per-character flag exists in the data as a red herring. *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*

### 2.4 AI packages / squad data model (the "NPCs act from state" substrate)

- **Squads** (Leader, Dialogue Leader/Squad, Faction, AI Packages, randomized roster lists), **AI Packages** (prioritized action lists with Signal Functions determining package end/target, plus an explicit "Unloaded Func" behavior path for off-screen squads), and **Dialogue Packages** (grouped by event trigger, with line-inheritance). *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md, spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **AI contracts** — dialogue-issued temporary package overrides (e.g., an allied NPC temporarily joining the player's squad on request). *kenshi-world-states-factions-and-legal-systems.md*
- **Off-screen simulation is fully suspended, not abstracted-and-continued.** Unloaded chunks freeze physics/pathfinding/combat entirely; world progression is faked purely via the boolean world-state/override system, not by any continued off-screen simulation of movement or combat. *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md, spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **A shallow, dialogue-boolean model of "the NPC remembers you."** `DA_Remember_Character` is a flag ("has spoken with a player character before"), attached to the whole *player faction*, not a rich per-encounter record; a documented dialogue/AI hybrid update added situational memory of past deeds plus insult-then-ambush thug behavior, still lightweight. *spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*

## 3. Shadows of Doubt

- **A 23-field Citizen Profile per NPC** (name — the single load-bearing field — plus blood type, shoe size, height, address, job, etc., each independently discoverable/vague). *spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **Static/dynamic/relational citizen data**: unique ID, procedurally generated voxel appearance, blood type/DNA/fingerprint hash (static); financial metrics, health, home/work pointers, daily schedule buffer (dynamic); an explicit social-network graph of spouses/roommates/colleagues/acquaintances (relational). *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md*
- **Deterministic physical/digital trace instantiation** on ordinary action: a purchase spawns a physical receipt object *and* a merchant digital-ledger entry; phone calls log to building switchboards (origin/destination/timestamp); footprints and fingerprints are spatially left and hash-linked to the actor. *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md*
- **A periodic global visibility-check sighting loop.** Traveling citizens are checked pairwise for line-of-sight; on a positive check, a sighting record (observed UUID, location, timestamp, a suspicious-vs-normal behavior flag) is appended to the observer's memory array. *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md*
- **Three-phase memory decay: Precise → Fuzzy → Purged.** Precise: exact identity/location/time. Fuzzy: identity degrades to a broad physical descriptor, exact time becomes a window. Purged: the memory is garbage-collected and can't surface in questioning. Decay rate is a function of witness familiarity with the subject, subject visual distinctiveness, witness alertness/age, and situational suspicion; time-accuracy degrades before identity does. *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md, spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **A "Facts" provenance graph with reliability-weighted edges.** Every discovered thing (citizen, address, object) has a case-file; "Facts" are distinct link objects connecting two-or-more evidence folders, each carrying a reliability value that limits how much "incrimination" can flow across it in a propagation pass — visualized as strings on a corkboard (redness = incrimination transferred, width = reliability, animation = direction). *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md, spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **Fingerprint/name resolution chain** — prints are lifted from weapons/surfaces/footprints and tied to an identity via employee records, the City Hall citizen database, direct questioning, or forcible printing of an unconscious/handcuffed citizen; no separate print database exists, the player builds their own via a notebook. *spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **Per-citizen alibi timelines**, plotted as walked routes on a map; two citizens' timelines can be overlaid to surface contradictions. *spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **Citizens can lie.** A citizen whose true presence looks incriminating may deliberately lie or bend the truth even when innocent (an explicitly under-explored mechanic, per the developer). *spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **Press-appeal mechanic** — publicizing a case can prompt witnesses to proactively come forward (a resource-cost lever that raises the chance). *spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **Batch-precompute-then-deviate scheduling.** Full daily routines for hundreds of citizens are computed in a 10–15 second batch before the day starts (not live), with only citizens actively deviating (reacting to a body, an alarm) computed in real time. The murder itself is pre-simulated at ~60× speed as the city loads. *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md, spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **Body-discovery failsafes, not emergent reliability.** Layered guarantees (signs of forced entry, a scream, eventually the smell of decomposition) ensure a body is *always* eventually found rather than relying purely on emergent witness behavior. *spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **The "Case Generator" as a provenance-anchored intervention pattern** (read as a director/GM mechanic, distinct from the witness system above): a killer is selected by archetype (Corporate Cutthroat, Dating Service Stalker, Apartment Sniper), then *actually executes the crime using in-engine systems against real simulated relationships* — a Corporate Cutthroat's evidence (business card, graffiti) targets a real career rival pulled from the simulated employment graph; an Apartment Sniper's bullet trajectory originates from an apartment they actually occupy. No clue is ever spawned disconnected from actual simulation state. *ai-directors-and-drama-management.md*

## 4. The Nemesis System (Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor/War)

- **Procedurally generated enemies from a base template** ("primordial goo"): randomized appearance, vocal library, personality archetype (The Friendly, The Assassin, Man-Eater), and mechanical strength/weakness traits (e.g., fear of fire, stealth immunity). *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md, spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **Discrete combat-outcome "verbs" that mutate persistent state.** Player Death → the surviving orc gains power, is promoted, and taunts the player next encounter. Player Flight → the orc logs cowardice, gains confidence, gains mocking dialogue. Severe Injury/near-death → a probabilistic "cheat death" roll lets the orc return later with procedural physical scarring (iron plates, burn scars, a peg leg) and dialogue referencing the specific defeat method. *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md*
- **Combinatorial dialogue selection**, not flat strings: audio fragments are chosen filtered by the target's personality archetype, current rank, past encounter outcome, and current physical condition. *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md*
- **A ranked hierarchy (grunts → captains → warchiefs → overlords) with automated vacancy-filling.** A vacancy from a player-caused death (or an inter-orc rivalry) triggers automatic promotion; an unnamed grunt who kills the player is granted a unique name/attributes and promoted to Captain on the spot. *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md*
- **Autonomous background hierarchy events**, independent of player action: Duels (rank contests), Hunts (an orc gains combat ability from beating a wild beast), Feasts (recruiting followers/power), Executions (a higher-rank orc attempts to execute a rival). *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md*
- **Domination, followers, and betrayal (Shadow of War).** The player can dominate orcs into followers/spies and place them in the hierarchy; a dominated orc who attacks/loses their Blood Brother triggers relationship parameters inverting to maximum hostility and a dynamic revenge-ambush event that invades the player's active space. Also: cross-player "Social Vendettas"/Social Conquest let one player's nemesis invade another's game and assault each other's forts. *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md, spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **Tuned probability curves for pacing.** Survival-of-lethal-blows probability scales inversely with the number of currently-tracked active rivalries (preventing rivalry saturation); ambush timers are throttled to prevent multiple nemesis orcs converging on the player simultaneously (preserving combat clarity). *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md*
- **The load-bearing simplification: only named captains/warchiefs carry persistent memory/rank; the masses are stateless fodder.** The "living world" feeling comes from a small stateful roster against a stateless backdrop, not universal simulation. *spatial-sim-shadows-of-doubt-nemesis-kenshi.md, spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **Documented feature-creep near-failure**: at one point every Uruk faction independently tracked separate Morale and Discipline bars, described internally as looking "like a Christmas tree"; the team cut back to the original "personal villains" core, keeping Domination. *spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*
- **Patent boundary (US 10,926,179 B2 family, not the initially-miscited patent).** What's protected: an avatar↔first-NPC interaction that automatically changes a *second*, different NPC's parameters, where that second NPC occupies a ranked hierarchy, and the change surfaces in that second NPC's dialogue/appearance — the specific *combination*, not any one piece alone. What's free: a single NPC remembering and reacting to its own encounters; per-observer reputation/grudges generally; standalone faction succession (vacancy-filling without the two-NPC linkage); "forts"/power-centers and cross-player vendetta-sharing are the narrowest, most encumbered edge. *spatial-sim-legal-boundaries-and-witness-propagation.md*

## 5. RimWorld

### 5.1 Mood / mental-break system

- **A mood-bar scalar with a per-pawn Mental Break Threshold stat** (default 35%, range 1–50%, modified additively by traits). Three escalating risk bands derived from the threshold: Minor (below threshold, ~10-day mean-time-to-break, "Stressed"), Major (below 4/7 of threshold, ~3-day MTB, "On edge"), Extreme (below 1/7 of threshold, ~0.7-day MTB, "About to break"). *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A documented weighted-random-within-band selection algorithm.** The intensity band is deterministic (how far below threshold); which specific break fires within that band is `TryRandomElementByWeight` over each break definition's `CommonalityFor(pawn)`, filtered by an eligibility check (`BreakCanOccur`). Traits gate eligibility and re-weight (Pyromaniac keeps "Fire starting spree" always eligible; Bloodlust biases toward violent breaks); situational gates apply too (a rage break is far less likely in a tiny colony, linearly ramping to normal likelihood by a population threshold; a Jailbreaker break requires a reachable prisoner). Non-player-faction pawns can only ever roll Extreme breaks. *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A large named break catalogue** spanning three intensities — Minor (Sad wander, Food binge, Hide in room, Insulting spree, Crying, Giggling), Major (Tantrum, Bedroom tantrum, Psychotic wandering, Sadistic rage, Corpse obsession, Insane ramblings), Extreme (Berserk, Fire starting spree, Murderous rage, Slaughterer, Jailbreaker, Catatonic breakdown, Run wild, Given up and leaving). *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Multi-channel telegraphing/legibility**: a threshold-marked mood bar, escalating-severity alerts with matching alert-sound intensity, a full itemized per-thought tooltip breakdown (each named cause with its exact numeric offset), and a visual state indicator during a break (name color + a lightning-bolt icon, colored by aggressiveness). *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Fixed recovery windows per break type**, endable early via arrest (chance-based, with a real social cost — a −8 "was imprisoned" thought on release instead of the normal catharsis), downing/beating (the only way to stop Berserk), a forced-calm psycast, or mod-added warden interventions scaled by a social skill. *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A named anti-spiral device: the "Catharsis" thought** — a large temporary mood bonus (+40 for 2.5 days, stackable higher, with a distinct variant for one DLC's mechanic) applied after a break resolves, explicitly designed to prevent a cascading spiral of repeated breakdowns. *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Design-pillar framing from the lead designer**: "not a game — a story generator," with the explicit corollary that mechanics must include *loss and recovery*, not just loss — mental breaks are the archetypal loss-then-recovery beat, and their legibility is treated as core to the "story generator" working at all. *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md, belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*

### 5.2 Opinion / social-thought system

- **Opinion is computed on demand, never stored** — a live sum of active social memory-thoughts + relationship base opinions + trait/beauty modifiers, recomputed on every query. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **`Thought_Memory` objects with an explicit duration and stack limit.** Each carries an age counter and a `durationDays`; by default it holds full effect for its whole duration then vanishes instantly (an optional field, `lerpOpinionToZeroAfterDurationPct`, enables a gradual linear taper instead, exposed to modding but not vanilla-default). Duplicate thoughts don't stack unboundedly — reapplication resets the existing thought's age ("renew") rather than adding a new copy, and a `stackLimit` caps count (trimming the oldest), with `stackedEffectMultiplier` giving diminishing returns per stacked copy. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A large published numeric table of social-thought values**: Insulted −15/20 days (stacks 10× at ×0.9 each), Kind words +15, Slighted −5, Deep talk +15, Harmed me −15/10 days, Botched my surgery −20/90 days, Rescued me +15/30 days, Recruited me +20/120 days; family/romance base opinions (spouse +30, lover +35, ex −15) plus large death-of-relation penalties (killed my spouse −65, killed parent/child/sibling −80); trait-based flat modifiers (Annoying voice −25, Abrasive ×2.3 insult chance, Psychopath nullifies certain penalties). *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Social fights as a probabilistic escalation from insults.** Every insult has a small chance to trigger a fight, itself randomly resolved as either cathartic (a large positive mood swing) or angering (a large negative one), each with its own duration. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*

### 5.3 Storyteller / world-event pacing director

- **Incidents sampled from probability tables on a mean-time-between (MTB) basis**, not simulated causally — the world doesn't simulate a raider band forming and marching, it rolls dice on a schedule and *then* generates a plausible-composition band. Different "Storyteller" personas have different MTB constants (a "Classic"-style storyteller: big threats ~3.75 days apart; "Randy Random": ~1.13 days with its own interval cap). *offscreen-entity-handoff-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A published raid-point formula gating both intensity and content type**: `(Wealth Points + Pawn Points) × Difficulty × Starting Factor × Adaptation Factor`, with wealth linearly interpolated across a wealth range, per-pawn point contribution scaled by colony wealth, and minimum-point *thresholds* gating specific raid types (mechanoids, sieges, sappers each need a different point floor) — a legibility feature disguised as balance, since the threat ladder becomes a learnable curve. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v4.md*
- **A documented, exploitable failure mode: wealth-gaming.** Because Threat Points scale off raw colony market value rather than actual defensive capability, players learn to deliberately destroy/hide/launder wealth (parking items in off-map caravans, letting weapons deteriorate to minimum value, capping "progress" wealth) to suppress raid intensity — a generalizable lesson that any input a director trusts as ground truth will be gamed unless it's either fully honest-and-visible or literally un-launderable by construction. *ai-directors-and-drama-management.md, ai-directors-and-drama-management-v4.md*

### 5.4 Off-screen entity persistence (WorldPawns model)

- **Object-identity preservation across the loaded/unloaded boundary.** An off-map pawn is the *same* object, parked in a `WorldPawns` collection (categorized Free/Dead/FactionLeader/Kidnapped/CaravanMember/InTransportPod/ForSaleBySettlement), not a regenerated summary — needs, health, relationships, and memories are never serialized-and-rebuilt. *offscreen-entity-handoff-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A "mothball" tier**: a pawn with no actively-changing condition (no bleeding wound, not traveling, not in a transport pod) is ticked only once per in-game day instead of continuously — lazy evaluation of health/needs, with a single catch-up tick applied right before the pawn re-enters play. *offscreen-entity-handoff-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Abstract per-day need accounting for caravans** (a fixed daily hunger-rate number, a skill-scaled daily forage rate) rather than per-meal simulation. *offscreen-entity-handoff-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A reachability-graph garbage collector (`WorldPawnGC`)** that marks "critical" pawns (faction leaders, kidnapped, caravan members, quest targets, anyone with a relation to an on-map pawn, anyone referenced in an active in-fiction "Tale") for preservation and discards the rest — with two documented, instructive failure modes: over-keeping (every minor character you ever interacted with lingers indefinitely, bloating saves) and under-pinning (a pawn not explicitly protected gets silently deleted, breaking sibling relationships that referenced it). *offscreen-entity-handoff-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **"Alibi generation" as the general pattern underlying all of this** (an academic/industry term, not RimWorld-specific): generate only the visible information up front, and retroactively generate an invisible backstory consistent with visible facts only when the player actually interacts — the explicit design goal being that a player cannot tell whether an NPC "was always there" or was just given a plausible history a moment ago. *offscreen-entity-handoff-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*

## 6. Dwarf Fortress

### 6.1 Stress / emotion → behavior mapping

- **Two hidden numeric stress axes**: short-term stress (correlates with a visible mood indicator, driven by individual emotions from fresh experiences or revisited long-term memories) and long-term stress (a slow accumulation of short-term stress, crossing named status thresholds — stressed, haggard, harrowed — that take years to rise and years to fall under normal conditions). Breakdowns require *both* axes to be elevated simultaneously. *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Personality facets modulating the stress mapping**: bravery (gain rate), stress vulnerability/capacity (breaking point), anxiety/calmness (dissipation rate), plus specific propensity traits for depression (→melancholy) and anger (→berserk). *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A branching outcome tree of temporary breakdowns** (Tantrum — throws objects, starts fights, destroys furniture; Depression — mopes, cancels jobs, repeats into Melancholy; Obliviousness — aimless wandering, repeats into Stark Raving Mad) and **permanent insanity flavors reached via prolonged high stress** (Melancholy — wanders, stops eating/drinking, dies of thirst/starvation; Stark Raving Mad — babbles, ignores needs, starves; Berserk — attacks friend and foe, becomes a civilization enemy, triggers traps; Catatonic — stares into space until death). *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Strange moods** — a separate, positive-flavored takeover independent of stress: a dwarf enters one of five named mood types, claims a workshop, evicts its current occupant, refuses all other labor, and hunts for specific materials; success produces a unique artifact and a large skill gain, failure (materials not found within a couple months) causes insanity and death. *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A true state-machine takeover, not a job-queue insertion.** The engine sets a distinct `mood_type`/breakdown-state enum on the unit that suppresses normal job-seeking entirely and overrides most external interruptions — mechanically different from merely adding a high-priority task. *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Pause-and-announce legibility**: the game pauses, centers the camera on the affected dwarf, and prints a colored, named announcement ("has gone stark raving mad!") — attributing a threshold crossing to a specific individual at a specific moment. *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Slow decay-to-baseline recovery**, driven by fulfilled needs (quality bedrooms, a legendary dining room, meaningful skill-increasing work, temple prayer, tavern socializing/drinking) and — the most-cited player trick — scheduled but barracks-less military training that drives idle dwarves to self-satisfy needs ("Therapy Squad"). Insanity states, once reached, are explicitly permanent and terminal. *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A documented historical failure cascade the designer deliberately dampened.** The pre-rewrite "unhappiness" model produced runaway tantrum spirals (a death → unhappy friends → tantrums → destroyed property → more deaths → more unhappy dwarves → berserk/melancholy, sometimes even suicide contaminating the water supply); the current dual-axis + years-long-accumulation + needs-based-recovery model was an explicit rewrite to slow and individualize breakdowns. *emotion-behavior-legibility-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*

### 6.2 Memory (fixed-slot buffer, distinct from RimWorld's recompute model)

- **A three-tier fixed-slot memory store per dwarf**: 8 short-term slots, 8 long-term slots, and a larger core-memory store, not a scalar and not an unbounded log. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Grouped, strongest-wins slot contention.** Memories are grouped by category; only the strongest emotion per group holds a slot. A new same-group thought replaces the existing one only if stronger; a new different-group thought fills an empty slot or *overwrites the weakest existing memory of any group*, even if that victim was objectively stronger — producing constant churn of the weakest slot, so effectively only 7 of 8 short-term memories have lasting influence. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Time-gated promotion between tiers.** A memory that survives roughly a year in short-term attempts promotion to long-term (again by the fill-empty/overwrite-weaker/overwrite-weakest rule); long-term memories are periodically "revisited" (each revisit re-applies a stress delta), and on revisit have a documented 1-in-3 chance of promoting further to core memory — which causes a *permanent personality-facet change* and is then removed from the long-term tier. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A two-tier world-vs-personal data split**: a global, unbounded `historical_event` log tracks canonical world history; only a small subset of entities ("historical figures") get full tracked histories, the rest are abstracted; a dwarf's *personal* memory is a small, lossy local buffer that merely references circumstances tied back to the global log. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*

### 6.3 Physical evidence / provenance objects

- **Engravings and slabs are generated from serialized links to real historical figures/events, not authored strings** — inspecting one (with the right mode/setting) surfaces a specific generated description naming the actual depicted figures and their relationship ("X is raising Y"). A documented weighting exists over which life-events get chosen for depiction (becoming a noble ranks above writing a book, which ranks above naming a weapon, etc.), and very recent events are disfavored. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Memorial slabs resolve ghosts without a body.** A slab engraved for a specific dead figure records their arrival/death (and killer, if any) and can lay a ghost to rest even with no recoverable corpse; deconstructing the slab can re-raise the ghost — the provenance link is bidirectional and persistent, not a one-time trigger. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Artifact descriptions pull from the creator's own preferences and their linked historical events**, generated and re-derivable at any time from stored links (proven by a documented bug where lost history references leave the description blank rather than a frozen stale string). Artifacts are described as "the ultimate expression of a dwarf's desires, fears, memories and hopes." From a later release, artifacts also *propagate*: they can be stolen, moved, warred over, and become political symbols; a "destroyed" artifact is actually just flagged Hidden and can respawn — effectively immortal provenance anchors. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*

### 6.4 Rumor system (secondhand knowledge, notably *without* content distortion)

- **Six discrete confidence tiers of secondhand knowledge** about a specific fact (e.g., an artifact's location): from direct possession/witnessing down through "heard someone had it recently," "heard someone has it (not where)," "generally known in legends-y fashion," to no knowledge at all. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **Time-stamped decay at four nested scopes simultaneously** (individual, site government, site culture, and civilization level), with detailed knowledge fading over weeks/years while a longer-term "reputation" effect persists after the specifics fade. *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*
- **A hard, explicitly-stated design rule: content is never distorted in transit** — the *only* sanctioned falsehood the rumor system produces is misattributed identity for figures with a secret identity (vampires, agents); everything else that spreads is true, just less detailed. Rumors propagate specifically when a knowing figure is "offloaded" (leaves active tracking); killing witnesses and hiding the body is the in-fiction lever to suppress a rumor's spread. Agents even store an asymmetric relationship record (the informant sees a source; the source sees only a friend). *belief-decay-and-physical-evidence-rimworld-dwarf-fortress.md*

## 7. AI directors / drama management (cross-game and academic layer)

### 7.1 Shipped director architectures

- **Façade's Beat Manager** — selects the next authored "beat" from a library based on discourse-act signals and a zero-sum tension/affinity score; documented failure modes: shallow character self-awareness (characters react to the beat manager, not to each other, since they lack real internal state), a beat-manager approach that only scales to a tightly confined single room (larger physical space explodes the coordination state space), an absence of "inciting incident" framing (pure reactivity drops players in with no sense of why anything matters now), and NLP reductionism (nuanced free-text input collapsed to one binary affinity score reads as jarring). *ai-directors-and-drama-management.md*
- **Left 4 Dead's Director** — a four-state pacing finite-state-machine (Build Up → Peak → Sustained Peak → Relax) driven by damage/health/mobility/incapacitation inputs, controlling zombie population caps and item-drop queues; state transitions are always multimodally telegraphed (ambient audio shift, a distinct musical stinger per threat type, character barks) *before* the intervention lands, so a difficulty spike never reads as untelegraphed. Documented exploits: players deliberately lingering in "safe zones" to manipulate the stress counter, and a stated design policy (punish players who rush ahead of the group) that community post-mortems document as failing to actually fire in practice — a "promised policy not implemented" gap distinct from an invisible-variable problem. *ai-directors-and-drama-management.md, ai-directors-and-drama-management-v4.md*
- **King of Dragon Pass's storylet engine** — a Prolog-style constraint-satisfaction match over faction/magic/season/clan-mood state, drawing from priority-banded storylets, presented as an advisor-commentary modal. *ai-directors-and-drama-management.md* (mechanics detailed further in §1.12 above)
- **PaSSAGE** (Thue et al., Neverwinter Nights) — player actions are pre-annotated with modifiers to a five-element playstyle vector (Fighter/Power Gamer/Tactician/Storyteller/Method Actor, after Robin Laws' RPG archetypes); the next encounter is selected by dot-product match between the tracked player vector and each candidate encounter's own annotation vector. Documented costs: authorial annotation overhead (every branch needs manual multi-axis tagging), vector drift (mechanically-motivated actions get misread as playstyle signal), and pacing flattening (pure preference-adaptation strips contrasting beats — a player who tests as a Fighter never sees quiet conversational content). *ai-directors-and-drama-management.md*
- **DeepMind Concordia's LLM Game Master loop** — agents emit natural-language *intent* strings; the GM validates each intent against grounded variables (physical presence, inventory, spatial bounds); on success it updates grounded state and synthesizes a natural-language "Event Statement" describing the outcome (on failure, it explains why); the Event Statement is pushed into nearby agents' memory logs. Core reusable principle: an LLM never directly mutates world state — it proposes, a deterministic validator accepts/rejects and performs the actual mutation, only the validator's outcome gets narrated. *ai-directors-and-drama-management.md*
- **RimWorld's Storyteller** — covered fully in §5.3 above; included here as one row of the "universal director pipeline" comparison.

### 7.2 Formal/academic drama-management lineages

- **Symbolic narrative planning (IPOCL and successors)** — classical STRIPS/PDDL-style search from an initial state to an authored goal state, with the specific innovation of forcing every individual character action to be motivated by that character's *own* goals (preventing "suicidal puppet" actions that only serve the global plot). Documented failure modes: combinatorial state explosion as agents/locations are added, precondition rigidity breaking under unscripted emergent player action, and heavy knowledge-engineering overhead — cited as the specific reason storylet/precondition-filtering systems won out commercially over full symbolic planning. *ai-directors-and-drama-management.md*
- **Declarative Optimization Drama Management (DODM)** — search-based drama management (Weyhrauch) re-applied to a different game (Anchorhead) and found *not to transfer*; the diagnosis was that the available intervention actions were "too weak to affect measured story quality" regardless of algorithm sophistication — an authorship-vocabulary problem, not an optimization problem. A later, richer-action re-implementation (Sharma et al.) *did* produce a measured, subject-noticed improvement, with the caveat that hints became actively frustrating when players couldn't act on them. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v2.md, ai-directors-and-drama-management-v4.md*
- **Targeted Trajectory Distribution MDPs** (Roberts et al.) — a director tuned to steer toward an author-specified *distribution* of possible experiences rather than a single "optimal" trajectory, explicitly to avoid a director that destroys player agency by forcing its own best story. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v2.md*
- **Plan-based mediation (Mimesis lineage, R. Michael Young)** — when a player does something the authored plan didn't anticipate, the mediator either *intervenes* (blocks/undoes the action — documented to make players "realize their behavior is limited" if repeated) or *accommodates* (replans so the action is absorbed). A later extension allows replanning *past* events to widen the accommodative search space, but strictly limited to aspects of the world the player hasn't already observed (maintains consistency). A related, sharper finding: when a reframe is unavoidable, revising the *world's* prior possibilities (retroactively establishing a hidden option existed) is empirically more believable than overriding the player's own just-taken action. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v2.md, ai-directors-and-drama-management-v3.md*
- **Daggerfall's actual quest format** — 227+ shipped quests gated by guild/social-class membership and a reputation rank (the same template means something different at different reputation levels); a documented binary-decompiled template format (resource declarations for Item/Person/Place/Clock/Foe plus startup/timer/condition-triggered task blocks); the open-source Daggerfall Unity reimplementation runs this as a `QuestMachine` — quests instantiated from text source at runtime, ticked at a fixed rate, serialized to save state, with community-registerable custom actions. Documented lesson: this "radiant content" works because templates are embedded in a reputation *economy*, not because the templates are individually clever. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v2.md*
- **A published storylet academic thread** — a quest engine (Breault et al.) that generates quests from world state *at generation time*, rated by human judges comparably to human-authored quests, with the property that the space of *possible* quests grows as the game progresses (richer world state yields richer quest material) — a direct endorsement of state-anchored generation scaling rather than exhausting a fixed template space. A companion mixed-initiative study (Questgram) found designers gave AI-assisted quest generation/suggestion tools a "mixed response" — useful as a creativity scaffold for human authors, not yet a validated fully-autonomous author. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v2.md*
- **Storylet role-casting**, named explicitly as the single highest-value transferable pattern across this whole literature: treat every generated situation as a King-of-Dragon-Pass-style storylet with precondition-gated *role slots*, and cast real, specific sifted entities (the actual grudge-holder, the actual target, the actual faction) into those roles — the specific mechanism that makes generated content read as authored rather than as a template. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v3.md*

### 7.3 LLM-narrator-specific failure/success record (recent, quantified)

- **AI Dungeon / Hidden Door consumer record** — AI Dungeon: characters "forgot who they were" within a few exchanges, NPCs changed gender mid-scene, a frozen enemy would attack again the very next turn identically, players had to hand-maintain a memory "pin" system described as "like learning a programming language"; memory drift remains the category's top complaint in recent reviews. Hidden Door (structured plot beats, explicit setup/payoff data, an engine layer tracking every character/item/location) still failed on *ungroundedness* — content ("does this chest have a trap?") is undetermined until presented, so player choices can retroactively rewrite the *present*, not just constrain the future, undermining a sense of autonomy even when players can't articulate why. Turn-cycle latency (documented 90–120 seconds per cycle in one system, 10–20 seconds with "thinking" disabled at a hallucination-rate cost) independently kills pacing. One team deliberately made their AI *dumber*, because NPCs that self-organize optimally "leave players with nothing to do." *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v2.md*
- **NCP-Bench** (2026 benchmark) — frames interactive narrative as long-horizon constraint satisfaction (Invariant/Ordering/Achievement constraints) under an adversarial player; published numbers: a top model's highest average interaction length (32.92 turns) still had only a 42% survival rate past 20 turns; fact conflicts occur in 40–68% of runs depending on model; fewer than 14% of narrative commitments are satisfied across all tested models; only ~3.5% of runs reach 100 turns with no explicit conflict. Root cause stated directly: LLMs lack explicit state-tracking and must implicitly reconstruct world state from dialogue history each turn, and "yield rather than maintain" established facts under pressure. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v3.md*
- **Orchestrated Reality / WorldLines** — formalizes an LLM game world as a Parameterized-Action POMDP where canonical state is a tree of JSON entities owned by one orchestrating agent; the LLM only observes a narrative projection and *proposes* changes through a Plan→Diff→Validate→Apply pipeline that commits schema-validated, content-hashed deltas — explicitly built to stop "unvalidated writes" (a model asserting any world change with no guard). Even its own authors note un-schematized details still drift (a tracked physical detail flipping between two states across a session). *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v3.md*
- **Neuro-symbolic TSL automata** (Temporal Stream Logic) — a correct-by-construction automaton, synthesized from a formal spec, decides which prompt modifier the LLM receives each turn, so long-horizon constraint enforcement lives in the automaton rather than the model; published result: 96%+ adherence for the automaton-gated approach vs. as low as ~15% for a pure-LLM approach on the same test set, with zero arithmetic errors vs. a third of runs erroring for the pure LLM. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v3.md*
- **Slice of Life** — pairs a deterministic, symbolically-represented social-simulation core (character actions chosen by summing weighted first-order-logic social considerations) with an LLM used *only* for surface-text realization of an already-decided outcome; explicit stated principle: keep simulation state deterministic and free of LLM influence to preserve authorial control, and never let dialogue feed back into simulation state. Cautionary finding: an over-engineered prompt granting the LLM more latitude *reintroduced* hallucination (inventing an unscripted fact) — the validation boundary must stay tight even where it looks safe to loosen. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v3.md*
- **Function-calling as a hard validity gate** (LLMaker) — the LLM emits structured function calls into a constraint-enforcing backend rather than free-form generated artifacts; measured result: function-calling never produced an invalid/domain-failing output, while free-form prompting on the same task never completed a test case without failing. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v3.md*
- **Drama Llama** (2025) — an LLM-powered storylets framework where human authors write only 3–4 natural-language "pivot point" triggers, and the LLM improvises freely *within* those bounds — explicitly positioned on the authorial-intent-vs-character-autonomy axis. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v2.md*
- **Symbolically Scaffolded Play** ("The Interview" study) — found no single prompt-design approach reliably improves play, that over-constraining actively diminishes improvisation, and that the working pattern is *role-differentiated* scaffolding: rigid symbolic structure for quest-giver-type roles, loose structure for open-ended/improvisational roles, with shared state persisted in a common structured (JSON) memory schema. *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v2.md*
- **Friends & Fables' public Franz-v1 → ACE-1 redesign** — the closest documented commercial analog to a belief-with-provenance model, reached independently through production pain. Franz v1 generated a response then updated state *from* the response, and failed exactly as theory predicts: long-campaign memory drift, a creature narrated as simultaneously dead and alive with no clean recovery path, "custom instructions" that could flavor text but couldn't impose mechanics or directly change state. The ACE-1 ("Agentic Campaign Engine") fix explicitly separates the AI Game Master ("the face") from the campaign engine ("the truth"), replaces hierarchical-summarization memory (which compressed away important details) with **atomic memory units** (single-fact records like "Neela offered 50 gold for the safe transport of her cat") ranked by type/recency/relevance, adds structured relationship scores, and ships a **"View Context" feature** letting players inspect exactly what the GM could see when it generated a given response. Their own stated diagnosis: "When an LLM lacks a source of truth, it's more prone to making things up." *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v4.md*

### 7.4 Named, general failure-mode taxonomy (synthesized across the whole AI-director literature)

A consolidated list of the ways a director/GM system breaks player trust, each independently documented in at least one shipped or academic system above: (1) **Visible railroading** — repeated nullification of a player's action. (2) **Ungrounded generation** — retroactively deciding world facts only at the moment they're presented, so player choices can rewrite the present as well as the future. (3) **Agency-destroying optimization** — a director that forces its own single "best" story/trajectory rather than a bounded range of outcomes. (4) **Memory inconsistency** — forgotten facts turning play into manual prompt/memory maintenance. (5) **Deceptive provenance** — generated content disguised as authored content, or content forced on the player without any opt-in. (6) **Static-world exposure** — sending the player back to a location that visibly hasn't changed, exposing that "the world" didn't actually update. (7) **Unsolvable/unrewarding generated content** — a completion or reward path that silently breaks. (8) **Promised policy not implemented** — a director's *stated*, legible rule that its actual runtime behavior doesn't match (a documentation/implementation gap, distinct from an invisible decisive variable). (9) **Simulation illegibility** — investing simulation depth (a growing/shrinking population, a dynamic threat map) that the presentation layer can never actually surface to the player, making the depth pure wasted cost. (10) **Perceived-vs-statistical fairness mismatch** — a system that is mathematically well-calibrated can still read as unfair if it violates what the player expected walking in (and, the mirror case, a genuinely fair fixed-seed system can read as *rigged* when a reload produces an identical outcome). *ai-directors-and-drama-management-v2.md, -v3.md, -v4.md*
