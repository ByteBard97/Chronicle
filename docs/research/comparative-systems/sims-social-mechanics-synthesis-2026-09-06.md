# The Sims: Synthesized Verdict (merging 3 independent reports — Claude, Gemini, Kimi)

Synthesis of three independent research passes on The Sims' social-simulation
mechanics (run from `notes/sims-social-simulation-research-prompt-2026-09-06.md`),
cross-checked rather than concatenated. Formatted to match this directory's
existing catalog style so it can sit alongside
`npc-social-mechanics-catalog-2026-09-06.md` as the Sims coverage that
catalog deliberately left out.

## 1. Verdict on the off-lot/background-simulation claim

**All three reports confirm the substance of the internal claim, but disagree on citation rigor — this is worth knowing before anyone cites it further.**

- **Kimi's report is the most rigorous and finds a real correction**: the "Richard Evans's AIIDE paper" attribution is wrong. No such paper exists under that description. The actual citable primary source for the verbatim "detailed social simulation was only performed on the characters in the current 'lot'" line is **Zubek, Horswill, Robison & Viglione, "Social Modeling via Logic Programming in City of Gangsters," AIIDE 2021** — which itself is quoting/characterizing Evans (2009). The same characterization also appears in Evans & Short's *Versu* paper (IEEE T-CIAIG 2014), giving it a genuine first-party fingerprint, but the correct citation for this project's library is Zubek et al. 2021, not a nonexistent Evans AIIDE paper.
- **Gemini's report treats an Evans AIIDE 2009 paper titled "AI Challenges in Sims 3" as directly retrievable** and cites it as primary, alongside Evans's GDC 2010 talk "Modeling Individual Personalities in The Sims 3" (real, on GDC Vault). It does not flag any citation problem.
- **Claude's report hedges in its caveats**: it says Evans's own verbatim words "could not be retrieved directly," and the LOD description rests on a secondary retelling (a Game Maker's Toolkit video) plus community/wiki corroboration — closer to Kimi's caution than Gemini's confidence, but doesn't chase down the actual correct citation the way Kimi did.

**Bottom line: use Kimi's citation (Zubek et al. 2021, AIIDE) as the primary source going forward, with Evans's GDC 2010 talk and the Versu paper as corroboration.** The underlying factual claim all three converge on is correct regardless of citation mess: **Sims 3's moment-to-moment social cognition (conversation resolution, relationship mutation) is scoped to Sims on the actively loaded lot; off-lot Sims get only a coarse, once-daily demographic event roll (Story Progression), not continuous social simulation.** This holds across Sims 1/2 (fully frozen off-lot, no abstraction at all) through Sims 4 (which launched in 2014 with *no* background progression at all, then retrofitted "Neighborhood Stories" in patches 121/125, 2021-2022).

## 2. Merged per-mechanic table

| Mechanic | Description | Titles | Corroboration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Directed scalar relationship pair (STR/LTR, or "daily"/"lifetime")** | Two floats per ordered pair (A→B independent of B→A): a fast-decaying short-term score and a slow-drifting long-term score that trails it. Discrete relationship levels (Friend, Best Friend, Enemy) are threshold projections of these scalars. | Sims 1 (from Hot Date expansion), Sims 2 | All three reports agree; exact decay constants differ slightly in phrasing but agree on shape (STR decays ~2/day, LTR normalizes toward STR periodically). |
| **Single consolidated relationship bar + named stages** | Sims 3 collapsed the dual scores to one 0-100 bar with named stages (Stranger→BFF, plus a separate romantic ladder Partner→Spouse); a lifetime reward ("Long Distance Friend") exists specifically to exempt a Sim from neglect-decay, confirming decay is otherwise unconditional. | Sims 3 | All three agree. |
| **Dual independent Friendship/Romance bars** | Sims 4 splits into two separately-decaying scalars, so max-friendship/zero-romance and zero-friendship/max-romance are both possible; combinatorial labels ("Bad Romance," "Total Opposites") are derived from the pair. | Sims 4 | All three agree. |
| **No provenance in the base scalar** | None of the four generations' core relationship number records *why* it holds that value — it is pure accumulation. This is the single most load-bearing negative finding for Chronicle: the franchise never solved "the number carries a reason." | All titles | Unanimous across all three reports — this is the strongest point of agreement in the whole batch besides Sentiments (below). |
| **Sims 2 Memories** | Typed, per-Sim, durable event records (Won a Fight, Got Married, Relative Died) with a `$Subject` slot referencing a second Sim, a positive/negative valence (sometimes aspiration-dependent), and salience decay that governs whether the memory still surfaces as a conversation topic. Visible in the Simology panel; editable via SimPE. | Sims 2 only | All three agree; Kimi and Gemini both note this is the closest the franchise gets to a structured event record with participants. |
| **Sims 2 Gossip propagation** | A Sim who witnesses a negative event forms an original memory; the "Gossip/Tell Secret" interaction packages it (event ID + subject ID) into an **invisible gossip token** copied into a listener's memory, re-transmittable further. Real secondhand information spread — the franchise's only instance of it. | Sims 2 only | All three agree it's real. **All three also independently flag the same weakness**: content is copied faithfully, never mutated or distorted in transit (unlike Chronicle's design goal); and — Claude and Kimi state this most explicitly — the gossip token is "semantically disconnected from transitive relationship evaluation": hearing that A wronged D does **not** cause the listener to adjust their own disposition toward A. It's conversational fuel, not belief update. Kimi's report additionally documents this caused real save-bloat/corruption bugs in vanilla (unbounded token cloning), fixed only by community tools (Pescado's Batbox, BoilingOil's NoSimLoaded). |
| **Sims 4 Sentiments (patch 103 / Snowy Escape, Nov 2020)** | Directed (asymmetric), named, cause-labeled, per-(Sim→Sim) states ("Festering Grudge," "Betrayed by Cheating," "Guilty" — a self-directed one for the offender) with short/long duration classes, a hard 4-slot cap per target (weakest evicted on overflow), and behavioral effects (proximity-triggered moodlets, biased autonomy). | Sims 4 only | **Unanimous, strongest convergence in the entire report set**: all three independently name this as the single closest shipped analogue to Chronicle's "belief with provenance" model, and all three independently note its two real limits versus Chronicle's ambition: it never propagates to third parties (strictly about the dyad that experienced the event) and it never mutates. |
| **Utility-AI / smart-object interaction selection** | Objects and other Sims continuously "advertise" motive-satisfaction scores; a Sim scores available interactions by need-deltas, traits, mood, relationship state, and distance, then picks stochastically among the top few (not a strict argmax) to avoid robotic optimality. This is the unbroken substrate since Sims 1. | All titles | All three agree in detail; Gemini gives the most formal treatment (an explicit scoring formula). |
| **Production-rule / precondition-gated interaction catalog** | Interactions are hand-authored units (behavior trees in Sims 1-2, tuned XML "mixers" in Sims 4) with hard preconditions (age, traits, relationship thresholds) and, in Sims 3, thousands of ranked production rules (input+conditions→output, most-specific wins, neutral fallback) resolving *how* an NPC reacts to a given joke/insult given traits and history. | Sims 3 particularly, general pattern across all | All three agree; this is the mechanic Claude explicitly recommends adapting as "a clean, moddable pattern for resolving how an NPC reacts given belief + disposition + mood." |
| **Short-Term Context (STC) conversational ladder** | A separate, fast-resetting per-conversation state (Boring→Okay→Friendly→Very Friendly on the friendly track; similar ladders for Romantic/Hostile) that gates escalation — e.g., even a high lifetime relationship can't unlock "Propose Marriage" without first climbing the STC ladder within the current conversation. Solves an "instant intimacy" problem. | Sims 3 | Claude and Gemini describe this in detail; Kimi covers the equivalent Sims 2 Furiosity state instead. All three agree some form of a fast/slow dual-timescale split recurs throughout the franchise. |
| **Witnessing via spatial "emitters," not line-of-sight/cognition** | An event (e.g., a kiss) instantiates an invisible radius-scanning object that checks nearby Sims and, for anyone with a Love/Crush bit toward either party, immediately flags an infidelity event and forms a memory — gated by room-portal boundaries (a solid wall with no door blocks it entirely; an open archway does not), not any belief-modeling. | Sims 2 (Gemini's report details this most; the others mention it more briefly) | Only Gemini gives this mechanical depth; Claude and Kimi mention witnessing exists but don't detail the emitter/portal-boundary mechanism. Treat as Gemini-sourced detail, not triple-corroborated. |
| **Chemistry/Attraction algorithm** | A computed compatibility function: personality-slider distance (same-side-of-median bonus), a hardcoded asymmetric 12×12 zodiac matrix, per-Sim Turn-On/Turn-Off matching, and a "repulsion override" that penalizes the total if either individual's one-sided attraction is very negative even when the average is positive. Displayed as −1 to 3 "Chemistry Bolts." | Sims 2: Nightlife | Claude and Gemini both cover this in detail (Gemini with exact formula shapes); revived in Sims 4 as "Attraction" (Lovestruck, 2024) and "Social Compatibility" (Growing Together, 2023) — a tiered (Amazing→Terrible) match on conversation-preference/characteristic bitmasks. |
| **Trait count discrepancy — flag, don't resolve silently** | Claude's report states Sims 3 shipped **63 base-game traits**, citing both a Sims 3 designer (Ray Mazza) and The Sims Wiki directly. Gemini's report states **~100 traits** in one table and **~80** elsewhere, citing an IGDA Japan SIG summary of Evans's GDC talk. These are real, uncorrected disagreements between two independently-sourced reports on the same factual question — worth a direct check against The Sims Wiki before using either number in a design doc. |
| **Autonomous NPC-to-NPC socializing — real but deliberately throttled** | On the active lot, background Sims genuinely chat/flirt/fight without player input via the same utility scoring. But romance-initiation is explicitly trait-gated (only Flirty/Hopeless Romantic Sims autonomously pursue romance in Sims 3) and Sims 4's initial vanilla tuning was so indiscriminate the opposite direction (random flirting wrecking scripted couples) that community mods ("Deeper Social Autonomy," MCCC's flirting toggles) exist specifically to rein it in. | All titles, most documented for Sims 3/4 | All three agree autonomy exists but is throttled/tuned, not a design lesson in "letting NPCs run free." |
| **Off-lot life-event engine (Story Progression / Neighborhood Stories)** | A scheduled, stochastic demographic manager that promotes/marries/impregnates/moves/kills unplayed Sims on coarse timers (hourly/daily), scored loosely by traits but **not** by actual social/relationship state in the vanilla implementation — documented failure mode: best friends randomly becoming rivals, an adult "befriending" an unrelated baby. Sims 4 launched (2014) with none at all; retrofitted 2021-2022 (patches 121, 125), and even then the 9 supported autonomous event types still exclude romance/marriage/divorce. | Sims 3 (native); Sims 4 (2021+, patches 121/125) | All three agree this is real, autonomous, and world-scale in scope — but explicitly **not** belief-level: it schedules life events, never propagates opinions or rumors. Unanimous framing across all three: this is "the world's vital statistics churn without you," not "the world thinks without you" (Kimi's phrasing, but the same substance appears independently in Claude's and Gemini's verdicts). |
| **NRaas StoryProgression (Sims 3) / MCCC (Sims 4) — community overhauls** | Modular manager architecture (separate Career/Money/Population/Relationship/Skill managers running on independent cycles, e.g. every 10-400 sim-minutes) replacing EA's flat demographic lottery with scenario-based, precondition-gated evaluation (e.g., "Consider Marriage" checks LTR threshold + trait compatibility + minimum courtship duration before firing) — richer, but still schedules discrete life events, never a rumor/opinion graph. | Community mods, Sims 3/4 | All three agree; Kimi's framing is sharpest: "even these maximal overhauls... schedule events, not beliefs. Nobody's story-progression mod propagates a rumor graph or computes an opinion." |
| **Clubs (Get Together, 2015)** | The one real "more than pairwise" group construct: up to 8 members, membership-eligibility rules, up to 5 encouraged + 5 discouraged behavior tags enforced during gatherings (temporarily overriding individual traits/norms), inter-club hostility settings. No persistent group state survives a gathering — no cohesion score, no memory of past meetings; a club feud is just two symmetric "be mean to" flags. | Sims 4 | All three agree on the mechanic and on its shallowness (no accumulated group state). |
| **Family Dynamics (Growing Together, 2023)** | Seven named, typed pairwise dyad tags between family members (Close/Distant/Supportive/Permissive/Difficult/Strict/Jokesters) biasing autonomous interaction mix — explicitly **symmetric by construction** (a parent and child cannot have differing perceptions of the same dynamic), and scoped only to kinship edges (in-laws excluded). | Sims 4 | All three agree; a real example of a group-flavored mechanic that's still architecturally just a typed pairwise tag, not genuine group state. |
| **Households as economic/spatial containers, not social units** | No family-level morale/reputation/disposition toward outsiders exists anywhere in the franchise; a fight between Sim A (household 1) and Sim B (household 2) updates only that one pairwise relationship — A's own spouse has zero systemic awareness unless physically present when the event's spatial emitter fired. | All titles | Unanimous. All three independently call this out as a hard ceiling the franchise never crossed — directly relevant since Chronicle needs faction/court-level state (Companions, Thieves Guild, Jarl's Court) that Sims never modeled. |

## 3. World-scale vs. player-scale classification

**Player-scale only (requires the cast loaded/co-present, foreground-only):** utility-AI interaction selection, the production-rule/STC conversational stack, moodlets/emotions, Clubs' peer-pressure override, chemistry/compatibility scoring, spatial-emitter witnessing, Family Dynamics tags, autonomous on-lot socializing.

**World-scale but explicitly not belief-level (evolves off-screen, but only as discrete demographic events with no opinion/rumor content):** Story Progression, Neighborhood Stories, NRaas, MCCC. All three reports converge hard on this being a real but shallow world-scale mechanism — useful as a *scheduling/tick-rate architecture pattern* for Chronicle's own off-screen tier, worthless as a model of *what* should get simulated off-screen.

**The two mechanics that carry genuine provenance/belief content — but neither is world-scale:** Sims 2's gossip-memory chain (propagates, but only among co-present Sims, no mutation, no transitive writeback into the listener's own disposition) and Sims 4 Sentiments (has real provenance and asymmetry, but is strictly dyad-local — never propagates to a third party at all).

**No mechanic in the entire franchise is both provenance-carrying and world-scale.** That gap — precisely the combination Chronicle is betting on — is confirmed empty by all three independent passes.

## 4. Final verdict

**The Sims is weak precedent for "the world reacts to the player and to independent events, autonomously, whether or not the player is watching."** All three reports reach this conclusion independently and explicitly, using nearly identical language (Kimi: "even at its most autonomous, a Sims world never *thinks* off-screen; it merely *happens*"; Claude: "it is evidence that a top-tier studio chose *not* to attempt it"; Gemini: "extremely weak precedent... the moment an agent leaves the active camera frustum, the engine abandons micro-social modeling entirely"). This is not a split decision — it's the strongest three-way convergence in the whole exercise.

**The single strongest individual mechanic worth adapting, unanimous across all three reports: The Sims 4 Sentiments system** (patch 103, Nov 2020) — a directed, named, cause-labeled, decaying, per-pair belief/grudge vocabulary that is close to a 1:1 design sketch of "belief with provenance," missing only propagation to third parties and mutation in transit (both of which Chronicle already intends to add). Second, also converged across all three: **Sims 2's gossip-memory chain**, valuable specifically as proof that a witness→token→person-to-person-transmission loop is implementable on old consumer hardware, with the explicit lesson to fix its two named flaws (no mutation, no transitive belief-update in the listener) rather than copy it as-is.

**One idea unique to a single report, worth surfacing even though uncorroborated:** Gemini's recommendation to model Skyrim factions/courts (Jarl's Court, Companions, Thieves Guild) as **Evans-style "Social Practices"** (deontic obligated/permitted/forbidden statuses that shift *all* bound members simultaneously when a triggering fact arrives — e.g., a murder rumor reaching Dragonsreach instantly flips the player's status to "Hostile Violator" across everyone in the room, rather than requiring N individual pairwise updates), drawing on Evans's real post-Sims academic work (Exclusion Logic, *Versu*) rather than on anything Sims itself shipped. This is architecturally distinct from anything in the six-game catalog already compiled and may be worth its own follow-up look at Evans's Exclusion Logic papers if Chronicle's faction-state design needs a group-level primitive.

**One new, more relevant precedent surfaced only by Kimi's report, not by the other two, and not yet in this project's research library at all:** academic systems built explicitly to exceed Sims 3's limitations — **Comme il Faut** (McCoy et al., AIIDE 2011, UC Santa Cruz) supports genuine third-party social reasoning ("x is cheating on y" inferable by an uninvolved z) and a shared social-facts database, both stated in its own paper as "completely missing from The Sims 3"; and **Zubek et al.'s City of Gangsters** (AIIDE 2021, the same paper that supplied the corrected off-lot citation above) runs logic-programming social inference with second-order propagation over ~1,200 NPCs. Both are named as closer commercial/academic precedent for Chronicle's actual goal than The Sims itself — **worth a dedicated follow-up research pass**, since none of the six games already in `docs/research/comparative-systems/` cover this specific academic lineage (Evans's Versu/Exclusion Logic work, Comme il Faut, City of Gangsters).
