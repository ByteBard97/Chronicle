## 2. Contested Picks (real, verified rating splits — not smoothed over)

- **Evans-style Social Practices** (group-level deontic status — obligated/permitted/forbidden — that flips for *all* bound members of a faction/court simultaneously on one triggering fact, e.g. a murder rumor reaching Dragonsreach flips the player's status across the whole room in one operation): all three rate the **idea** High, but split hard on **readiness**. Kimi ranks it #2 in its top-10 outright, no caveat: "the single most architecturally transformative addition for Chronicle." Grok rates it High but explicitly flags it "Gemini-only, uncorroborated single-report recommendation" and recommends "flag for follow-up research before committing... the interaction with per-individual provenance needs design." Claude similarly keeps it out of its own "build-first" top-5 specifically because it wants Evans's actual Exclusion Logic/Versu papers read first. **Net: 1-of-3 (Kimi) says build now; 2-of-3 (Grok, Claude) say verify against primary sources first, mirroring the exact discipline this project applied to Talk of the Town.** Given that discipline has already caught real gaps twice this project (HAMLET's empty repo, Talk of the Town's stripped-out belief code), the 2-of-3 caution is the better-supported position — recommend gap-research Prompt 2's Versu coverage settle this before treating Social Practices as ready-to-design.
- **STC (Short-Term Context) conversational ladder** (a fast-resetting per-conversation escalation state gating big asks — even a Sworn Friend can't get an enormous favor without first climbing greeting→rapport→openness rungs within the current conversation): Kimi rates High ("solves a real Skyrim dialogue problem"), Grok rates High ("prevents instant intimacy and makes every significant conversation a small minigame"), **Claude rates only Medium** ("conversation pacing that makes social wins feel earned; doubles as an LLM-behavior guardrail" — a real endorsement, just a notch below the other two). A minor, not load-bearing split — 2-of-3 High is the stronger signal.
- **Spatial-emitter witnessing gated by room portals** (an event like a kiss or a theft spawns an invisible radius-scanning trigger checked against room/portal boundaries — a solid wall blocks it, an open archway doesn't — as a cheap alternative to true line-of-sight raycasting): a genuine three-way spread. **Kimi rates it High** ("fixes Skyrim's telepathic crime system with a concrete, debuggable mechanism"). **Grok rates it Medium** ("useful alternative/complement to pure LOS"). **Claude rates it lowest, L–M**, and is the only one to flag it explicitly as sourced from a single upstream report (Gemini's original Sims-research contribution) rather than something all three original Sims researchers corroborated — "flagged single-source, but the technique stands on its own merits regardless of citation." Worth noting: this doesn't mean the *mechanic* itself is contested (all three rate it positive, none discard it) — it's specifically a disagreement about confidence/priority, tracking directly back to how many of the *original* three Sims-research reports actually detailed this mechanism (only Gemini's did, per that earlier synthesis).
- **Off-lot life-event engine (Story Progression / Neighborhood Stories) as an architecture pattern**: Kimi and Grok both independently land on **Medium, "architecture only"/"useful... but not for the 'world thinks' fantasy."** **Claude alone rates it High**, but specifically because Claude adds a fix on top of the base mechanic — "every off-screen event must cite the beliefs that motivated it, and arrives to the player as news with that provenance attached" — inverting the exact vanilla failure mode (best friends randomly becoming rivals for no visible reason) rather than adopting Story Progression's actual selection policy. This is the same pattern as Claude's gossip-fix elevation above: Claude consistently rates higher specifically where it can name a concrete fix to a named flaw, not where it's adopting a mechanic wholesale. Recommend keeping Kimi/Grok's caution about the *base* mechanic (schedule-only, not belief-level) while adopting Claude's specific "motivation-citing" rule as the concrete addition that changes the rating.
- **Autonomous NPC-to-NPC socializing** (background NPCs chat/flirt/fight via utility scoring, without player involvement, trait-gated to avoid Sims 4's launch-era "random flirting wrecking established couples" complaint): **Kimi rates High** ("makes taverns and streets feel alive with genuine social dynamics, not just idle animations"). **Claude and Grok both rate Medium** — Claude frames it as "doctrine with two independent shipped precedents; prevents Chronicle's most predictable community complaint," Grok as "world-feels-alive win when throttled correctly." A real 1-vs-2 split on enthusiasm, though direction (positive, ship-with-throttling) is unanimous.
- **Clubs (Get Together's temporary behavior-tag groups, no persistent group state)**: **Kimi and Grok both rate Low** ("shallow group construct; Chronicle needs lasting faction state" — near-identical phrasing independently). **Claude rates Medium**, salvaging the encouraged/discouraged behavior-tag idea specifically as faction-flavor texture ("Companions encourage brawl-settling, Thieves Guild discourages guard contact") while agreeing the group-*state* half is genuinely shallow and superseded by Chronicle's planned collective-state build. Net: 2-of-3 discard-leaning, with Claude finding one salvageable half.

## 3. Unique Standout Ideas Per Agent

- **Claude** — the pattern across Claude's whole pass is consistent and worth naming explicitly: **Claude rates highest wherever it can articulate a specific fix to a specific documented flaw**, rather than treating "adopt the Sims mechanic" as the win condition. This produced two ideas neither Kimi nor Grok isolated as their own standalone build items:
  1. **Belief-motivated off-screen life-event scheduler** — Story Progression's tick architecture, kept, but every event must cite the belief that motivated it (inverting Sims' own worst failure mode).
  2. **Kin-priority belief routing** — the "households are just economic containers" negative finding, inverted into a concrete build: household/kin members receive high-reliability beliefs about events involving their relatives on privileged, faster rumor edges. Kimi and Grok both treat the Households finding as gap-*confirmation* only (a design-doctrine validation); Claude is the only one to extract an actual new mechanic from it.
- **Kimi** — the most explicit at doing the cross-catalog equivalence move Fable pioneered for the six-game batch (spotting when two systems are "the same subsystem, build once"). Kimi's own §6 states it directly: "Sentiments is a more refined version of CK's 'named relationships' + RimWorld's 'thoughts'"; "Social Practices is the missing group-level primitive that CK's 'House relations' and Kenshi's 'faction scalar' both approach but don't formalize as a deontic logic system." This is valuable specifically because it tells a designer where *not* to build a third parallel system for something Chronicle's other research already covers.
- **Grok** — the most disciplined about flagging citation provenance mid-pass rather than treating every idea as equally well-sourced: it's the only one of the three to explicitly call out that Social Practices and the spatial-emitter witnessing technique each trace back to a *single* upstream report rather than corroborated-by-all-three-Sims-researchers, and to recommend research-before-commit specifically because of that. Also the only one of the three to independently reference the six-game batch's Nemesis-pacing-cap doctrine when discussing autonomous socializing throttling ("doctrine confirmation from a second lineage") — a genuine cross-thread citation, not just a same-document callback.
- **(Gemini, flagged low-confidence)** — despite the quality problems, its pass does correctly identify the same core items as the other three (Sentiments, gossip propagation and its transitive-writeback gap, STC, spatial emitters, off-lot scheduling, households-as-containers) with roughly the same verdicts, which is at least soft corroboration that the *content* judgment is sound even where the *format and focus* clearly broke down.

## 4. Merged Discard List

- **Single consolidated relationship bar** (Sims 3's collapse of the dual STR/LTR scores into one 0-100 bar) — all 3 rate Low, unanimous: "a documented design regression; nothing new to take" (Claude), and Kimi/Grok both independently note Sims 4 itself reverted this design. Consistent, resolved discard.
- **No provenance in the base scalar** — treated by all three as a confirmation of Chronicle's existing design direction, not a mechanic to build. Not a contest; just not actionable on its own.
- **Family Dynamics' symmetric-by-construction dyad tags** — all 3 skip/discard: "strictly dominated by directed beliefs... the interesting case is exactly the asymmetric one Sims forbids by construction" (Grok); Chronicle's existing crystallization design already supersedes it.
- **Trait-count discrepancy (63 vs. ~80-100)** — unanimous meta-flag only, not a mechanic; resolve against The Sims Wiki if ever needed, don't design against either number.
- **Households as economic/spatial containers** (the base finding, not Claude's kin-routing extension of it) — unanimous negative-lesson confirmation, not itself buildable.

## 6. Final Take

The strongest, best-corroborated result from this pass is that **Sims 4 Sentiments and the gossip-transitive-writeback fix are both ready to fold directly into Chronicle's core belief design**, independently confirmed by three separately-run agents with near-identical reasoning — as strong a signal as this project's research process produces. The **Production-rule reaction layer** is the single most valuable genuinely-new architectural idea this batch surfaced, filling a real gap (nothing currently sits between "belief state exists" and "LLM renders a line") that neither the six-game catalog nor prior Chronicle design docs named explicitly. **Evans-style Social Practices** is the most exciting single new idea in the batch by all three agents' own account, but is exactly the kind of claim this project has twice already found didn't survive primary-source contact (HAMLET, and — though ultimately vindicated — Talk of the Town) — hold it pending the Versu/Exclusion Logic research (gap-research Prompt 2) rather than designing against it now.
