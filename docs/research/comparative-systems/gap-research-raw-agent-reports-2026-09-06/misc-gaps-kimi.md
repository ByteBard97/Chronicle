# Comparative-Systems Gap Research — Prompt 4: Remaining Smaller Gaps in Already-Covered Games

*Research date: 2026-09-07. Companion to `docs/research/comparative-systems/npc-social-mechanics-catalog-2026-09-06.md`. Covers the six flagged sub-items; each section reports the concrete mechanic with citations and closes with a one-sentence catalog verdict.*

## Executive Summary

All six items resolved with citable mechanics. The two highest-value findings for the project are the **CK3 Struggle system** — which turned out to be an exact, documented fit for the intended "Skyrim civil war as multi-phase conflict" template, with phase names and catalyst values confirmed against the official wiki and Paradox's own dev diary — and the **Watch Dogs: Legion Census system**, where Ubisoft's own developers confirm the grievance chain is assembled **at runtime from a per-character generated profile**, not drawn from a pre-simulated social graph. The Dwarf Fortress **loyalty cascade** is confirmed as a real, documented failure mode of faction-membership bookkeeping (Toady One's own "civil war bug"), directly relevant as a cautionary case for any forced-compliance design.

## 1. Crusader Kings III — The Legends System (Legends of the Dead)

### 1.1 Structure: seeds, protagonists, types, and quality tiers

A legend in CK3 is a first-class narrative object with a named **protagonist** (the creating ruler or a family member, living or dead, aged 16+), an **Owner**, and optionally **Promoters** — other rulers who have adopted the legend and help spread it ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Character)). Legends are instantiated from **Legend Seeds**, which are acquired primarily through a Court Chronicler with high Aptitude, through deeds (winning a great holy war, killing the animal in a Legendary Hunt, completing a Pilgrimage, etc.), or through dynasty membership — including a large set of one-per-campaign **historical seeds** restricted to specific dynasties, cultures, or faiths ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Character)). Creation costs **200/300/400 gold** by rank (Count/Duke, King, Emperor) plus a monthly maintenance fee for as long as the legend is being promoted, and a legend must exist for at least **5 years** before it can be completed or abandoned ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/crusader-kings-3-ck3-how-spread-legends-guide/)).

Legends come in **three types — Heroic, Holy, Legitimizing** — crossed with **three quality tiers — Famed, Illustrious, Mythical** ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Character)). Paradox's own design diary frames the feature explicitly as medieval myth-making: rulers commissioning Arthurian-style legitimizing narratives about themselves or their ancestors, citing Geoffrey of Monmouth's *Historia Regum Britanniae* and Jacques Le Goff's "race for historic-mythical sponsorship" as the historical model ([Paradox Dev Diary #145](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?feed=steam_community_announcements&appids=1158310&enddate=1712133013)). Notably, the legend's narrative text itself is **written through a chain of events as the legend is being completed** — the "story of the legend will be written in various events" ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Character)) — so the content is assembled during play rather than declared at creation.

### 1.2 How spread is actually modeled

Spread is **not a scripted radius** and not a full adjacency-diffusion simulation with per-tick flux; it is a **chance-based per-barony acquisition process gated by distance**, steered through a player-tunable **Legend Spread Chance** stat. Each barony independently rolls to acquire the legend, with the base chance modulated by local factors — community testing points to county **development level and geographic connectivity** (e.g., rivers) as inputs, and baronies beyond a distance threshold are simply ineligible ("too distant") ([Paradox Forums](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/what-determines-base-legend-spread-chance.1832182/), [r/CrusaderKings](https://www.reddit.com/r/CrusaderKings/comments/1pi59sn/legend_not_spreading/)). The player raises spread chance through legend events (whose success odds scale with the Chronicler's Aptitude), through the court-position tasks **Extol Domestic Legend** and **Commend Legend Abroad** for Chroniclers, Musicians, and Poets, through the **Spread Legend intent** at activities such as grand tours and tournaments, and by convincing other rulers — via opinion, bribes, or hooks — to become Promoters ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/crusader-kings-3-ck3-how-spread-legends-guide/)).

Quality tiers are **gated purely by cumulative geographic spread**: a legend starts at **Famed**, stops spreading at **100 baronies** until paid-upgraded to **Illustrious**, and stops again at **300 baronies** until upgraded to **Mythical** ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Character)). The design is thus a resource-conversion loop — gold and prestige are converted, via spread, into tier — rather than an autonomous diffusion process. This is consistent with how Paradox described the system's genesis: plagues were the free feature, and legends were deliberately built as a second, player-driven thing "that could spread across the map" ([Paradox Dev Diary #161](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/dev-diary-161-2024-in-review.1720433/page-11)).

![CK3 legend quality tiers gated by barony spread](assets/ck3_legend_tiers.png)

### 1.3 Mechanical effects beyond flavor

Spread legends confer real, tiered mechanical payoffs in three channels. **Owner** bonuses scale by type and tier — e.g., a Mythical Heroic legend grants **+25% Prestige** and **+300 Legitimacy**, while a Mythical Legitimizing legend grants **+0.50 monthly Renown, +600 Legitimacy, and an Unpressed Claim on every title de jure of the protagonist's primary empire title** ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Character)). **Promoter** bonuses give attribute points and income-scaled gains (e.g., +4 Martial and +10% Prestige at Mythical Heroic), which is what makes AI rulers rationally willing to adopt someone else's legend ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Character)).

Most relevant to a social-simulation reading, **every county the legend has spread to** receives a standing modifier while the legend is active — +5 to +20 Popular Opinion, plus type-specific effects such as Development Growth (+1%/+2%/+6% for Legitimizing) or Monthly Control (+0.10/+0.25 for Holy) — and **a single county can stack bonuses from multiple legends** ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Character)). Completing the legend converts the ephemeral spread into permanent rewards (legendary buildings, decisions such as Launch a Legendary Adventure, claims) while ending the owner/promoter upkeep bonuses ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Character)). So the system models "reputation as territory": narrative penetration of a region has a direct, enumerable payoff in that region.

**Catalog verdict:** this is a natural extension of existing rumor/propagation coverage — a *deliberately funded and promoted* meme with an explicit spread stat and per-region payoff — rather than a genuinely new mechanic; its transferable novelty is the economic gating (maintenance cost → spread → tier) and the county-stacking payoff structure.

## 2. Crusader Kings III — The Struggle/Phase System

### 2.1 Exact phase names and transition structure

The Struggle system (introduced with Fate of Iberia, reused for the Iranian Intermezzo in Legacy of Persia) models a regional conflict as a **cyclic state machine over four named phases: Opportunity, Hostility, Conciliation, and Compromise** ([Paradox Dev Diary #94](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/crusader-kings-3-dev-diary-94-anatomy-of-a-struggle.1522115/), [CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Struggles)). (One widely read guide misprints the list as "Hospitality, Opportunity, Compromise, Conciliation" ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/crusader-kings-3-fate-of-iberia-complete-guide-iberian-struggle-andalusia-asturias/)); the correct name is **Hostility**, per Paradox's dev diary and the game data.) The Iberian Struggle always starts in **Opportunity**; from Opportunity it can move to *either* Hostility *or* Conciliation — whichever of two competing catalyst tracks reaches **1,000 points** first — and both Hostility and Conciliation lead to **Compromise**, which in turn leads back to Opportunity ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Struggles), [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/crusader-kings-3-fate-of-iberia-complete-guide-iberian-struggle-andalusia-asturias/)).

Transitions are driven by **Catalysts**: a per-phase table that assigns point values to specific character actions performed by **Involved** characters (and, with reduced effect, **Interlopers** — outsiders with claims or alliances in the region) ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Struggles)). During Opportunity, for example, breaking a truce with an Involved character adds **+25** toward Hostility, exposing a secret or becoming a rival/nemesis adds **+10**, a kill or forced conversion adds **+5**, and there is always **+1 yearly drift**; the mirrored conciliatory track awards +25 for releasing an Involved prisoner unconditionally, +10 for granting independence or converting to the local culture/faith, down to +3 for gifts and alliances ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Struggles)). The key structural property: **phase transitions are an aggregate of every actor's behavior in the region**, not a script or a single player's decision — the system reads the character-simulation layer (wars, hooks, murders, friendships, marriages) as its input alphabet.

![CK3 Iberian Struggle phase cycle](assets/ck3_struggle_cycle.png)

### 2.2 Phase-gated action spaces and endings

Each phase meaningfully rewrites the rules for everyone in the region — this is the property the project cares about as a civil-war template. **Hostility** enables the Forced Vassalization casus belli, halves CB costs against different-faith targets, shortens truces by 900 days, and imposes −30% development growth and −10 different-culture/faith opinion; **Conciliation** disables Invasion and Conquest CBs entirely, forbids executing Involved prisoners, extends truces by 900 days, boosts development +30%, and unlocks cross-faith friendship and interfaith marriage mechanics; **Compromise** is an armed peace with +10 white-peace acceptance, enforced truces by dukes, and larger garrisons; **Opportunity** is the mobile, scheme-rich opening phase with cheap claims and unlocked schemes like Abduct and Claim Throne ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Struggles)).

Struggles terminate only through **Ending Decisions** taken by a single qualifying ruler in the matching phase — for Iberia: **Status Quo** (from Compromise, destroys the Hispania title and freezes de jure boundaries), **Détente** (from Conciliation, requires an alliance with *every* independent Involved ruler, permanently disables holy wars between the involved cultures), and a dominance ending by conquest ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Struggles), [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/crusader-kings-3-fate-of-iberia-complete-guide-iberian-struggle-andalusia-asturias/)). The Iranian Intermezzo demonstrates the system's generality: different phase names (**Unrest, Stabilization, Concession**) and a catalyst table keyed to its Supporter/Detractor-of-the-Caliphate character markers, showing the phase machine is a reusable engine onto which a region-specific action vocabulary is grafted ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Struggles)). For modeling Skyrim's civil war, the transferable pattern is exact: a regional state machine whose transitions are *earned* by aggregated NPC/player behavior, and whose phases gate which interactions are legal, cheap, or impossible.

**Catalog verdict:** genuinely distinct — no existing catalog entry covers a **phase-gated regional conflict with behavior-aggregated transition tracks**; this is the single most template-ready mechanic in this batch.

## 3. Middle-earth: Shadow of War — Nemesis Intel and Interrogation

### 3.1 How intel is acquired: a tiered information hierarchy

Intel in the Nemesis system is structured as **tiered disclosure of a target's character sheet**. An unknown Nemesis enters the system as a silhouette; dominating or interrogating an ordinary grunt reveals only **name, Power Level, and location** (in Shadow of War, also Strengths), while **Weaknesses can only be learned from a Worm, a Captain, or an intel drop**, and Warchief intel only from Captains or Worms ([Nemesis Wiki](https://shadowofwar.fandom.com/wiki/Nemesis)). In the original Shadow of Mordor the tiers were even stricter — grunts yielded no trait information at all ([Nemesis Wiki](https://shadowofwar.fandom.com/wiki/Nemesis), [Shadow of Mordor manual](https://www.feralinteractive.com/en/manual/shadowofmordor/latest/steam/)). **Worms** are specially flagged low-rank orcs — green diamond icon overhead, green glow in the Wraith World — whose defining property is carrying intel on their superiors ([IGN](https://www.ign.com/wikis/shadow-of-war/Worms), [Shadow of Mordor manual](https://www.feralinteractive.com/en/manual/shadowofmordor/latest/steam/)).

Intel also exists as **environmental artifacts**: dead orc bodies in the wild that provide clues, bulletin boards in outposts, and discarded documents in tents all grant the same reveal without an interrogation ([IGN](https://www.ign.com/wikis/shadow-of-war/Worms)). Once revealed, the target is marked with an orange skull on the map (vs. white for unknown), and hovering shows name, level, Strengths and Weaknesses ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/101638/shadow-of-war---interrogate-worms-and-kill-captains)). The mechanical payoff of intel is concrete: Weaknesses include exploitable fears and vulnerabilities (e.g., fleeing from caragors), while fighting blind means discovering Strengths — invulnerabilities and combat bonuses — the hard way ([Shadow of Mordor manual](https://www.feralinteractive.com/en/manual/shadowofmordor/latest/steam/)).

### 3.2 Cost and risk of interrogation

Interrogation is **low-cost but not free, and the cost is positional rather than resource-based**. The player must physically reach and grab the Worm; Worms **attempt to flee when spotted**, so the recommended approach is an aerial interrogate from high ground before they can run ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/101638/shadow-of-war---interrogate-worms-and-kill-captains)). Worms are frequently embedded among allied orcs, so the practical risk is exposure — initiating the grab in a crowd — though the game grants a concession: **during the intel-selection screen itself the player cannot be harmed** ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/101638/shadow-of-war---interrogate-worms-and-kill-captains)). After interrogation the Worm can be killed or (in Shadow of War) dominated; dominating regular soldiers remains the renewable, weaker intel channel ([IGN](https://www.ign.com/wikis/shadow-of-war/Worms)).

Two design properties stand out for a social-mechanics reading. First, **knowledge about an NPC is itself a stratified game resource with typed sources** — the identity/weakness split means "knowing of someone" and "knowing how to hurt them" are different epistemic states with different acquisition paths, which maps cleanly onto any belief system that wants to distinguish hearsay from actionable detail. Second, intel acquisition is **entangled with the hierarchy it reveals**: Captains are both intel sources about Warchiefs and obstacles guarding them, so the information graph and the power graph are the same graph ([Nemesis Wiki](https://shadowofwar.fandom.com/wiki/Nemesis)).

**Catalog verdict:** a natural extension of the Nemesis knowledge-gating already covered — the transferable refinement is the **typed tiering of knowledge sources** (grunt ≠ worm ≠ captain ≠ document) rather than a new mechanic.

## 4. RimWorld — Ideology DLC and the Art/Tales System

### 4.1 Ideoligions: a collective belief layer over individual mood

The Ideology DLC gives each pawn membership in an **ideoligion** — a composed belief system built from **memes** (high-level themes such as Pain Is Virtue, Transhumanist, Tunneler, Proselytizer) that gate lists of **precepts**: concrete stances on issues (slavery, executions, body modification, drug use, apostasy…), each with graded severities (Abhorrent / Horrible / Disapproved / Preferred / Required…) carrying explicit mood, opinion, behavior-prohibition, and stat effects ([RimWorld Wiki — Ideoligion](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ideoligion)). Precepts operate as a **collective modifier layer on top of individual mood**: the same event (an execution, a skull spike, a blinded colonist) produces opposite mood outcomes for pawns of different ideoligions, and opinion modifiers flow between pawns according to whether their ideoligions condemn each other's conduct ([RimWorld Wiki — Ideoligion](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ideoligion)).

Ideoligions also carry **rituals** — scheduled or triggered ceremonies (leader speech, trial, conversion ritual, sacrifice, festivals) with computed **quality** from factors like participant count, altar/lectern use, room impressiveness, and the moral guide's Social Impact, mapping to outcome tiers (Terrible/Boring/Satisfying/Spectacular and kin) with different rewards, including ideoligion **development points** used to reform the belief system itself ([RimWorld Wiki — Rituals](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/rituals)). Ideoligions are thus partially dynamic objects: development points, earned through conversions and precept-aligned acts, let a player (or theoretically the world) **edit the belief system** — adding a meme or restructuring — making doctrine an accumulating, spendable resource rather than static lore ([RimWorld Wiki — Ideoligion](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ideoligion)).

### 4.2 Certainty: conversion as a tug-of-war over a per-pawn scalar

Each pawn holds a **Certainty score from 0% to 100%** in their current ideoligion; conversion attempts reduce it, and at 0% the pawn flips to the attacker's ideoligion, restarting at **50%** ([RimWorld Wiki — Certainty](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Certainty)). Certainty **regenerates daily, scaled by mood** — 1%/day at ≤20% mood, 2% at 50%, 3% at ≥80%, linearly interpolated between anchors — which creates the signature coupling: **unhappy pawns are persuadable pawns**, so the mood system and the belief system are mechanically linked rather than parallel ([RimWorld Wiki — Certainty](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Certainty)).

Conversion pressure arrives through several typed channels: ambient **social chats** whose frequency is multiplied ×300/×500/×700 by the Proselytizing precept's grade; the Moral Guide role's on-command Convert ability (3-day cooldown, effectiveness ×2 for the guide); prisoner conversion by wardens; and the **conversion ritual**, which at Effective quality cuts certainty by 30% and at Masterful quality converts outright — while a *failed* ritual **backfires and increases certainty** ([RimWorld Wiki — Certainty](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Certainty)). The loss formula itself is a clean multiplicative stack — 6% × converter's Conversion Power × (×2 if Moral Guide) × convertee's Global Certainty Loss Factor × relic factor × trait/meme agreeableness — and **Apostasy precepts** make defectors permanently easier to reconvert (×40–80% future certainty loss) while imposing opinion penalties on them, modeling social punishment of apostasy ([RimWorld Wiki — Certainty](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Certainty)). The extreme mental break **Crisis of Belief** halves certainty in one blow and can flip a pawn to a *random* ideoligion at 0% ([RimWorld Wiki — Certainty](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Certainty)).

![RimWorld certainty loop](assets/rimworld_certainty_loop.png)

### 4.3 Art descriptions and the recorded-event layer ("tales")

Every artwork in RimWorld carries a **semi-randomly generated description composed from two pools: records of actual colony history and pre-written fictional segments** ([RimWorld Wiki — Sculptures](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Sculptures)). The colony-history pool is drawn from a defined vocabulary of recorded event types — killings, social fights, drunkenness, vomiting, drop-pod landings, crafting completions, prior artwork creation, skill mastery, ore strikes, roof-collapse escapes, captures, recruitments, animal training and hunting, surgeries, fires, psychotic episodes, and mental collapses ([RimWorld Wiki — Sculptures](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Sculptures)). A description typically weaves one such real event with decorative elements (a border, a texture, a style, a location), which is why a statue can "commemorate the time Urist burned the kitchen" with full referential accuracy ([RimWorld Wiki — Sculptures](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Sculptures)).

Against Dwarf Fortress's engraving/provenance-object pattern, the mechanical difference is significant. DF's engravings reference a **persistent, world-gen-deep historical-events ledger** shared across legends mode, world sites, and artifacts — provenance is a property of the world. RimWorld's art references a **session-local event log of the current colony only**, mixed with explicitly fictional filler, and the referenced events are chosen from a fixed type vocabulary rather than from arbitrary world history ([RimWorld Wiki — Sculptures](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Sculptures)). In other words: DF's pattern is *historiography* (the object cites the world); RimWorld's is *commemoration* (the object cites the shared player-colony chronicle, plus plausible invention). For Chronicle's purposes, RimWorld demonstrates the lighter-weight version of the same idea — a bounded event-type registry is sufficient to make generated text feel referential — at a fraction of DF's bookkeeping.

**Catalog verdict:** mixed — the **certainty tug-of-war with mood-coupled regeneration** is a genuinely distinct mechanic worth its own entry; the art/tales system is an extension of DF's provenance-object pattern with a deliberately cheaper, session-local event registry.

## 5. Dwarf Fortress — Villains (v0.47) and the Loyalty Cascade

### 5.1 The villains release: intrigue networks across world-gen and fortress mode

Update **0.47.01**, the "villains release," introduced villainy at the world-generation layer — assassinations, coups, and embezzlement as simulated historical acts by networked organizations — and, in fortress mode, **artifact theft plots executed by visiting agents** ([Rock Paper Shotgun](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/dwarf-fortress-has-new-villainous-actions-and-pet-able-pets), [PCGamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/dwarf-fortress-introduces-interrogations-and-lets-dwarves-have-multiple-partners/)). Members of these villainous **organizations** visit the fortress under cover — sometimes under **pseudonyms**, so names obtained from earlier interrogations may not identify them — and attempt to **corrupt residents into plots** such as stealing artifacts, with success determined by the social skills of initiator and recipient ([DF Wiki — Intrigue](http://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Intrigue)).

The player's counter-layer is an actual investigative loop: a sheriff/captain of the guard receives crime reports, suspects can be **interrogated through the Justice screen**, and interrogations yield names and connections that populate a **counterintelligence screen** — catching a handler can expose their master, and a petty theft can unwind into a larger plot ([PCGamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/dwarf-fortress-introduces-interrogations-and-lets-dwarves-have-multiple-partners/), [DF Wiki — Intrigue](http://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Intrigue)). Architecturally this is a **directed, multi-hop conspiracy graph** (agent → handler → villain master → plot) where information about the graph is only accessible through risky social actions against its nodes — the closest shipped analogue to Chronicle's evidence-chain ambitions in this batch ([DF Wiki — Intrigue](http://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Intrigue)).

### 5.2 The loyalty cascade: documented mechanics of the failure mode

The **loyalty cascade** is real, documented, and structural — Toady One himself calls it the "civil war bug" — and it arises directly from how DF tracks faction membership: loyalty is a per-creature label relative to both the **civilization** and the **fortress government**, and killing a fellow citizen flips the killer into an "enemy of the civilization" state **without removing fortress membership** ([DF Wiki — Faction](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Loyalty_cascade)). The canonical trigger matches the project's description: order the military to kill members of your own civilization (merchants, or a citizen ordered executed), and the attackers become **separatists** — enemies of the civ but still order-following fort members; dwarves sent to subdue *them* become **loyalists** with the opposite split, and anyone who then kills a citizen becomes a **renegade**, enemy to both — three mutually hostile "factions" inside one fort, escalating into literal civil war that historically did not resolve on its own ([DF Wiki — Faction](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Loyalty_cascade)).

Documented variants include attacking a werebeast citizen in dwarf form, attacking a berserk citizen (the defenders "lose loyalty and are hunted down as traitors," bug 7107), and releasing tamed enemy mounts, which instantly attack citizens and seed the cascade ([DF Wiki — Faction](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Loyalty_cascade), [DF Wiki — Monarch](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Monarch)). As of DF v0.50, cascades "should periodically end on their own," and DFHack ships a dedicated `fix/loyaltycascade` command — evidence the failure survived for years as an accepted hazard ([DF Wiki — Faction](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Loyalty_cascade)). The design lesson for any faction-membership or forced-compliance mechanic is precise: **when "enemy of faction" and "member of institution" are independent labels, a single sanctioned act of violence can create an unrecoverable logical contradiction that propagates to every actor who touches it**. Punishment systems need either single-source-of-truth allegiance or an explicit reconciliation pass after every intra-faction killing.

**Catalog verdict:** the intrigue/counterintelligence network extends the catalog's DF coverage naturally; the **loyalty cascade is a distinct artifact** — the only fully documented case in the set of a faction-labeling invariant failing catastrophically, and it deserves to be cited as a constraint, not a feature.

## 6. Watch Dogs: Legion — Play as Anyone and the Grievance Chain

### 6.1 Census: runtime-generated profiles on a plausibility knowledge base

Every pedestrian in Legion is procedurally generated **at runtime** by the **Census** system, described by technology director Martin Walsh and team lead designer Liz England as a knowledge base that "fill[s] in all of the blanks in a plausible way": from seed tags (gender, fashion, occupation, income, personality) the system generates a workplace, an income-matched home, hobbies, relationships (a lawyer gets clients; anyone might get a spouse, sibling, best friend), and a **24-hour schedule that is actually simulated** — characters physically travel between scheduled activities, and their off-screen paths are simulated at a rate fine enough that the player can intercept them en route ([GameDeveloper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-watch-dogs-legion-s-play-as-anyone-simulation-works)). Census was the subject of its own GDC 2021 talk by lead AI programmer Christopher Dragert, "Census: The Systemic Backbone Behind Play As Anyone" ([Ubisoft Toronto](https://toronto.ubisoft.com/ubisoft-toronto-programmers-2021-game-developers-conference/)).

Characters exist in **simulation tiers**: background crowd members are deleted when unobserved; any character the player interacts with — or who is connected to such a character — is **"uprezzed"** into a persistent layer with full profile, memory, and schedule, and an aging pool of hundreds of such characters is maintained and culled ([GameDeveloper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-watch-dogs-legion-s-play-as-anyone-simulation-works)). Generation is deliberately **not forward-chained from occupation alone** — the system can anchor on whatever fact is salient (e.g., a pedestrian mid-brawl locks personality and fashion first, and occupation is filled in later), a "spider-web" approach explicitly designed to avoid both "procedural soup" and stereotype templates ([GameDeveloper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-watch-dogs-legion-s-play-as-anyone-simulation-works)).

### 6.2 The grievance chain: assembled at runtime, justified backward

The answer to the prompt's central question is clear from the developers' own account: **the grievance is not drawn from a pre-simulated social graph of faction wrongdoing; it is assembled at recruitment time and justified backward from the generated profile**. The team's stated pipeline is that each recruitable character gets "some sort of problem that's uniquely purposed to DedSec to help them with — we try to match this with the profile of the person that we've created" ([GameDeveloper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-watch-dogs-legion-s-play-as-anyone-simulation-works)). Recruitment missions are then built as gameplay-beat skeletons ("hack a server" → "resolve a hostage situation") whose **locations and participating characters are selected at runtime** from the recruit's Census profile and relationships, per mission-systems lead Jurie Horneman — the what/when/where is templated, the who/why comes from the generated backstory ([GameDeveloper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-watch-dogs-legion-s-play-as-anyone-simulation-works)). Mechanically, the player-facing chain runs: scan → add to contacts → (with the Deep Profiler upgrade) inspect schedule and **recruitment leads** → complete the generated recruitment mission → operative joins ([IGN](https://www.ign.com/wikis/watch-dogs-legion/How_to_Recruit_People)).

The backward-justification pattern is stated explicitly for the opinion system: rather than simulating Albion's crimes in advance, "we've actually spawned somebody who doesn't like DedSec, now let's give them a reason why" — player actions are recorded and fed back into character generation as *post hoc explanations* of generated dispositions, a technique Walsh directly links to Ben Sunshine-Hill's **alibi-generation** research ([GameDeveloper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-watch-dogs-legion-s-play-as-anyone-simulation-works)). Consequences do propagate forward once characters exist in the uprezzed layer: memories persist, hatred of DedSec can make a character unrecruitable, and sufficiently aggrieved NPCs can trigger **revenge missions**, including kidnapping one of the player's operatives to force a rescue ([GameDeveloper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-watch-dogs-legion-s-play-as-anyone-simulation-works), [Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/2020/11/17/21571804/watch-dogs-legion-adversary-london-population-chaos/), [TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/a311733/a-dish-best-served-cold-achievement)). So the social graph is real and causal *after* uprezzing — but the initial grievance that makes a stranger recruitable is synthesized on demand, not discovered in a simulation.

**Catalog verdict:** genuinely distinct — Legion's **generate-then-justify runtime backstory assembly** (alibi-generation lineage) is a different architecture from anything in the catalog, and it is a directly relevant alternative model for Chronicle: grievances and relationships need not be pre-simulated to be consistent, only *made* consistent at the moment they surface.

## 7. Cross-Cutting Notes for the Catalog

Three of the six items converge on a single transferable insight worth flagging beyond the individual entries: **spread and allegiance are cheapest to model as gated scalar state, not as simulated process**. CK3 legends are a funded scalar (spread chance → barony count → tier), RimWorld belief is a per-pawn scalar (certainty 0–100%) with typed attack/regeneration channels, and CK3 struggles are two competing scalar tracks (catalyst points to 1,000) aggregated across a region ([CK3 Wiki — Character](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Character), [RimWorld Wiki — Certainty](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Certainty), [CK3 Wiki — Struggles](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Struggles)). In every case the scalar is legible to the player, tunable by explicit actions, and robust to simulation scale — and in the one counterexample where a shipped game instead maintained rich per-entity relational labels (DF's civ/fort membership pair), the failure mode was the loyalty cascade ([DF Wiki — Faction](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Loyalty_cascade)).

The counterpoint is that scalar models sacrifice exactly what Chronicle's vision document bets on: provenance. CK3's legend does not record *who told it to whom*; RimWorld's certainty does not record *which argument moved it*; the struggle track does not record *which murder* contributed +5 ([CK3 Wiki — Struggles](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Struggles)). Watch Dogs: Legion offers the pragmatic middle path — consistency manufactured at read-time rather than maintained at write-time ([GameDeveloper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-watch-dogs-legion-s-play-as-anyone-simulation-works)) — while DF's villains system shows genuine multi-hop provenance (agent → handler → master) surviving interrogation-based partial disclosure ([DF Wiki — Intrigue](http://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Intrigue)). For the catalog, items 2 (Struggles) and 6 (Census/alibi-generation) warrant new entries; items 1, 3, and the RimWorld art system extend existing entries; item 5 splits — intrigue networks extend, loyalty cascade is a new cautionary-constraint entry.
