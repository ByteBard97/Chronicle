# Six Game-Mechanics Gaps for Chronicle: Research Findings

## TL;DR
- All six mechanics were located with concrete primary- or wiki-sourced detail; four map cleanly onto Chronicle's existing catalog (belief/rumor propagation, grudge tracking, provenance objects, faction membership) as natural extensions, while two — CK3's Struggle phase-state machine and Watch Dogs: Legion's lazy "uprezzing" social-graph generation — are genuinely distinct architectural patterns worth adopting rather than extending.
- The single most transferable pattern for Skyrim's civil war is CK3's Struggle system: a regional conflict as a cyclic finite-state machine of named phases (Iberia: Opportunity → Hostility/Conciliation → Compromise) whose transitions are driven by accumulated "catalyst" points from character actions, with each phase gating which actions are legal.
- The starkest cautionary tale is Dwarf Fortress's loyalty cascade — which Toady One reportedly calls the "civil war bug" — a faction-membership failure where ordering an execution flips the executioners into enemies of their own civilization, recursively, until civil war; a direct warning for any forced-compliance or membership-flag mechanic in Chronicle.

## Key Findings
1. **CK3 Legends** spread barony-by-barony as a resource-fed diffusion gated by a "Legend Spread Chance," with three quality tiers (Famed → Illustrious → Mythical) unlocked at 100 and 300 baronies, conferring real mechanical bonuses, not just flavor. **Natural extension** of rumor/belief propagation plus provenance objects.
2. **CK3 Struggles** are explicit multi-phase finite-state machines gating available actions; Iberia and Persia use different named phases and transition rules. **Genuinely distinct** — a formal phase-gated conflict layer Chronicle does not currently have.
3. **Shadow of War intel** is acquired by interrogating "worms" (low-rank orcs) to reveal a named captain's strengths/weaknesses/location; the cost/risk is situational (exposure, worms flee, botched grabs kill them) rather than a resource price. **Natural extension** of provenance/knowledge-about-agents.
4. **RimWorld Ideology** adds ideoligions (memes → precepts), rituals, conversion, and per-pawn certainty as a collective belief layer distinct from mood; its Tale system logs real colony events that art then references. **Natural extension** of belief propagation and provenance objects.
5. **Dwarf Fortress Villains (v0.47)** adds world-gen intrigue networks (agreements, embezzlement, corruption, plots) and fortress interrogation; the loyalty cascade is a documented emergent failure of the faction system. **Natural extension** (intrigue) plus a critical **cautionary case study** (cascade).
6. **Watch Dogs: Legion Census** generates NPC profiles, relationships, and grievance chains lazily ("uprezzing") at profiling time from a rules database, not a fully pre-simulated social graph. **Genuinely distinct** generation architecture relative to Chronicle's presumed persistent-simulation approach.

## Details

### 1. Crusader Kings III — Legends (Legends of the Dead, patch 1.12.1 "Scythe," released March 4, 2024; announced February 7, 2024)

A Legend is a narrative object built from a **Legend Seed** (three types: **Legitimizing, Holy, Heroic**), with a chosen **Legend Protagonist** — the ruler themselves or a family member, including long-dead ancestors. The player pays a large upfront gold cost plus a continuous monthly maintenance fee to keep it being promoted.

**How spread is modeled:** It is a resource-fed diffusion across the map's baronies rather than a scripted radius. A per-tick "Legend Spread Chance" pushes the legend from barony to adjacent barony. The player raises that chance primarily by assigning a **Court Chronicler** (and optionally poets/musicians in paid sub-jobs) to one of two tasks: **Extol Domestic Legend** (spread within your own realm) or **Commend Legend Abroad** (persuade bordering rulers to become **promoters** who spread it through their realms, more slowly). Neighboring rulers can be recruited as promoters via events, hooks, bribes, or opinion. Crucially, spread is gated by a **language barrier**: bordering realms that don't speak your language slow growth "to a crawl" or stop it entirely — a concrete adjacency/similarity constraint on diffusion.

**Quality tiers:** A legend starts at **Famed**. Per the CK3 wiki: "When a legend is created it will start at Famed quality. Once it has spread to 100 Baronies it can be upgraded to Illustrious quality and will not spread further until upgraded. Once it has spread to 300 Baronies it can be upgraded to Mythical quality and will not spread further until upgraded." Each upgrade requires another cash (and often prestige/piety) injection. So "quality" is a function of geographic reach plus paid promotion, not narrative content.

**Mechanical effects beyond flavor:** Counties carrying the legend get a legend modifier (bonuses depend on type). While promoting, the owner and promoters receive ongoing bonuses; the base-game Legitimacy mechanic is boosted by legends. On **completion**, type-specific rewards are substantial: per a detailed player summary, Heroic legends let you vassalize neighbors via war for 15 years regardless of realm size; Holy legends let you mass-convert provinces and declare an extra kingdom-level holy war; Legitimizing legends give free claims and let you instantly de jure drift a title into your primary title. Completion also unlocks Legendary Buildings and a Heroic dynastic Legacy.

**Catalog fit:** Natural extension — a Legend is essentially a provenance-tagged rumor object (named protagonist, embellishment thresholds) whose propagation Chronicle could model with its existing belief/rumor-propagation layer, adding a similarity/language gate for adjacency.

### 2. Crusader Kings III — Struggles (Fate of Iberia; Legacy of Persia)

Per the CK3 wiki, "Struggles are situations that go through multiple phases that change 3 months after sufficient Catalysts, actions that push the struggle towards the next phase, take place." Every character is **Involved**, **Interloper**, or **Uninvolved**, determined by whether their realm capital is in the struggle region and whether their faith/culture is involved; only Involved and Interloper characters generate catalysts.

**Iberian Struggle (Fate of Iberia):** Four cyclic phases, always starting in **Opportunity**. The cycle: Opportunity → either **Hostility** or **Conciliation** (depending on whether characters' catalyst actions are violent or friendly) → both build/decay toward **Compromise** → Compromise decays back to Opportunity, restarting the cycle. Phase effects gate actions:
- **Opportunity:** transitional; unlocks interfaith marriage, blocks holy wars, makes war and culture/faith conversion cheaper.
- **Hostility:** wars cheaper and more brutal; reduced economic/tech progress; increased capacity for hostile schemes.
- **Conciliation:** pragmatic diplomacy; harsher biases dropped for expediency; interfaith marriage.
- **Compromise:** diplomacy peaks; Struggle Clashes limited to a single county and cost more prestige; longer truces.

Transitions fire "3 months after sufficient Catalysts" — i.e., accumulated catalyst points from qualifying character interactions (a war hastens Hostility; an alliance pushes Conciliation). The struggle can be ended three ways by independent Involved/Interloper rulers, each granting 10,000 Renown; ending it also yields a Heroic legend seed. (Note the widely reported balance quirk that late-game Conciliation dominates because gift-giving and city upgrades spam pro-Conciliation catalysts, making Hostility — and thus forming the Empire of Hispania — hard to reach.)

**Iranian Intermezzo (Legacy of Persia, 867 only):** A different, more compressed design with **three phases — Unrest, Stabilization, and Concession** (confirmed by the Legacy of Persia tutorial description). Concession is a new **"Ending Phase"**: reaching it instantly triggers a struggle ending, and unlike the decision-based endings, every Involved character can push toward it by triggering relevant catalysts. Characters carry a **Detractor of the Caliphate** or **Supporter of Caliphal Authority** trait (switchable by decision only every 20 years unless the Sunni Caliph dies) that determines their stance. Involvement threshold differs too: a faith/culture is involved if 50% of its counties are in the Persia region (vs. 80% for Iberia). Documented balance quirk: players report catalysts sometimes route counterintuitively (e.g., raiding during Stabilization adding to Concession), and the vanilla Concession timer is tight (~80 years), which modders extend to ~200.

**Catalog fit:** Genuinely distinct. Chronicle's civil war would gain a formal region-scoped finite-state machine whose phases gate legal actions and whose transitions are driven by accumulated points from tracked NPC/faction actions — a template the project does not currently have as a binary questline.

### 3. Middle-earth: Shadow of War — Worms and Intel

Captains and Warchiefs are procedurally generated by the Nemesis System with hidden **Strengths, Weaknesses, Immunities, and Vulnerabilities**. Per the in-game Tutorials/Hints and multiple guides, you cannot see or exploit these until you acquire **Intel**. Intel comes from two sources: **interrogating "worms"** (low-rank orcs marked as green diamond icons on the map/minimap and in Wraith-world) and **Intel pickups** scattered in the world.

**How intel is acquired and "spent":** You grab a worm and hold the interrogate input (a drain-style action; worms are skittish and flee, so players use Attract to separate them from groups or interrogate from an aerial attack to prevent escape). A successful interrogation opens the Army screen and lets you **Reveal Intel** on a specific captain — exposing their strengths/weaknesses and their location (captains you have intel on show as orange skulls; unknown captains show white skulls). Intel isn't a spendable currency; it's per-target knowledge that unlocks the ability to exploit weaknesses and guarantees a rune drop when a Vulnerability is exploited.

**Cost/risk of interrogation:** There is no resource price, but there is real situational risk. Worms are usually surrounded by allies, so interrogating exposes you to a fight; worms flee at the first sign of trouble; and a botched grab (or certain gear that causes stealth-kill head explosions) **kills the worm instead**, wasting it. A documented "feature": once only one unknown commander remains before the death-threat story mission, interrogation is disabled. Note this mechanic also exists in the predecessor, Shadow of Mordor.

**Catalog fit:** Natural extension — intel is a provenance/knowledge object about a specific tracked agent (captain), acquired from a source NPC (worm). Chronicle already models "NPCs form beliefs with provenance"; this is the same pattern applied to actionable tactical knowledge about a named adversary.

### 4. RimWorld — Ideology DLC and the Tale system

**Ideoligions (released July 20, 2021):** Each pawn belongs to an ideoligion built from **memes** (high-level themes) that branch into **precepts** (specific rules governing behavior, opinion, and preference — e.g., Proselytizing frequency, cannibalism, execution ethics). Ideoligions define **social roles** (Leader, Moral Guide, skill Specialists) and **rituals** (feasts, sacrifices, conversion ceremonies).

**Certainty (the per-pawn belief-strength score):** Per the RimWorld wiki, "Certainty is how willing a colonist is to maintain their current ideoligion. Conversion attempts reduce certainty. Once certainty hits 0%, a pawn's ideology will change. Certainty increases by a set rate per day, which is dependent on current mood." Conversion is computed as: "Certainty loss = 6% × Conversion Power of the Converter × (×2 if Converter is Moral Guide) × Global Certainty Loss Factor × Relic Conversion Power Factor × Trait/Meme Agreeableness × Storyteller low-pop boost." Conversion vectors include: passive day-to-day conversation (governed by Ideoligion Spread Chance, raised by the Proselytizer meme); the Moral Guide's Convert ability (3-day cooldown); warden conversion of prisoners; the Conversion Ritual (~20-day cooldown, can convert a ~70%-certainty pawn in one go if high quality); and shocks — a **Crisis of Belief** mental break immediately cuts certainty by 50%, and hitting 0% converts to a *random* ideoligion. Brainwipe sets certainty to 0%.

**Distinct from mood:** Certainty is a separate axis from the mood/needs system, but the two interact — certainty regenerates faster at good mood, and forced mood breaks are a deliberate exploit to trigger Crises of Belief. This is exactly the "collective belief layer distinct from but interacting with individual mood" the project describes.

**Tale system (art provenance):** RimWorld internally records colony events as **Tales** via the **TaleManager**. Per community/modding documentation: "Every time something relevant happens on a colony, a 'tale' is registered on the save so it can be used on art (like deaths, research, training and so on)." When a pawn creates artwork, the piece stores a `<taleRef>` with a `<seed>` and a specific `<tale>` ID (e.g., `Tale_3300`); the description is generated from that tale plus the seed using RulePack grammar files (e.g., `RulePacks_Art_Descriptions`). Changing the seed re-words the same event; changing the tale ID makes the art depict a different real recorded event; setting it to null makes it about nothing.

**How this differs from Dwarf Fortress's engraving/provenance pattern:** Both reference recorded historical events, but the coupling differs. In DF, engravings are tied to the deep world-history/legends layer (thousands of historical figures and events spanning world-gen and play), and — per the DF wiki — dwarves can emotionally react to *seeing* an engraving/statue that reminds them of an event they personally experienced, so the provenance object feeds back into the observer's mood. RimWorld's Tale→art link is a lighter, colony-scoped, grammar-driven flavor layer keyed to a bounded log of recent colony events; the artwork carries provenance but does not (in vanilla) trigger the same personal-memory emotional feedback loop when viewed. DF's pattern is world-historical and reactive; RimWorld's is colony-local and descriptive.

**Catalog fit:** Natural extension on both counts — ideoligion/certainty is belief propagation with an explicit per-agent belief-strength scalar; Tales are provenance objects. Chronicle's design already blends these, so RimWorld is a close template, with DF showing the more ambitious reactive-provenance variant.

### 5. Dwarf Fortress — Villains update (v0.47.01, released January 29, 2020) and the loyalty cascade

**Intrigue networks:** The "villains release" added, per the DF wiki, intrigue as "a collection of mechanics and tools that enable historical figures to enact villainous plots." The core mechanism "works through agreements: historical figures reach out to one another and use a variety of tactics (persuasion, blackmail, bribes) to reach an agreement" for funding, assassination, theft, etc. Documented plot types include "theft, sabotage, abduction, assassination, coups, corrupt advancement promises, embezzlement, starting wars, and framing enemies." Villains operate in networks called **organisations**. Civilizations have counter-intelligence: spymasters and law-enforcement position-holders "keep track of the evidence they've collected from witnesses, interrogation and surveillance," generating leads on whom to surveil or interrogate next.

**Fortress-mode interrogation:** Members of villainous organisations visit your fortress to corrupt residents into plots. Per the wiki: "Unless you successfully interrogate them through the Justice screen, there will be no indication that the visitor has nefarious intentions; they may also use one or more pseudonyms, so any names of members acquired from previous interrogations may not be enough to identify them." Interrogation uses persuade/intimidate skills; the feature was shipped acknowledged-incomplete (evidence linkage limited in v0.47).

**The loyalty cascade (cautionary case study):** This is an emergent failure of the faction/loyalty system. Per the DF wiki (Faction): "Loyalty cascades are the result of entity members attacking each other. The conflict will escalate into a full civil war (hence Toady One referring to it as the 'civil war bug') and won't resolve until one side of the conflict is wiped out." The classic trigger: ordering your military to attack your own civ's merchants, or executing/killing a citizen. Per the wiki, this "makes the members of your military who attacked the traders become enemies of your civilization, but remain members of your fort's government." These "separatists" attack other citizens as enemies but still follow fort orders; citizens who then attack the separatists become "loyalists" (loyal to civ, not fort); and each new kill of a civ member flips the killer's flags again. As a player summary on the DF Steam forum puts it: "anything attacking a member of a civilisation becomes an enemy of said civilisation, but when it is their own civilisation then everyone around becomes their enemy... It rapidly devolves into a huge melee where everyone is everyone else's enemy" — a self-sustaining civil war that ends only when one side is wiped out. It can be triggered indirectly too (releasing a caged wild beast that has killed a dwarf; a werebeast reverting mid-fight; even tavern brawls).

**Why it matters for Chronicle:** Any mechanic that flips a membership/enemy flag on an NPC as a side effect of a forced action (execution, forced compliance, conscription) risks exactly this class of recursive contagion if loyalty is binary and re-evaluated on each hostile act. The lesson: model loyalty/faction membership with damped, non-recursive transitions and explicit "witnessed by / retaliation" scoping, or a single grudge can cascade a whole settlement into mutual hostility.

**Catalog fit:** The intrigue layer is a natural extension of faction membership plus grudge tracking; the loyalty cascade is the cautionary counter-example showing how naive faction-flag flipping produces runaway failure.

### 6. Watch Dogs: Legion — "Play as Anyone" and the Census system (Ubisoft Toronto, 2020)

**The core question — pre-simulated vs. assembled-fresh:** Census is a persistent **relational database and rules/knowledge base**, but individual NPCs' full profiles, relationships, backstories, and schedules are generated **lazily, on demand, at profiling/interaction time** — a process the team internally calls **"uprezzing."** It is *not* a pre-computed store of every Londoner's complete life. Creative Director Clint Hocking (Gamasutra/Game Developer, June 2019): "The most important thing about census is that it allows us to spawn NPCs in the world just like you do in many other games, but then when you profile those NPCs the relational database is able to fill in the blanks on who they are and sort of generate them in real time and then make them persistent and keep them in the world."

Per the AI and Games documentary (Tommy Thompson, Game Developer, Dec 2020) interviewing Liz England (Team Lead Game Designer), Jurie Horneman (Team Lead Programmer, Mission Systems), and Martin Walsh (Technology Director, Gameplay & AI): a character is spawned as generic crowd; if ignored, it's deleted once you're far enough away; but "any character you interact with... will be promoted into the deeper simulation. But in addition, it will also promote their personal connections: their family members or friends." Any uprezzed character "has their full profile generated at runtime, including their backstory, their affinity towards DedSec, their relationships to other characters and their schedule of daily activities." England describes generation as a "spider-web: we start with one fact about a person and then you can start adding more facts... until at the very end of the uprez process, what you now have is a completely unique character." Walsh describes **tiered simulation**: on-screen animated NPCs; a "fully uprezzed" layer (contacts + mission-cast characters); and a deeper layer for incidentally-encountered or related NPCs — actively tracking hundreds of citizens that cycle out over time.

**How the grievance chain is generated/gated:** Recruitment missions are authored by designers as abstract **"gameplay beats"** (Horneman: "go do this thing here, then go do this thing there but the actual locations where that action takes place are selected at runtime"). The characters are defined abstractly and Census supplies a matching **"actor."** The recruit's specific grievance — e.g., a relative held by Albion — is bound via **"dynamic casting"/"recasting"**: the system matches already-uprezzed contacts/relatives to mission roles. Technology Director Christopher Dragert (Ubisoft news, March 2021): "people have webs of connections in real life... memories and affinity actually propagate across relationships. So, if you help someone out, their relatives will like you more, and will like DedSec more as well." Hocking's own example: he recruited operative Lionel Galant by rescuing his father from an Albion cage; hours later he encountered the father, Andrew Galant, alive in the world as persistent relationship proof. A **Player Attention System** governs when these opportunities surface. NPCs carry **permanent memories** that "propagate to their enemies and friends," and NPCs who hate DedSec can trigger **revenge missions** via a matcher that slots them into predefined mission roles. Population distribution is modeled on **real-world London census data** (Lead Producer Sean Crooks). Notably, the game caps the active recruit/roster set (community sources cite 42 including candidates and paid agents), reinforcing that depth is generated only where it is needed.

**Catalog fit:** Genuinely distinct as an *architecture*. The grievance chain is not drawn from a fully pre-simulated persistent social graph; it is assembled at runtime from a rules database plus a small, lazily-promoted web of uprezzed connections, with authored abstract mission templates cast dynamically. Chronicle's stated design ("NPCs form beliefs with provenance... grudges/obligations write back into behavior") implies a persistent simulation; Census offers the opposite, performance-driven pattern — generate depth only where the player looks — which is a distinct option worth weighing.

## Recommendations

**Adopt as templates (highest value first):**
1. **Model Skyrim's civil war on the CK3 Struggle FSM.** Implement named phases (e.g., Opportunity/Escalation/Stalemate/Settlement) as a region-scoped finite-state machine where each phase gates which player and faction actions are legal, and transitions fire only after accumulated "catalyst" points from tracked NPC/faction actions cross a threshold (with the CK3 "3-month lag after threshold" as a debounce). Prefer Iberia's four-phase cyclic model for a long war; borrow Persia's "Ending Phase" concept for decisive resolutions. **Benchmark to change course:** if playtests show one phase dominating (as CK3's Conciliation does when gift-giving spams catalysts), rebalance catalyst weights before shipping.
2. **Treat the loyalty cascade as a hard design constraint.** Before implementing any forced-compliance, execution, or conscription mechanic, verify that faction/loyalty flags use damped, non-recursive transitions scoped to witnesses and direct participants. **Threshold:** if a single hostile act can flip more than its direct participants' allegiance in one evaluation tick, redesign — that is the cascade signature.

**Extend existing catalog systems:**
3. **Legends → rumor/provenance layer.** Add a similarity/adjacency gate (CK3's language barrier) to belief propagation, plus explicit quality tiers unlocked by reach, and let a spread belief confer real behavioral modifiers (not just flavor) to carry the provenance object's weight.
4. **Certainty scalar for beliefs.** Adopt RimWorld's per-agent certainty score (regenerating with mood, reducible by conversion attempts, with a shock-based 50% drop) as the numeric backbone for how firmly an NPC holds a provenance-tagged belief, and make conversion a multiplicative formula so social skill, role, and relics/relics-analogues matter.
5. **Intel-as-knowledge and Tale-as-provenance** are already near-identical to Chronicle's belief-with-provenance model; implement them as the same object type (knowledge about an agent; record of an event) with acquisition sources (interrogation; observation) rather than as bespoke systems.

**Evaluate, don't blindly adopt:**
6. **Census lazy generation.** If Chronicle's persistent simulation becomes a performance bottleneck on Skyrim's NPC count, consider Census-style lazy "uprezzing" — generate deep social/grievance detail only for NPCs the player engages — but note this trades away the persistent, always-simulated world the project's premise implies. **Decision threshold:** adopt only if full persistent simulation misses frame-time budgets; otherwise keep persistence, which better serves "rumors mutate as they spread."

## Caveats
- Several CK3 completion-reward specifics (e.g., Heroic legends' 15-year vassalization) come from a detailed player summary rather than the official wiki; the tier thresholds (100/300 baronies, Famed/Illustrious/Mythical) and spread mechanics are wiki-confirmed. The Legends of the Dead expansion shipped with the free 1.12.1 "Scythe" update on March 4, 2024 (announced February 7, 2024).
- Shadow of War's interrogation "cost" is situational risk, not a resource price; sources are guides and the in-game tutorial text rather than a developer postmortem.
- The DF Villains feature shipped explicitly incomplete in v0.47.01 (limited evidence linkage), so some intrigue behavior is abstract/opaque; the loyalty cascade is well-documented across the wiki and community but is an emergent bug-turned-feature, not a designed system.
- For Census, Ubisoft marketing language ("millions of playable denizens," "everyone is unique") is framing; the shipped reality per developer interviews is lazy on-demand generation over an active set of hundreds. Dragert's full GDC 2021 talk video ("Census: The Systemic Backbone Behind Play As Anyone") is members-only, so exact database sizes/row counts were not disclosed publicly.
- RimWorld's exact conversion-formula coefficients are wiki-sourced and version-dependent; they may shift across game versions. Ideology released July 20, 2021.
