# Six NPC/Social-Simulation Mechanics: Concrete Mechanical Breakdown

## TL;DR
- Four of the six (CK3 Legends, CK3 Struggle, RimWorld Ideoligions, Watch Dogs: Legion "Play as Anyone") are *genuinely distinct* new mechanics that go beyond standard NPC-social-simulation patterns; the Nemesis intel/interrogation loop is a natural extension of an existing procedural-antagonist system, and Dwarf Fortress's Villains intrigue is a distinct world-gen-layer system while its loyalty cascade is an emergent failure mode of ordinary faction-membership logic rather than a designed feature.
- The most transferable templates for "a multi-phase regional conflict, not a binary questline" are CK3's Struggle (four named cyclic phases gated by accumulated "Catalyst" points) and, for a diffusion-style belief/reputation object, CK3's Legends (a typed narrative object that spreads county-by-county and confers per-county modifiers while active).
- The clearest cautionary tale for any forced-compliance or faction-membership mechanic is Dwarf Fortress's "loyalty cascade": ordering a citizen's death makes the executioners themselves civ-traitors, which recursively makes their attackers traitors, escalating to a self-terminating civil war.

## Key Findings

**1. CK3 Legends** — A legend is a typed narrative object (Heroic/Holy/Legitimizing) with a named Protagonist and three quality tiers (Famed → Illustrious → Mythical). It spreads across the map from its origin, plague-style, and confers a *per-county* modifier on every county it reaches while active. Upgrading quality requires spreading to 100 baronies (Illustrious) then 300 baronies (Mythical).

**2. CK3 Struggle** — A regional situation with four named cyclic phases: **Opportunity, Hostility, Compromise, Conciliation**. Phase transitions are driven by accumulated "Catalyst" points from character interactions, not a scripted timeline (the Iberian Struggle uses a 1,000-point threshold).

**3. Nemesis intel/interrogation (Shadow of War)** — "Worms" are interrogatable orcs that each yield intel on one captain/warchief of the player's choice; intel reveals name, level, strengths, and weaknesses. The system is a natural extension of the procedural-antagonist Nemesis System.

**4. RimWorld Ideology** — Ideoligions layer collective belief (memes → precepts → rituals) on top of individual mood, with a per-pawn Certainty score governing conversion. Separately, RimWorld's art "Tales" system attaches a recorded colony event object to a generated artwork.

**5. Dwarf Fortress Villains (v0.47)** — World-gen intrigue networks of agents running plots (embezzlement, corruption, assassination, theft) via agreements; fortress-mode interrogation via the Justice screen. The "loyalty cascade" is a documented emergent failure mode.

**6. Watch Dogs: Legion "Play as Anyone"** — Every pedestrian is procedurally generated by the "Census" backbone, with a real schedule, job, relationships, and a recruitment grievance assembled at "uprez" time — largely generated fresh, not drawn from a fully pre-simulated global social graph.

## Details

### 1. Crusader Kings III — Legends (Legends of the Dead, 2024)

**The object.** Per Paradox Dev Diary #145 ("Legends & Legitimacy," PDX-Trinexx, Feb 19 2024), "Legends have a Type and Quality level. Because no legend can be entirely mundane, they start at Famed, then Illustrious and finally Mythical, each of them giving out progressively more powerful rewards." Types are Heroic, Holy, or Legitimizing. Every legend has a chosen Protagonist ("your relatives (even if dead)"), and choosing a dead ancestor grants extra Legitimacy (+20, or +50 if that protagonist has a virtue trait). Legends originate from a "Legend Seed" — some historical (e.g., the Capetians' "Heirs of Charlemagne," "Hunnic Heritage" for nomads), others earned by winning wars, hunting a legendary animal, or hiring a Court Chronicler.

**The spread model.** Spread is a map-diffusion process, described by developers and reviewers as plague-like ("Legends spread — much like diseases — from a center point," per a Medium writeup by Pascal Wagner). It is driven by a "Legend Spread Chance" that the player raises by spending resources: paying Court Position tasks (Chronicler, Musician, Poet) to "Extol Domestic Legend" (a monthly fee, boost scaling with the courtier's Aptitude), and "Commend Legend Abroad" to recruit neighboring rulers as promoters. Random events also nudge spread up or down. Cross-realm spread requires geographic contiguity — to become a promoter a ruler must border a county carrying the legend, know the creator's language, or be a lover/friend of the creator. Community testing emphasizes a language barrier: legends spread fast through same-language baronies and "slow to a crawl" across foreign-language borders. **Caveat:** Paradox has not published a numeric spread-chance formula (base value, exact per-modifier weights), and no official source explicitly states a strict county-adjacency rule — adjacency-diffusion is strongly implied by dev diaries and reviewers but not quoted as a hard rule.

**Quality tiers and cost.** Per the CK3 wiki, verbatim: "When a legend is created it will start at Famed quality. Once it has spread to 100 Baronies it can be upgraded to Illustrious quality and will not spread further until upgraded. Once it has spread to 300 Baronies it can be upgraded to Mythical quality." Creation costs 200 Gold (Count/Duke), 300 (King), or 400 (Emperor), plus 75 Prestige for Legitimizing legends, plus monthly maintenance until completed; a legend needs at least 5 years before it can be completed or abandoned. Promoters pay half maintenance.

**Mechanical effect beyond flavor — yes, a per-county modifier while active.** Per the CK3 wiki, each county the legend has spread to gets a modifier that scales by type and tier (in addition to +5/+10/+20 Popular Opinion by tier):
- **Holy:** +0.10 / +0.15 / +0.25 Monthly Control (Famed/Illustrious/Mythical).
- **Heroic:** +5% / +10% / +20% Stationed Men-at-Arms Damage.
- **Legitimizing:** +1% / +2% / +6% Development Growth.

Dev Diary #145 confirms this in prose: "enjoy the control boosts from having it spread over people that now believe you a hero." Completion rewards scale by tier — e.g., a Legitimizing legend gives double Legitimacy plus unpressed de jure claims (Mythical grants up to +600 Legitimacy and empire-wide claims). Because these bonuses accrue widely and cheaply for a large realm, players and Paradox forum posters noted Legends became overpowered and trivially easy to max in a big realm ("mythical in a year with a big enough realm").

**Assessment: genuinely distinct.** A typed, named narrative object that diffuses geographically and paints active per-province modifiers onto the map is not a standard NPC-social pattern; it is closer to a reputation/propaganda diffusion layer.

### 2. Crusader Kings III — Struggle / Phase system (Fate of Iberia, 2022)

**Exact phases.** Per Dev Diary #94 ("Anatomy of a Struggle") and the game's own patch notes/wiki, the Iberian Struggle has four phases: **Opportunity, Hostility, Compromise, Conciliation.** (Note: some third-party guides misspell these — e.g., "Hospitality" — but the correct four names are Opportunity, Hostility, Compromise, Conciliation.) The dev diary states: "The Iberian Struggle's phases are Opportunity, Hostility, Compromise, and Conciliation. Opportunity can lead to either Hostility or Conciliation, depending on how the peninsula's leaders treat each other, whilst both Hostility and Conciliation respectively build or degrade towards Compromise, which in turn decays into Opportunity, starting the cycle again."

**Exact transition triggers.** Per the CK3 wiki, "Struggles are situations that go through multiple phases that change 3 months after sufficient Catalysts... take place." Catalysts are actions by Involved characters (and, rarely, Interlopers) that push toward a phase. The Iberian Struggle always starts in Opportunity. TheGamer's guide states the threshold explicitly: "The phase that reaches the requisite one thousand Catalyst points first wins. A Hostility phase is always followed by a Conciliation phase, and a Conciliation phase is always followed by an Opportunity Phase." Hostile interactions (wars, hostile schemes) generate Hostility catalysts; friendly ones (interfaith marriage, alliances, befriending) generate Conciliation catalysts. Character status is Involved / Interloper / Uninvolved, determined by whether their realm capital is in the struggle region and whether their faith/culture is "involved."

**Per-phase gating of actions.** Each phase changes the ruleset: Opportunity makes war and culture/faith conversion cheaper, unlocks interfaith marriage, blocks holy wars; Hostility makes wars cheaper but bloodier, reduces economic/tech progress, boosts hostile scheme capacity; Compromise makes peace/truces easier and longer; Conciliation temporarily drops many biases. Phases also alter unlock criteria on existing content (e.g., Claim Throne scheme, Found Holy Order). The struggle has multiple possible **Ending Decisions** taken by a single independent Involved/Interloper ruler; each ending grants that dynasty 10,000 Renown (and, post-Legends DLC, a Heroic legend seed). A documented balance flaw: at large realm sizes Conciliation catalysts dominate, making the Hostility phase (needed to form Hispania) very hard to reach.

**Assessment: genuinely distinct** and the strongest direct template for "a multi-phase regional conflict rather than a binary questline" — discrete named phases, accumulation-based transitions, and phase-gated action availability.

### 3. Nemesis System intel/interrogation (Middle-earth: Shadow of War, Monolith/WB, 2017)

**Worms and intel acquisition.** "Worms" are low-rank orcs marked green (diamond icon) in Wraith Vision and on the minimap. Interrogating a Worm reveals intel on **one captain/warchief of the player's choice in that region** — name, power level, location, strengths, and weaknesses. Per the Shadow of War wiki: in Shadow of War the player gets name, power level, location and Strengths from dominating regular grunts, but "can only obtain the Weaknesses of a Nemesis from a Captain, an intel drop or a Worm... only get intel on a Warchief from Captains or Worms." Weaknesses can be exploited (e.g., immunity/vulnerability to fire, poison, stealth, headshots). Intel on undiscovered captains changes their map marker from a white skull (unknown) to an orange skull (known, strengths/weaknesses viewable).

**Cost/risk of interrogation.** The interrogation is single-use per Worm — "Once you've used a Worm to receive information about an Orc, you will kill them and have to find another one if you want to reveal another Captain." Worms are typically surrounded by allies and try to flee, so interrogation carries a combat/exposure cost (players are advised to aerial-attack to pin them, then retreat to high ground). A key alternative use: instead of interrogating, the player can send a Worm to a captain with a Death Threat, spawning a harder Nemesis mission with better rewards. There is no resource "spend" — the cost is the Worm's life and the tactical risk of the encounter. Higher-tier intel (warchiefs, the Overlord) is gated behind story progress (e.g., capturing the region's first fortress).

**Developer material.** Monolith lead systems designer Chris Hoge's GDC 2018 talk "Helping Players Hate (or Love) Their Nemesis" (freely on the GDC YouTube/Vault) documents the design philosophy of the expanded Nemesis System — creating "Orcs that you loved to hate and Orcs you hated to love" — though it centers on relationship/emotion design rather than intel plumbing specifically.

**Assessment: natural extension.** The intel/interrogation loop is a supporting mechanic layered onto the pre-existing procedural-antagonist Nemesis System; it is an information-economy wrapper (fog-of-war over a roster of persistent NPCs) rather than a fundamentally new social simulation.

### 4. RimWorld — Ideology DLC ideoligions + the Tales/art system (Ludeon, Tynan Sylvester, 2021)

**The belief layer.** Per the RimWorld wiki, the Ideology DLC was "released on July 20, 2021. The DLC managed to run out of Steam keys for www.rimworldgame.com buyers within an hour." It gives every person a belief system built from **memes** (high-level ideas with an "impact" tier) that require/forbid/unlock **precepts** (specific rules dictating reactions, e.g., "Cannibalism: Preferred," "Execution: Required") and **rituals**. An ideoligion also has a Structure, Culture, styles, and lore (these are cosmetic, no gameplay effect). Ideoligions can be "fluid," gaining Development Points to reform over time (10 points for the first reformation, +2 each subsequent).

**Certainty and conversion — distinct from mood.** Each pawn has a per-pawn **Certainty** score in their ideoligion. Conversion works by lowering Certainty: a converted pawn starts at 50% Certainty; when Certainty hits 0% the pawn converts to a *random* ideoligion (not necessarily the intended one). The wiki gives the formula: "Certainty loss = 6% × Conversion Power of the Converter × (×2 if Moral Guide) × Global Certainty Loss Factor of Convertee × Relic Conversion Power Factor × Trait/Meme Agreeableness × Storyteller Low-pop boost." Pawns naturally attempt conversion in conversation (governed by Ideoligion Spread Chance, boosted by the Proselytizer precept); a Moral Guide has a "Convert" ability on a 3-day cooldown; prisoners can be set to Convert. Set-percentage reductions come from the Conversion ritual (effective = −30% Certainty; masterful = instant conversion; poor = *increases* Certainty), a "Crisis of belief" mental break (−50%), and Brainwipe (Certainty → 0%). This is explicitly a collective belief layer that interacts with, but is mechanically separate from, individual mood (following one's ideoligion, wearing its color, and rituals give mood buffs; violating precepts gives mood penalties).

**The Tales/art system.** Per the RimWorld wiki, a "Tale" is a RimWorld object recorded when something relevant happens in the colony (killings, drunkenness, someone landing in drop pods, artwork created, ore struck, person captured/recruited, animal hunted, surgery, someone on fire, someone went psychotic, etc.). When a pawn creates an artwork, the artwork stores a **taleRef** (a seed + a tale ID) rather than a static description; the description text is *generated at runtime* from that seed and tale. Community save-file analysis confirms the description is not saved: "It's generated every time you play... based on a random seed and a Tale... You can change the seed... or change the tale field to null to make it describe no event at all." Artworks can alternatively depict something random unrelated to any event ("this sculpture bears a portrayal of a cube"). RimWorld's own wiki notes the art description system "is based on the DF art system."

**How this differs from Dwarf Fortress's engraving/provenance pattern.** Both reference recorded history, but the mechanical binding differs. In Dwarf Fortress, engravings and artifacts are provenance objects: the game records a persistent historical event and the depicting object references that specific historical figure/event as durable world-state, and artifacts carry named, world-persistent histories (who made them, what they depict, where they traveled). In RimWorld, the artwork stores only a lightweight taleRef (seed + tale ID) and re-generates the prose each load; the Tale is a colony-local event record, not a world-historical figure with persistent legend/provenance across sites. DF's system is the deeper provenance/history-object model; RimWorld's is a lighter, seed-driven descriptive reference to a local event log.

**Assessment: genuinely distinct** for ideoligions (a real collective-belief simulation layer distinct from per-pawn mood); the Tales/art system is an acknowledged, lighter-weight descendant of DF's art/provenance pattern.

### 5. Dwarf Fortress — Villains update (v0.47, 2020)

**Release.** Per the DF Wiki Development arc, "The villains release (first part) (0.47.01)" first shipped January 29, 2020, and "added villains to the game, giving them goals and the ability to subvert other people into various corruption activities such as embezzlement, sabotage, kidnapping or assassination."

**World-gen intrigue networks.** Per Tarn Adams's Steam devlog ("Development Progress: Plots and Villains"), the villainous plots are resolved "in history" during world generation: "Assassination, sabotage, embezzlement, theft, coups, corruption, bribery, counter-intelligence and surveillance, with mercenaries, merchant companies, guilds, religions, and new relationships." Per the DF wiki, "The main mechanic works through agreements: historical figures reach out to one another and use a variety of tactics (persuasion, blackmail, bribes) to reach an agreement for a variety of things like funding, assassination, stealing." Historical figures maintain a list of desired plots. Networks ("organisations") have a leader, members, and corrupted "assets" (position-holders providing funds); these can be exported as a graph in Legends mode. Civilizations have abstract counter-intelligence (spymasters, law enforcement) that collect evidence from witnesses, interrogation, and surveillance to generate leads. A concrete devlog example: "Bax Doomedwane... became the chief executioner in town... soon had the harvest administrator embezzling money for her... corrupted the local magistrate and turned her eyes to thievery."

**Fortress-mode interrogation.** Members of these networks visit the fortress to corrupt residents. Per the DF wiki: "Unless you successfully interrogate them through the Justice screen, there will be no indication that the visitor has nefarious intentions; they may also use one or more pseudonyms." Corruption success depends on the initiator's and recipient's social skills; interrogation likewise uses social skills (persuade/intimidate). The v0.47 release notes explicitly list "Traitor dwarves, artifact heists, interrogation for crime from the justice screen, counterintelligence readout with organization charts." The feature was shipped incomplete — the v0.47 release was "cut short, leaving out most of the fortress mode and adventure mode villainy, to hasten the Premium release."

**The loyalty-cascade failure mode — the cautionary case study.** Per the DF wiki (Faction page), verbatim: "Loyalty cascades are the result of entity members attacking each other. The conflict will escalate into a full civil war (hence Toady One referring to it as the 'civil war bug') and won't resolve until one side of the conflict is wiped out." The mechanism: anything that attacks a member of a civilization becomes an enemy of that civilization; but when the attacker is *itself* a member of the same civ, everyone nearby becomes its enemy — and since those attackers are also members, anyone who then attacks *them* becomes a civ-enemy too. Dwarves fracture into "separatists" (loyal to civ, not fort), "loyalists" (don't follow orders), and "renegades" (a separatist/loyalist who killed a citizen — total enemies). The classic trigger relevant to forced compliance: ordering the execution of a citizen — or sending the military to kill a citizen who has turned werebeast/berserk — is treated as *assaulting a citizen*, so the loyal executioners lose loyalty and are hunted as traitors, cascading further. Recovery requires wiping out one side or the DFHack `fix/loyaltycascade` tool. Real forts report 50–80+ dead from a single cascade started by a tavern brawl or a toddler's tantrum.

**Assessment.** The intrigue network is a genuinely distinct world-gen-layer system (persistent villain agents, agreements, counter-intelligence). The loyalty cascade is *not* a designed feature but an emergent failure mode of ordinary faction-membership logic — precisely the cautionary example for any mechanic that makes an NPC "attack a member of their own group": binary in-group/out-group flags with recursive re-tagging can self-amplify into total collapse.

### 6. Watch Dogs: Legion — "Play as Anyone" (Ubisoft Toronto, released October 29, 2020)

**The backbone: Census.** Per Ubisoft Toronto's GDC talk "Census: The Systemic Backbone Behind Play As Anyone" and an in-depth Game Developer/AI-and-Games interview with the dev team (Liz England, team lead game designer for Play as Anyone; Jurie Horneman, mission-systems lead programmer; Martin Walsh, technology director for gameplay & AI), every pedestrian is procedurally generated at runtime. Census is "a knowledge base" that fills in a plausible life from a starting fact: "if you see somebody who is a bartender, census knows roughly what a bartender makes and what kind of place this person could live." Each character spawns with tags (gender, fashion, occupation, income, personality) which then seed a schedule (work location, a home matching income, hobbies), relationships (spouse, sibling, best friend, clients), and daily activities.

**Schedules are real and simulated.** Walsh: "they really live at that address, they will really physically go to that address and sleep there in the night." Schedules fill a 24-hour day; when a character isn't loaded, the game "fully simulate[s] the path they take" so the player can intercept them mid-route. Metro stations act as fast-travel nodes for NPCs. Interfering with a character (e.g., hospitalizing them) rewrites their schedule and propagates to friends/family ("if you go and murder their husband... instead of going out to eat, they're gonna mourn their husband").

**The recruitment grievance chain — assembled at uprez, not from a pre-simulated global graph.** Characters exist in tiers: on-screen spawns, a "fully uprezzed" simulation layer (anyone added to your database or cast in a mission), and a lower layer for incidental contacts and relatives. A background pedestrian who is ignored "will simply be deleted once you're far enough away." Only when the player takes interest is a character "uprezzed" — at which point Census generates their full profile, backstory, Dedsec affinity, relationships, schedule, and crucially "some sort of problem that's uniquely purposed to Dedsec to help them with." Recruitment missions are *not* fully procedurally generated stories; they are authored templates defined as abstract "gameplay beats" with actors and locations filled at runtime ("go hack a server in a pub... find the nearest pub with a server"). So the grievance is **assembled fresh per-pedestrian at recruitment time from Census-generated attributes**, not drawn from a persistent, fully pre-simulated global social graph.

**But there is persistent per-character memory and an "alibi"/recasting layer.** Walsh: characters "have permanent memories so they remember things you've done for them or against them, those memories propagate to their enemies and friends." A "memory system" records player actions and injects them back as the *reason* a character has a given Dedsec opinion — mirroring Ben Sunshine-Hill's "alibi generation" (which Walsh cites as an inspiration): "we've actually spawned somebody who doesn't like Dedsec, now let's give them a reason why." Opinion tiers (Hates / Dislikes / Neutral / Supports Dedsec) gate recruitment: a disliking recruit requires two extra tasks (found via the Deep Profiler or via their acquaintances) to unlock the recruitment mission; a hating NPC can become unrecruitable and even trigger a procedurally-launched revenge mission. Recasting lets an already-uprezzed acquaintance be "shoehorned" into later events/missions ("does the player know any lawyers?").

**Assessment: genuinely distinct.** The combination of runtime Census generation, on-demand uprezzing, real simulated schedules with propagating consequences, and alibi/memory-driven grievance assembly is a substantially new approach — closer to just-in-time generation of a social graph than to maintaining a full persistent one.

## Recommendations

**If the goal is a multi-phase regional conflict template:** Model it on CK3's **Struggle**, not a binary questline. Concretely: (1) define 3–5 named phases with a directed transition graph (Opportunity→Hostility/Conciliation→Compromise→Opportunity is a proven cyclic shape); (2) drive transitions by an *accumulator* ("Catalyst"-style points) that different actor actions push toward competing next-phases, with a threshold (the Iberian Struggle uses 1,000; the later Iranian Intermezzo uses a lower 500-point threshold for its instability/stability/lull phases) and a short commit delay (CK3 uses 3 months) rather than a fixed timeline; (3) have each phase *gate the available action set* and re-tune costs of existing actions rather than adding bespoke content; (4) provide multiple explicit Ending Decisions with large one-time rewards. **Benchmark that should change the design:** watch for CK3's documented failure where one phase becomes unreachable at scale (Conciliation dominance) — if one actor class can flood the accumulator, add diminishing returns or per-phase caps.

**If the goal is a spreading belief/reputation object:** Use CK3 **Legends** as the template — a typed, named object that diffuses from an origin and paints *active per-province modifiers* while in progress, with tiered thresholds (100/300 baronies) gating quality upgrades and a maintenance cost to sustain spread. **Threshold to revisit:** CK3 Legends became trivially strong in large realms; if your spread cost scales sub-linearly with holdings, the mechanic will trivialize — tie maintenance or spread difficulty to realm size or a "language/culture barrier" friction term.

**If the goal is per-agent belief distinct from mood:** Adopt RimWorld's **Certainty** pattern — a 0–100% per-agent scalar, converted at 0%, with conversion driven by a multiplicative formula (converter power × role multiplier × target resistance × situational factors) and set-percentage shocks (rituals, crises). Keep it separate from the mood system but let them cross-feed (belief adherence → mood buffs; low mood → faster certainty loss).

**If the goal is intel/fog-of-war over persistent NPCs:** The Nemesis **Worm** loop is the pattern — single-use informants that each reveal one target's exploitable weaknesses, with acquisition cost being tactical exposure rather than an abstract resource, and deeper intel gated behind progression.

**If the goal is just-in-time social generation at scale:** Follow **Legion/Census** — generate background NPCs cheaply and delete them when ignored; "uprez" to a full profile (schedule, relationships, grievance) only on player interest; assemble recruitment hooks from generated attributes at recruitment time; and add a persistent per-character memory + "alibi" layer so consequences and grievances feel authored without maintaining a full global simulation.

**Universal cautionary rule (from DF):** For *any* faction-membership or forced-compliance mechanic, never let "attack a member of your own group" recursively re-tag attackers as enemies without a damping/terminating condition. DF's loyalty cascade shows binary in-group/out-group flags with recursive re-tagging can self-amplify into unrecoverable civil war from a trivial trigger (a tavern brawl, a toddler's tantrum, an ordered execution). Add hysteresis, a "this is a sanctioned punishment" exemption, or a hard cap on cascade depth.

## Caveats
- **CK3 Legends spread model:** Paradox has not published a numeric spread-chance formula or an explicit hard county-adjacency rule; the plague-like center-point diffusion is strongly implied by dev diaries and reviewers but not stated as a formal algorithm. The 100/300-barony upgrade thresholds and per-county modifier values are from the CK3 wiki (verify against your patch version, as Paradox rebalances).
- **CK3 Struggle numbers:** The four phase names and cyclic structure are confirmed by Dev Diary #94/#99 and the wiki; the "1,000 Catalyst points" and "3 months" transition specifics come from the wiki/TheGamer guide and vary by struggle type — later situations reuse the framework with different phase names and thresholds (the Iranian Intermezzo uses a 500-point threshold with instability/stability/lull phases; the Chinese "Dynastic Cycle" uses Advancement/Tension/Expansion/Division "eras").
- **Shadow of War:** Precise, formula-level intel numbers aren't published by Monolith; mechanics here are from the Shadow of War wiki and multiple guide sources, corroborated. The GDC 2018 talk documents philosophy more than the intel plumbing.
- **RimWorld:** The Certainty formula and tale mechanics are from the RimWorld wiki and save-file analysis; exact coefficients change across versions. The DF-vs-RimWorld provenance comparison is a mechanical characterization, not a quoted developer statement.
- **Dwarf Fortress:** v0.47 villainy shipped deliberately incomplete; some fortress-mode intrigue interactions were unfinished at that version. The loyalty cascade is widely documented as a bug/emergent behavior, partially mitigated over versions and by DFHack, not a designed feature.
- **Watch Dogs: Legion:** Details are from a developer interview and GDC talk; Ubisoft has not published the internal grievance-generation code, so "assembled fresh at uprez" is the developers' described behavior, not a reverse-engineered spec.
