## Executive summary (bottom line up front)

**ADR-0011's core principle — "the engine decides, the model renders" — is not a leap into the unknown. It is the consensus pattern that three independent research traditions converged on across twenty years, and it has now been demonstrated end-to-end with an actual LLM in an actual playable game.** Comme il Faut/Prom Week (2012) and Versu (2013–2014) proved the symbolic half: a rule-driven social simulation can decide what is sayable and gate authored content on belief state. CICERO (2022) proved the neural half at production scale: a strategic engine computes intents and a controllable language model renders them, with measurable improvements in groundedness from that conditioning. And *Slice of Life* (FDG 2025) — from the same UCSC lineage as Prom Week — is almost exactly Chronicle's architecture: a symbolic social simulation whose state is rendered into dialogue by an LLM that is used **strictly one-way**, as a surface realizer ([ACM FDG 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3723498.3723806)).

**Where Chronicle genuinely has no precedent is its second move: committed dialogue lines feeding back into the simulation as provenance-tracked claims that propagate and mutate.** The closest systems to that bet are *Talk of the Town* (2016) — whose NPCs originate, propagate, mutate, and forget beliefs, including deliberate lies, and whose dialogue is generated from that belief graph — and, at commercial scale, *Dwarf Fortress*'s witness-originated rumor system. But Talk of the Town renders with a context-free grammar rather than an LLM and never shipped commercially, and Dwarf Fortress presents state through menus, not embodied conversation. Nobody has shipped the combination Chronicle is proposing. That is a genuine differentiator, but it also means the feedback loop — not the rendering — is where the engineering risk concentrates, and where the confirm-vs-autoplay open question deserves the most care. CICERO's published numbers are the caution: even with intent conditioning, **24% of candidate messages were filtered out** for contradicting the intent they were conditioned on ([CICERO supplementary material](https://hughbzhang.com/data/diplomacy.pdf)).

## 2. Talk of the Town (James Ryan, UC Santa Cruz, 2013–2018)

### 2.1 What it is and how dialogue is rendered

*Talk of the Town* is the closest academic precedent to Chronicle's full design, and the prompt's instinct was correct: it should have been reviewed earlier. It is a life-simulation engine in which roughly a town's worth of NPCs live simulated daily routines over 140 years of generated history, building up **subjective, per-character knowledge** of the world as they go. Its dialogue system, published at AIIDE 2016, explicitly rejects both branching dialogue trees and the classical NLG pipeline in favor of authored **context-free grammars whose productions carry metadata**, expanded by an unusual "middle-out" procedure that targets mid-level symbols and traverses the grammar by forward *and* backward chaining, **expanding symbols conditionally by testing against current game state** ([Ryan, Mateas & Wardrip-Fruin, AIIDE 2016](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12877)). So to answer the prompt's first question directly: generation was **templated/grammar-based, not LLM**, and — critically — the grammar cannot invent facts, because every expansion is conditioned on the NPC's actual belief state. The NPC can be *wrong* (its beliefs may be mutated or confabulated), but the renderer faithfully expresses those beliefs; invention happens at the knowledge layer, by explicit, inspectable rules, never at the language layer.

The knowledge framework underneath is the part most directly relevant to Chronicle's claims.py design. The companion paper lays it out as a lifecycle with named mechanisms: knowledge **originates** via observation, transference (mistaken identity by feature overlap), **confabulation** (unintentional invention, drawn from the town's actual feature distributions), and **lies** (intentional statements of things the speaker does not believe); it **propagates** via statements and eavesdropping at set probabilities; it **deteriorates** via **mutation** governed by a hand-authored per-facet schema — e.g. a "brown hair" belief mutates to "black" or "red" with higher probability than to "white" — modulated by a per-character memory attribute; and it **terminates** via forgetting, again modulated by memory and salience ([Toward Characters Who Observe, Tell, Misremember, and Lie](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12825/12825-52-16341-1-2-20201228.pdf)). Beliefs are structured as an ontology of interlinked per-entity mental models, and the authors explicitly note the representation is agnostic enough to support metaknowledge — beliefs about the source, time, and location of where a belief was acquired — which is precisely provenance tracking, described as future work they never fully built out. Chronicle's claim provenance/confidence model is, in effect, that promised extension, implemented.

### 2.2 Fate, availability, and LLM-era continuation

The engine was never shipped as a conventional commercial game. Its playable offspring was *Bad News*, an award-winning live-performance piece in which the simulation is generated fresh per show, a hidden human "wizard" sifts the town state for narratively charged situations, and a live actor improvises each NPC **constrained to the underlying simulation** — personality, life history, and knowledge — fed through a discreet interface ([Bad News paper](https://www.academia.edu/21609903/Bad_News_A_Game_Of_Death_And_Communication), [Narrative Instruments, ICIDS 2021](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/NarrativeInstruments_ICIDS2021.pdf)). Ryan's dissertation reframes the whole endeavor as "curationist emergent narrative": simulations produce nonfiction-like material, and something — a human wizard, or an automated sifting system — must curate it into tellable stories ([Curating Simulated Storyworlds, 2018](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1340j5h2)). That framing is worth sitting with: it is an argument that even a perfect social simulation does not produce dialogue worth hearing without a selection layer on top — which in Chronicle's terms is the menu-ranking open question, restated by the field's foremost practitioner.

Two practical facts matter for Chronicle. First, **the code is public**: the simulation engine and an earlier testbed are on GitHub under Ryan's account ([james-owen-ryan/talktown](https://github.com/james-owen-ryan/talktown), [talktown-testbed](https://github.com/james-owen-ryan/talktown-testbed)), so the mutation schemas, salience-gating, and propagation probabilities can be read as working code rather than reconstructed from papers. Second, on whether anyone has rebuilt or extended this with an LLM: not in public, as far as this research found — but Ryan himself has moved into the LLM era with a different division of labor. His recent proprietary story engines (Karavani, and Arnie at Hexagram) are beat systems in the Façade lineage where beats are structured as goal sets with triggers, "and the triggers are defined in natural language and evaluated by an LLM that has access to all chat transcripts" — in his own words, "a symbolic AI system… but with LLMs swapped in to evaluate conditions — a brittleness point for a purely symbolic approach when used in domains like conversation" ([jamesryan.computer](https://jamesryan.computer/)). That is the LLM-as-judge pattern, not LLM-as-renderer: the LLM evaluates whether symbolic conditions hold rather than generating world content. It is a data point that the person who built the strongest pre-LLM belief-propagation system, given LLMs, chose to use them as *evaluators* inside a production system — and still kept the beat structure symbolic.

## 3. Comme il Faut, Prom Week, and the Ensemble lineage

### 3.1 How simulated state selects what can be said

Comme il Faut (CiF) is the canonical "social physics" engine, and its loop is the cleanest pre-LLM statement of Chronicle's principle. Characters perceive a symbolic **social state** (traits, relationships, non-reciprocal scalar "social networks" of feelings, and a time-ordered **Social Facts Database** recording every prior exchange); a large rulebase of **influence rules** — each a social condition with a scalar weight — is evaluated to compute each character's **volition** toward each available **social exchange**; the playable experience picks an exchange; CiF then computes whether the responder accepts or rejects the intent via a second scoring pass; the most **salient instantiation** (an authored performance with its own preconditions — salience correlating with how much social state the authored content renders) is selected and customized with NLG templates; **effect changes** update the social state; and finally **trigger rules** fire to model unforeseen consequences, setting up the next round ([McCoy et al., Social Story Worlds with Comme il Faut, IEEE TCIAIG 2014](http://www.ben-samuel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/TCIAIG-social-story-worlds-with-comme-il-faut.pdf)). Generation inventing facts is not a coherent concept here: the words are authored instantiations, and the *selection* of which authored words appear is a pure function of simulation state. What CiF contributes conceptually is the separation that Chronicle's menu design already embodies: **the set of things a character can plausibly say right now is a computed ranking over a closed vocabulary of intents**, and the computation is inspectable — Prom Week's UI literally shows the player the volition-ranked list the engine produced.

Two extensions in the lineage are directly load-bearing for Chronicle. The **Ensemble Engine** (2015), CiF's publicly released successor in JavaScript, generalized the action structure specifically because Prom Week's authors had wanted a **Spread Rumors** exchange — an action taking an initiator, a responder, *and a third-party victim* — and CiF's two-party architecture couldn't express it ([Samuel et al., The Ensemble Engine, FDG 2015](http://www.fdg2015.org/papers/fdg2015_paper_07.pdf)). That is worth pausing on: the most sophisticated social-physics engine family in the academic literature treated rumor-spreading as a *desirable social action with weighted volition*, but modeled only its social effects, not the rumor as a propagating, mutating data structure — Chronicle's claim graph goes further than any of these systems attempted. Separately, Ryan, Walker and Wardrip-Fruin's *Toward Recombinant Dialogue* (2014) documented CiF's known weakness honestly: the authored-instantiation bottleneck meant the surface text couldn't keep up with the richness of the underlying state, a problem they proposed solving by recombining annotated dialogue segments ([Toward Recombinant Dialogue](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~maw/papers/9255-40158-2-PB.pdf)). That unsolved rendering bottleneck — social state richer than any feasible authored line-bank — is precisely the gap Chronicle's LLM rendering tier is meant to close, and §7 shows how the same lab eventually closed it.

### 3.2 The Skyrim datapoint: CIF-CK

Buried in the literature is a result Chronicle should treat as unusually relevant: **CiF has already been ported into Skyrim**. Guimaraes and Santos' CIF-CK architecture adapted CiF's four components into Creation Kit structures and shipped the result as the "Social NPCs" mod on the Steam Workshop and Nexus Mods in 2017; the authors report positive player reception and surveys supporting improved engagement, and note the architecture required no access to Skyrim's internals — it grafts social reasoning onto an existing commercial game ([Prom Week Meets Skyrim](https://www.games.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2017/05/prom-week-meets.pdf), [CiF-CK, IEEE TCIAIG](http://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8080425)). CIF-CK even added a CiF feature Chronicle already has: characters act on their **perceived** social state, which may diverge from ground truth ("Sarah thinks John likes her; she acts accordingly").

The significance is feasibility evidence, not design guidance. CIF-CK proved that an external social-simulation layer with volition-ranked interactions can be bolted onto Skyrim's engine and that players respond well to it — using only scripted interactions and vanilla dialogue assets. Chronicle is attempting something much deeper (belief graphs, propagating claims, LLM rendering, voice), but the specific doubt "can a social-physics overlay work inside Skyrim at all, and do players notice?" has an affirmative published answer.

## 4. Versu (Richard Evans & Emily Short, 2011–2014)

### 4.1 Architecture: belief-gated quips over a social model

Versu modeled each character with beliefs, desires, and inclinations on top of a logic-programming core (the Praxis DSL, with Graham Nelson's Prompter language for authoring), and its published design goal was explicitly *simulationist*: social outcomes like a romance or an enmity "can occur even if the author did not write an arc specific to those two characters" ([versu.com/about](https://versu.com/about/about-versu/), [Evans & Short, Versu — A Simulationist Storytelling System, IEEE TCIAIG 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263050997_Versu-A_Simulationist_Storytelling_System)). Conversation was organized around **social practices** — there is always at least one social situation in force, which gates which affordances are contextually appropriate — and a simplified version of Sacks' turn-taking systematics: conversations tracked a *selected speaker*, a *set of salient topics*, and a *selected speech act*, with norm violations (interrupting, ignoring a direct question, changing the subject) noticed and reacted to as social events ([Emily Short, Versu: Conversation Implementation](https://emshort.blog/2013/02/26/versu-conversation-implementation/)).

The mechanism Chronicle should study hardest is the **quip gating**. Dialogue pieces ("quips") could be unique to a speaker or generally speakable; could directly or indirectly follow other quips; could introduce topics or only continue them; and — the key rule — **a quip conveying factual information was speakable only if the speaking character believed that information to be true**. Quips carried factual, evaluative, and emotive payloads, and listeners responded through general systems: characters who heard a negative evaluation of themselves could get angry, listeners could accept stated facts and **incorporate them into their own world view** ("from then on other characters may also view Ryan as an inferior student"), and beliefs could be declared to *imply* other beliefs ([Emily Short, Versu: Conversation Implementation](https://emshort.blog/2013/02/26/versu-conversation-implementation/)). This is spoken lines writing back into per-character belief state, in a commercially shipped (if short-lived) product, in 2013. Versu also practiced an early form of what Ryan's dissertation later formalized as **causal bookkeeping**: characters "could record a string that represented why they'd adopted a particular attitude towards the player" — provenance for attitudes, exactly the thing Chronicle wants to trace in its dashboard ([Emily Short on Curating Simulated Storyworlds, ch. 4–5](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/23/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan-2/)).

### 4.2 What happened to it, and how well it's documented

The history is worth stating precisely because it is often garbled. Linden Lab acquired Short and Evans' studio (Little TextPeople) in 2012, launched Versu on iPad in February 2013, then cancelled it in a February 2014 product review and refused to sell the IP back — at which point the 240,000-word flagship *Blood & Laurels* was three days from launch ([Gamasutra/GameDeveloper, "The end of Versu"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-end-of-versu-emily-short-looks-back)). Linden subsequently reversed course enough to allow *Blood & Laurels* to release in June 2014 under a new arrangement, but the engine's broader future died there; the apps were later removed from the App Store, and the codebase remains locked in Linden's vault ([Modemworld Versu coverage](https://modemworld.me/tag/versu/)). So: shipped commercially, yes; extant, no.

Documentation quality is nonetheless high — arguably the best-documented of any system in this report for a working designer's purposes. There is a peer-reviewed architecture paper ([Evans & Short 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263050997_Versu-A_Simulationist_Storytelling_System)), Emily Short's detailed public authoring notes on the conversation model ([emshort.blog](https://emshort.blog/2013/02/26/versu-conversation-implementation/)), her earlier taxonomy of conversation models (topic/fact/quip/effect as separable base units) that reads today like a design checklist for grounding dialogue in state ([Emily Short, "Conversation"](https://emshort.blog/how-to-play/writing-if/my-articles/conversation/)), and a formal reconstruction of its logic DSL published years later ([Praxish, AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310/31588)). The lesson Chronicle should take from the fate, though, is not technical: Versu demonstrates that belief-gated authored dialogue at this fidelity is **possible but authorially expensive** — 240,000 words for ~7% visibility per playthrough — which is itself an argument for a rendering tier that does not scale linearly with writer-hours.

## 5. CICERO (Meta AI, 2022) — verified, with two important nuances

### 5.1 The claimed separation is real

The prompt asked for verification rather than headline-trusting, and the primary sources hold up. CICERO couples a **strategic reasoning engine** — a planning algorithm (piKL variants) over RL-trained value/policy functions, anchored to a dialogue-conditional behavioral-cloning policy to stay human-compatible — with a **controllable dialogue model**, a 2.7B-parameter BART-like transformer fine-tuned on 12.9M messages from 40,408 human webDiplomacy games ([Science 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ade9097), [Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/cicero-ai-negotiates-persuades-and-cooperates-with-people/)). The interface between them is exactly the one ADR-0011 posits: planning outputs **intents** — a set of planned actions for CICERO and its conversation partner, current turn plus a short future rollout — and the dialogue model was trained, via automatically annotated intents on the human corpus, to be *conditioned* on such intents at inference time. The paper's own words: "the intents provided an interface between the symbolic reasoning and neural dialogue generation," and conditioning on proposed actions "relieved the dialogue model of most of the responsibility for learning which actions are legal and strategic" ([CICERO supplementary material](https://hughbzhang.com/data/diplomacy.pdf)).

The measured effect of this grounding is the strongest published evidence that Chronicle's principle *works*, not just that it's principled. In expert annotation of generated messages in 126 game situations, moving from an ungrounded language model to game-state grounding raised "consistent with game state" from 61.9% to 84.1%, and adding intent grounding (full CICERO) raised it to 87.3%, with "consistent with plan" at 92.9% and notably-high-quality ratings nearly doubling from 20.6% to 37.3% ([CICERO supplementary material](https://hughbzhang.com/data/diplomacy.pdf)). Across 40 anonymous online league games against humans, the system scored more than double the average human and ranked in the top 10% of multi-game participants — so the architecture is not merely safe, it is competitively effective ([Science 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ade9097)).

### 5.2 Nuance one: the conditioning is lossy, so CICERO ships a verification layer

Intent conditioning is imperfect by the team's own accounting, and the mitigation is the most directly borrowable mechanism in this whole report. "Every so often the dialogue model generates messages which contradict the intents the message is conditioned on," so generated candidates pass through an **intent-correspondence filter**: an action-prediction model scores whether the candidate message makes the conditioned intent *less* likely, and messages failing the check are discarded — on a 1,013-message expert test set this filter ran at 65% recall while **filtering 24% of all generated messages**, alongside nonsense filters and a value-based filter that rejected candidates which measurably lowered CICERO's expected score ([CICERO supplementary material](https://hughbzhang.com/data/diplomacy.pdf)). Read that number against Chronicle's open question #1: even the best-resourced grounded-dialogue system ever published needed to reject roughly **one in four** raw model outputs before committing them. Unfiltered autoplay of LLM-rendered lines into a propagating claim graph is not a defensible default; either a human confirm step or an automated consistency gate — ideally both, with the filter as first line and the player as second — is what the prior art supports.

### 5.3 Nuance two: it is not a pure one-way renderer, and its honesty is a design choice

Two subtleties separate CICERO from Chronicle's stricter separation. First, dialogue feeds *back* into CICERO's planning: the anchor policies are dialogue-conditional, so received messages reshape the predicted policies of other players, and intents are recomputed after every message — the loop is closed, but closed through a *planner*, not through a world-state claim store ([CICERO supplementary material](https://hughbzhang.com/data/diplomacy.pdf)). Second, CICERO is deliberately **honest**: its intents are its true intended actions at time of speaking (it may change its mind later), and the dialogue training data was filtered with a learned lie detector that removed the 5% most deceptive turns — the system was engineered to *not* exploit the gap between saying and intending, a gap Diplomacy's human players exploit constantly ([CICERO supplementary material](https://hughbzhang.com/data/diplomacy.pdf), [LessWrong technical discussion](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/3TCYqur9YzuZ4qhtq/meta-ai-announces-cicero-human-level-diplomacy-play-with)). A later independent analysis confirmed the architectural reading: Cicero's messages are generated from a conditional language model given its internal move intents, which the analysts could log directly as "initial intents" for commitment-keeping measurements ([More Victories, Less Cooperation, arXiv 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04643v1)).

For Chronicle the transfer is: **intent-conditioned rendering plus an output-side consistency filter is a validated, quantified pattern; strategic deception must be modeled at the intent layer (choosing what claim to make), not left to emerge from the renderer.** Talk of the Town's explicit "lie" origination type is the right precedent for deception in a social sim: lying is an engine-level event with provenance, not a tone the LLM improvises.

## 6. Façade (Mateas & Stern, 2005)

### 6.1 The architecture, briefly and precisely

Façade remains the canonical attempt at free-text input against a closed authored response space. Player text was interpreted by forward-chaining Jess rules into **discourse acts**; **reaction proposers** re-interpreted those acts in context (agreement, provocation, alliance); and the drama manager sequenced **beats** — each a bag of 10–100 **joint dialog behaviors** (JDBs) written in the ABL reactive-planning language, where Grace and Trip's behaviors literally negotiate with each other before executing coordinated exchanges. Beats carried canonical goal sequences plus **handlers** that could add, remove, or reorder beat goals and interject **mix-in** JDBs responding to player input ([Mateas & Stern, Structuring Content in the Façade Interactive Drama Architecture, AIIDE 2005](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18722), [Writing Façade: A Case Study in Procedural Authorship](https://electronicbookreview.com/publications/writing-facade-a-case-study-in-procedural-authorship/)). Scale numbers worth internalizing: ~2,500 JDBs, 27 beats, ~15 seen per ~20-minute run, at most 25% of content visible per playthrough, built by essentially two people over years ([GameDeveloper, The Story of Facade](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-story-of-facade-the-ai-powered-interactive-drama)).

### 6.2 How well it worked, and the post-LLM revisits

The honest assessment comes from its own creator: the NLU layer worked "maybe half the time" in Andrew Stern's retrospective phrasing, after a year spent building the custom rule engine to parse player language ([Voices of VR interview with Andrew Stern](https://voicesofvr.com/293-ai-the-future-of-interactive-drama/)). That is the brittleness data point: free text into a hand-built rule grammar gives you a coin-flip comprehension rate at a cost of a person-year, for a 20-minute experience. No successor shipped. The planned follow-up ("The Party") was abandoned by 2013, and the architecture was never reproduced — "an interesting hot pot of tools, systems, and languages that has never really been reproduced in the same way. Largely I suspect because of the literal years taken to build it" ([GameDeveloper, The Story of Facade](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-story-of-facade-the-ai-powered-interactive-drama)).

What the LLM era did with Façade's pattern is clarifying rather than resurrecting. A 2026 survey of AI-native games treats Façade explicitly as a *historical precursor* whose symbolic NLU and drama management "demonstrated that player language, character response, and dramatic progression could serve as the primary interactive material" — but excludes it from the post-LLM corpus on precisely those grounds ([AI-Native Games: A Survey and Roadmap, arXiv 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00527v2)). The actual modern revisitation of the beat architecture is James Ryan's proprietary work (§2.2): keep the symbolic beat/production structure, but let an LLM *evaluate natural-language triggers* against chat transcripts — converting Façade's brittleness point (rules trying to parse language) into the LLM's strength, while keeping narrative structure symbolic and authored ([jamesryan.computer](https://jamesryan.computer/)). For Chronicle's open question #6 (the free-text advanced path), the combined lesson is sharp: the failure mode of free-text input was never the input itself but the **hand-built NLU mapping** onto a closed space — and the validated fix is to let the model do the *interpretation* (into closed intents, with abstention) while the engine keeps authorship of the response space. That is a very different design from letting the model free-generate replies, and prompt 41 covers its feasibility directly.

## 7. The post-2023 LLM-era landscape: the principle, demonstrated

### 7.1 Slice of Life (FDG 2025): the closest thing to Chronicle that exists

This is the single most important find of the research pass, and it postdates anything report 37 could have covered. *Slice of Life* is a playable social-physics game built on **Ensemble with Social Practices (ESP)** — the direct descendant of CiF (§3) — in which a deterministic symbolic social simulation (schema-defined fact classes, a time-ordered social record, microtheories of weighted social considerations, volition scoring, strict turn-taking practices with intent-tagged actions) drives gameplay, and an **LLM generates one line of dialogue per selected action** from a prompt assembled out of symbolically retrieved game state ([Slice of Life, ACM FDG 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3723498.3723806)). The design rule is stated in Chronicle's own terms: "keeping all state symbolically grounded, and using the LLM solely to do surface-text realization of that symbolic state," with dialogue that explicitly "**does not feed back in to the symbolic simulation state**" — a deliberately one-way usage chosen to preserve interpretability, consistency, agency, and authorial control. They even articulate the same rejection of prompt-as-memory that report 37 concluded independently, contrasting their approach with Smallville/Generative Agents, where "LLM-generated text [is] the simulation representation itself."

Two details deserve direct borrowing. First, the **prompt contract**: each generated line is conditioned on the practice name, prior dialogue, the speaker's intent, the *preconditions that made the action eligible*, the microtheory considerations that contributed (and detracted from) its volition, the action's effects on social state, speech style, pronouns, and style directives — a deliberately **large input-to-output ratio**, which they cite as their answer to Kreminski's "dearth of the author" problem ([Slice of Life, ACM FDG 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3723498.3723806)). Second, their preliminary three-configuration case study is the only published A/B of grounding depth for this exact task: an "empty" prompt produced bland, interchangeable characters; a "diegetic" prompt (full state, no style directives) produced characterful dialogue with **no hallucinations** ("neither character makes claims that are not grounded in actual game state"); and a further prompt-engineered variant produced the richest dialogue but with **two fabrications** — Claire asserts it is Tuesday and Simon remarks on the weather, neither of which exists in the simulation ([Slice of Life, ACM FDG 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3723498.3723806)). The lesson is counterintuitive and actionable: grounding depth and *task framing* trade off against each other — asking the model to be more vivid reintroduces invention. Where Chronicle differs, deliberately, is the feedback direction: Slice of Life's authors explicitly cut the write-back path for safety, while Chronicle's entire bet is that write-back is safe *if* the line is committed as a structured, provenance-tracked claim rather than as prose. Nothing in this paper refutes that bet; it simply declines to take it.

### 7.2 The formalizers: Web World Models and the AI-native-games roadmap

The same division of labor has now been *formalized*, not just built. The Web World Models paper (December 2025) proposes decomposing world state into a deterministic **Physics Layer** maintained by code — inventories, coordinates, invariants, logical consistency — and a stochastic **Imagination Layer** where "the large language model synthesizes the perceptual layer conditioned on this updated symbolic state," with state transitions computed by code *first* and rendered second ([Web World Models, arXiv 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23676v1)). The analogy the authors reach for is telling: physics engine versus rendering pipeline — which is precisely Chronicle's claims-versus-rendering split. Meanwhile the 2026 AI-Native Games survey, drawing on the post-2019 corpus of LLM games, names the unsolved design problem as "**rule-bounded controllable generation**": a generate-and-verify pipeline where "the model first proposes an event, quest, action interpretation, or rule outcome, and the game system then checks whether it is compatible with the current rules and state," falling back to authored content on validation failure — and explicitly frames the open question as deciding "what the model may invent, what the system must verify, and which rules and states must remain fixed" ([AI-Native Games: A Survey and Roadmap, arXiv 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00527v2)). Chronicle's design is, in effect, an aggressive answer to that framing: the model may invent *wording*, the system verifies *claims*, and the claim graph stays fixed.

The adjacent literature adds useful warnings rather than alternatives. Work on character-consistent NPC dialogue shows the field's default response to "character hallucination" is fine-tuning plus knowledge-graph retrieval (an AMR–KG structure merging player-input semantics with NPC memory) — effective on benchmarks (hallucination-rate reductions around 21% against instruction-tuned baselines) but still probabilistic at the output layer, with the authors conceding models "may still generate inappropriate content, especially in player-guided dialogues" ([Personalized Non-Player Characters, MDPI AI 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-2688/6/5/93)). And the "chatter versus dialogue" distinction — using LLMs only for non-story-critical filler because story-bearing generated text goes off the rails — is cited by Slice of Life as the position their symbolic grounding is designed to move past ([Slice of Life, ACM FDG 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3723498.3723806)). The through-line across all of it: nobody credible is arguing the renderer should decide facts; the disagreement is only over whether, and how, rendered output may re-enter state.

## 8. Provenance-tracked and mutating information in shipped games: the empty quadrant

### 8.1 Dwarf Fortress: the real thing, minus mutation and minus dialogue

The strongest shipped precedent for Chronicle's information model is *Dwarf Fortress*'s knowledge and rumor system (present since the DF2014 era). Rumors originate from **witnesses of actual historical events**; they spread when characters tell each other in conversation, when a knowledgeable character is "offloaded" from a loaded area, or when the player brags, summarizes incidents, performs stories, or presents physical evidence like a corpse part ([DF2014:Rumor, Dwarf Fortress Wiki](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Rumor)). Knowledge degrades realistically at the edges: artifact whereabouts have six graded levels of knowing (holding it yourself → having seen it → "heard so-and-so had it at a location recently" → heard *that* someone has it but not where → vague legends-tier awareness → none), and knowledge **fades over weeks and years** while being continuously reconciled by timestamp at individual, site-government, site-culture, and civilization levels ([DF2014:Rumor](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Rumor), [Knowledge, Dwarf Fortress Wiki](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Knowledge)). This is provenance-graded, witness-originated, time-decaying information spread at commercial scale — the category-3 "simulate hard" cheat (per Chronicle's own taxonomy) taken furthest.

The gaps relative to Chronicle are exactly the interesting ones. Per the wiki, "in the current version, **no false rumors will ever spread**, barring those regarding events involving individuals with secret identities" — there is no mutation of content in transit, no lying mechanism, no confabulation; distortion is limited to identity masking ([DF2014:Rumor](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Rumor)). And none of it is rendered as dialogue: the player's interface to this knowledge web is menus, reports, and legends text. So the game that most faithfully simulates *information with provenance* deliberately sidesteps spoken conversation, and the games that do spoken conversation don't simulate information with provenance. Chronicle is targeting the intersection that both traditions avoided.

### 8.2 Crusader Kings and Heaven's Vault: asymmetry and context-sensitivity, but not propagation

Crusader Kings III's secrets-and-hooks system models **knowledge asymmetry** — characters hold secrets that can be discovered and converted to blackmail leverage through schemes ([CK3 Dev Diary #5 coverage](https://www.gamereactor.eu/crusader-kings-iii-is-introducing-schemes-secrets-and-hooks/)) — but secrets don't propagate socially or mutate; they are discovered, held, and spent. It validates that players engage deeply with per-character knowledge asymmetry as a core loop (it is one of the best-selling strategy games ever made), which supports Chronicle's bet that this is fun, but offers no mechanism to borrow for propagation.

Heaven's Vault is the opposite shape: inkle built a **history-tracking engine** where "everything is described in terms of your present knowledge: map labels, navigation choices, references in dialogue," with dialogue assembled from adaptive authored content plus procedurally selected "glue" so that any route through a non-linear archaeology plot stays coherent ([Zarf, Heaven's Vault design ruminations](https://blog.zarfhome.com/2019/05/heavens-vault-design-ruminations), [Jon Ingold, GDC: Creating a Dynamic Detective Story](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025149/-Heaven-s-Vault-Creating)). This is the player-side mirror of Chronicle's NPC grounding — the *player's* knowledge state gates and contextualizes dialogue — and it is the clearest shipped proof that grounding every line in tracked knowledge state is shippable and critically successful. But the knowledge is the player's, single-threaded, and never mutates in transit between NPCs.

### 8.3 Verdict on the landscape

Beyond report 37's settled finding (no Skyrim LLM mod ships provenance-tracked rumors), the wider scan finds: **Talk of the Town** does originate/propagate/mutate/forget beliefs including lies, but never shipped and renders via grammar; **Dwarf Fortress** ships witness-originated, graded, decaying knowledge but no mutation and no dialogue; **Versu** shipped belief-gated quips with listener belief-incorporation but no propagation beyond earshot; **Ensemble** wanted rumor-spreading as a first-class social action but modeled only its social effects; **Generative Agents** propagates information between agents but stores it as unstructured generated text — the known failure mode. **No shipped or prototype system combines all of: per-NPC beliefs with provenance, content mutation in transit, and generated dialogue rendered from that state.** Chronicle's specific bet remains unoccupied territory — validated in its components, unproven as a combination.

## 10. Verdict: does the landscape validate "engine decides, LLM renders"?

### 10.1 Yes — and it is now the emerging consensus, not a fringe position

Across twenty years and three independent research programs — UCSC's social-physics lineage (CiF → Ensemble → ESP), the Evans/Short simulationist-IF work (Versu), and Meta's game-AI program (CICERO) — every system that successfully combined *reactive simulated worlds* with *natural-language dialogue* arrived at the same architectural division: a structured, inspectable engine layer decides what is true and what may be said; a separate rendering layer (authored instantiations, state-conditioned grammars, or a conditioned language model) turns that decision into words. The LLM era did not overturn this; it confirmed it under new technology. Slice of Life demonstrates the pattern working end-to-end with a modern LLM in a playable game and reports zero hallucinations under full diegetic grounding ([ACM FDG 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3723498.3723806)); Web World Models formalizes it as physics-layer/imagination-layer separation ([arXiv 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23676v1)); and the 2026 AI-native-games survey frames the entire field's open problem as deciding what the model may invent versus what the system must verify ([arXiv 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00527v2)). Meanwhile the counterexamples — Generative Agents and the Skyrim LLM mods — are precisely the systems that let the model hold state in prose, and they exhibit exactly the provenance-free failure mode report 37 documented. ADR-0011's principle should be treated as **validated by convergence**: no better-proven alternative pattern was found in this landscape.

The one genuine divergence worth stating plainly: Slice of Life, the nearest sibling, deliberately refuses the write-back step that is Chronicle's differentiator, on authorial-control grounds. The write-back precedent comes instead from the older symbolic systems — Versu's belief-incorporating listeners and, above all, Talk of the Town's statement/lie/eavesdropping events feeding a mutating belief graph. So Chronicle's architecture is best understood as a *synthesis with no complete precedent*: Slice of Life's one-way LLM rendering discipline + Talk of the Town's claims-as-simulation-events feedback. The components are each proven; the combination is the research contribution, and the risk budget should be allocated accordingly — the feedback loop, the commit boundary, and propagation correctness deserve the prototyping effort, not the rendering.

### 10.2 Concrete mechanisms worth borrowing

Ranked by direct applicability to Chronicle's current design:

1. **CICERO's intent-correspondence filter** ([supplementary](https://hughbzhang.com/data/diplomacy.pdf)). Before a rendered line commits as a claim, score whether the rendered text is consistent with the grounded intent (a small classifier or a likelihood-delta check on the intent under a conditioned model). Published baseline: 24% of unfiltered candidates contradicted their intent. This is the empirical answer to open question #1 (confirm-vs-autoplay): ship an automated gate regardless; the player's confirm step is the second layer, and CICERO's numbers suggest autoplay-with-filter is survivable while autoplay-without is not.
2. **Talk of the Town's knowledge lifecycle taxonomy** ([paper](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12825/12825-52-16341-1-2-20201228.pdf), [code](https://github.com/james-owen-ryan/talktown)). Origination types (observation / statement / **lie** / **confabulation** / transference) as first-class, inspectable claim metadata; salience-gated statement probability; eavesdropping at fixed probability; **hand-authored per-facet mutation schemas** (brown hair drifts to black or red, not white) modulated by a per-NPC memory attribute; salience-weighted forgetting. If claims.py lacks a mutation-schema concept, this is the design to steal wholesale — mutation should be schema-driven and reviewable, not emergent.
3. **Slice of Life's prompt contract and its calibration warning** ([FDG 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3723498.3723806)). Condition each render on the intent, the *reasons* (preconditions and considerations that made the option eligible — which is also exactly the provenance the dashboard already wants to display), recent dialogue, effects, and voice; keep the input-to-output ratio large. And heed their case study: style-pressure ("be vivid, be sitcom") reintroduced fabrication even with full state grounding. Chronicle's voice-card rendering should be tested for this regression specifically.
4. **CiF/Versu gating vocabulary for the menu** ([CiF paper](http://www.ben-samuel.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/TCIAIG-social-story-worlds-with-comme-il-faut.pdf), [Short on Versu](https://emshort.blog/2013/02/26/versu-conversation-implementation/)). Volition scoring as the menu-ranking mechanism (open question #3: rank by the NPC's computed volition, not by salience heuristics — CiF has a decade of evidence this produces legible, player-trusted menus); quip-level belief gating ("speakable only if believed") as the integrity rule for options; topic/speech-act context tracking to prevent non-sequitur menus.
5. **Versu's causal bookkeeping** ([Short, 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/23/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan-2/)). Record *why* an attitude or belief changed at the moment it changes, as a first-class string/claim — retrofitting provenance is the historically documented failure.
6. **Ryan's LLM-era beat systems** ([jamesryan.computer](https://jamesryan.computer/)). If open question #6 (free-text advanced path) survives prompt 41, the validated shape is LLM-as-condition-evaluator against an authored trigger space — the model interprets; the engine owns the response space — not LLM-as-free-generator.

### 10.3 What this report did not settle

Three honest limits. First, Slice of Life's evaluation is a preliminary single-practice case study, not a controlled user study; "zero hallucinations under diegetic grounding" is an encouraging anecdote at n=1 conversation, not a guarantee. Second, Versu's and CiF's internals are documented in papers and blogs, but neither codebase is public (Versu's is locked at Linden Lab; CiF's immediate successor Ensemble is the publicly available one), so mechanism details there are reconstructed from publications rather than source. Third, the scan for provenance-tracked rumor systems was web- and literature-wide but cannot rule out unpublished commercial systems; the claim in §8.3 is "nothing found in public," stated at the confidence a search of this breadth supports. None of these caveats changes the recommendation: **the principle stands, the differentiator is real, and the risk lives in the commit boundary** — which is where the already-planned confirm step and a borrowed CICERO-style filter should absorb it.
